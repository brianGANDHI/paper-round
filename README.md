# Paper Round

Offline PWA for a fixed newspaper round. React + Vite, data in localStorage, no server. It follows the iPhone's light or dark appearance and is styled like a native iOS app.

## Use
**Routes** keeps every round you run, for example a Monday round and a Saturday round. Tap a route to use it, *New Route* to add one (it takes you straight to Setup to draw it), and *Rename*, *Duplicate* or *Delete* to manage the one in use. Each route keeps its own sections, named sections, street names, stops and progress. The route in use is named at the top of Setup and on the Round start screen; tap its name to switch.

**Plan on a computer, run on your phone.** The app works in a computer's browser too, at the same web address: the map is bigger and a mouse makes drawing and placing stops quicker. Draw the route there, then in Routes tap *Back Up This Route* and either *Copy* it or *Save File*. On your phone open Routes, tap *Restore from Backup*, and *Paste* (an iPhone and a Mac signed in to the same Apple ID share their clipboard, so the copied text is simply there) or *Open File* (AirDrop it, or keep it in iCloud Drive). Make changes on the computer later and send it again: the phone asks whether to replace the route of the same name. Restore it in the app you use for your round. On an iPhone, Safari and the Home Screen app keep separate routes, and the Home Screen app is the one to use. The computer keeps its own copy of what you drew, but it lives in that browser, so *Save File* is the safe copy.

**Setup** edits the route in use. It has four steps, shown as a segmented control at the top of the sheet (it gives way to the tool's own controls while you draw, paint a stretch or add a section): Route, Throw Side, Stops and Sections. Once the route is drawn, the first three each end with a *Next* button.

1. **Route**: no driving needed. Search for a street or town, tap *Draw Route*, then tap along your route in the order you drive it. The *Both / Left / Right / Transit* picker on the drawing panel says what the stretch you are drawing is for (the sides it is thrown on, or *Transit*, where you only drive): change it and keep tapping, and a new section starts there, so you can lay out the whole route with its sides in one go. To fix a mistake, drag a point to move it, tap a point to select it and *Delete Point*, tap a **+** between two points to add one there, or *Undo* the last point. Zoom in for the bends. *Done* finishes and fits the route on screen. (*Record by Driving Instead* still works if you prefer to trace it with GPS.)

   **Follow roads** (optional, needs internet): switch it on beside *Undo* and *Done* while drawing, and each tap follows the streets from your last point to the tap instead of joining them with a straight line, so a curvy street takes one tap instead of many. The side you pick works exactly as before. It routes for a car, so one-way streets are respected: a tap on the far side of a one-way pair can send the route round the block, so tap the stretch you actually drive. While a road is being looked up the tap shows as a ring and *Undo* turns into *Cancel*; afterwards, *Undo* takes back a whole stretch of road at once. If there is no signal, the service is slow (over 5 seconds) or down, or there is no road near the tap, that stretch is drawn as a straight line and a note says why. Dragging and adding points work on the result as usual, and the switch is remembered. With it off, tapping is unchanged and works fully offline.

   **Route by Address** (needs internet): choose *Transit* in the picker and a *Route by Address* button appears under it, for the stretches between the places you deliver. Type where the transit starts (*From*) and where it ends (*To*), then tap *Find Fastest Route*. The app looks both addresses up, asks for the fastest road for a car between them, adds it to the end of your route as a Transit section and zooms to it. A note says which places were matched, how far it is and roughly how long it takes, so a wrong match is easy to spot, and *Undo* takes the whole stretch off in one go. Once the route has points you can choose *End of Route* instead of typing a start, and the road runs from where your route ends now. A typed start that is more than about 100 m from the end of your route is driven to first, so the route stays one connected drive. If an address isn't found, add the suburb or postcode. When there is no signal, a service is down or slow, no road joins the two places, or they are the same place, nothing is added and a message says why (there is no straight-line fallback here). What you typed is kept if you close the box and open it again. The road is the fastest by the routing service's car profile, which ignores traffic and respects one-way streets, so glance at it before you rely on it.

   **Street names** (optional, needs internet): once the route is ready, *Look Up Street Names* under *Route ready* finds the name of the street each turn leads into, so the round can show the street under the turn's distance and say *Turn left onto Oak Road*. It asks the same FOSSGIS routing server as Follow roads, at most one request for each turn (a corner turned the same way twice is asked once, and a turn with no room before the next one is not asked), one at a time and never more than one a second, and keeps the answers with the route, so the round itself needs no internet. About 150 turns take around four minutes, so do it at home with the screen on and stay on this screen: *Stop* ends it, going to another step or tab (or changing the route) stops it too, and what was found is kept. If the signal drops, the lookup stops after three failures in a row (or at once if the service says it is busy) and a note says why; a run that finishes with a failure or two says how many could not be looked up. What was found is kept, the panel counts the turns still without a name, and once some turns have one the button reads *Look Up the Rest* and asks for just those. Each turn is asked about the road it leaves by, a little past the corner, and only roads running that way count, so it doesn't name the cross street. A road with no name, or no road within 25 m of the point asked about, is remembered as having none (the panel counts both as roads with no name), and the turn is shown and said as it always was. A turn onto the same street as the turn before is shown plain. *Clear Street Names* forgets them, and so do *Clear Route* and *Record Again*. Names are kept with a backup, and a turn that moves is asked about again the next time you look up. Once some names have been looked up, the start screen reminds you of any turns still missing.

2. **Throw Side**: each section of the route has a *Both / Left / Right / Transit* toggle, and the route on the map is coloured to match (blue both, green left, orange right, and a dotted grey line for Transit). *Transit* is for the stretches between the places you deliver, where nothing is thrown at all: the round screen shows *Transit* instead of a side, no-throw houses on it stay quiet (the app tells you if marking a stretch Transit silences any), and the Stops list says so. A bundle drop or a special request on a transit stretch still alerts.

   **To change a section's side, tap its line on the map** (here, or on the Route tab once the route is drawn). A small box opens right where you tapped, with the section's name and length and *Both / Left / Right / Transit*: tap one and that whole section takes it, and the box closes. The box goes when you tap the map, drag or zoom it, switch tab or tap its cross, and it stays clear of the sheet. The section is picked in the list too, and each card there has the same toggle if you would rather work from the list. To change just part of the road, tap *Change just part of it* in the box (or *Paint a Stretch* in the list): choose a side (it starts on one the section doesn't already have), then tap the route where the stretch starts and where it ends. The stretch becomes a section of its own, joining a neighbour that is already on that side.

   **To delete a section**, tap *Delete* in that box, or *Delete Section* on its card in the list (the card shows it once the section is picked, and not while you are painting). You are asked first, and told how much of the route goes and how many stops along it go with it. A section at the start or the end of the route just comes off, so the route starts or ends where the next or previous one does. One in the middle leaves a gap, which is closed with a straight line of transit between its two ends, since a stretch nobody has drawn can't have a side: redraw it with *Edit Route* or *Route by Address* if you want the real road. A stop goes with the section it was along unless another part of the route is still within reach of it, for example the other pass of a street you drive twice. A stop that is off the route (see Stops) was never along a section, so it stays. The only section can't be deleted (use *Clear Route*), and there is no undo, so the question is there to be read. *Merge* joins a section back to the one before. Left and right are as you face the way you drive, and the arrows on the map show that direction.

   **Driving a street more than once** (for example down it and back, throwing to a different side each time): tap *Repeat* on the section, then *Drive it back* (the same road the other way) or *Drive it again* (the same way, with a link back to its start). It is added to the end of your route as a new section with a side of its own, starting with the label that covers the other side of the street, and you can change it. Passes over the same road are drawn side by side on the map with their own arrows. The app follows the pass you are on, using the direction you are heading, so the side pill changes as you turn round, and a stop on that street warns you on every pass. To change the side partway along one pass, select its section first and then use *Paint a Stretch*.
3. **Stops**: choose *No-Throw*, *Bundle Drop* or *Special Request*. Type or paste addresses, one per line (with the town or postcode), and tap *Look Up Addresses*. Each is matched on the map near your route and ticked when it is a close, exact match; check any that are flagged, then *Add*. Every stop has a *name* (a business name, for a bundle drop) and an *address*, kept separately and both optional. The address is the line you looked up. Each address that is found has a box for the name and, for a bundle drop, one for the number of *papers* it gets; a stop placed on the map has boxes for the name, the address and the papers. A stop with only an address shows that as its name. Each stop in the list shows its name with its address under it and, for a bundle drop, how many papers (or *no amount set*, in orange, when it has none), and the pencil beside it changes the name, the address and the papers whenever you like, for a no-throw house as well as a bundle drop. Stops added before addresses were kept apart may have the address in the name: the pencil offers a name that looks like an address (a house number, then words) as the address instead, and nothing changes until you save. Swipe or tap the grabber at the top of the sheet to see more of the list. Each stop in the list shows which side of the road it is on as you drive the route (*Left*, or *Left, then Right* on a street you drive both ways), with a button to change it: pick *Left* or *Right* by hand, or go back to *Work It Out From the Map*. The side is worked out from where the stop sits beside your route, so a stop placed right on the route line (for example with *Use My Current Location*) may have none. A no-throw house like that takes the side your route throws on there, and when that can't say either (you throw both sides) the list shows an orange *Set side* button and the app tells you when you add it. A side you pick is as you drive past it the first time, and swaps on the way back along a street you drive both ways.

   **Special requests** are for a place that needs something done by hand, such as a house with no driveway and only a gate. A special request has an address and a short note saying what to do, up to 60 characters, such as *Blue gate, deliver by hand*. It has no name. Choose *Special Request*, then add it like any other stop: look up its address and type the note in the box beside it, or place it on the map after filling in the *Address* and *Note* boxes. The pencil beside it changes the address and the note. A request with no note says *no note set* in orange in the list, because its alert then can't say what to do.

   **A stop doesn't have to be along your route.** Place or look up a house on a street you didn't draw, or one set well back from the street you did. A stop more than about 60 m from the route is marked *off the route* in the Stops list, with how far, in place of a side, and the app says so when you add it. Its alert comes up by where you are, not by how far along the route you have got: whenever you get within the alert distance of the stop, on whatever road and whichever way you are going. Such an alert needs a GPS fix accurate to 40 m or better, so a poor fix can't set off alerts for houses you are nowhere near, and the stop counts as done once you have been that close and are away again. A stop that is only a little off the route still alerts as you drive along it, as before. A no-throw house off the route is never silenced by a transit stretch beside it.

4. **Sections**: give parts of the route a name, such as a street or an estate, so a round can start from any of them. These are separate from the throw-side sections above: they can start anywhere and say nothing about the side thrown. Tap *Add a Section*, tap the route where the section starts, and type its name; keep tapping to add more, and tap *Done Adding* when you have finished. On a road the route goes along more than once, the app asks which time through you mean (unless the passes are separate throw-side sections, where the line you tap says). A section runs from its start to where the next one starts, or to the end of the route, and the part before the first is *Start of route* (tap *Name It* to give that a name too; if the first section starts within 25 m of the start it takes the start, and there is nothing to name). A section can't start within 25 m of another or of the end of the route, nor at the turn of a road that is driven out and back, a name is up to 40 characters, and a route keeps up to 200 sections. The list shows where each section starts and ends along the route (for example *320 m to 1.4 km*) and how many stops are along it (a no-throw house on a Transit stretch never alerts, so it isn't counted); tap one to see it on the map, *Rename* it, or delete it with the bin (the route is not touched: the section before just carries on). A section is kept by where its start is on the map, not by its place in the route, so it stays put when you edit the route, and is set aside, with a note, if its start is more than 100 m from the route now or within 25 m of another section's start. *Clear Route* and *Record Again* remove the named sections. Sections are kept with a backup.

**Round**: tap *Start Round* (unlocks sound, keeps the screen awake). If the last round was finished, the button says *Start New Round* and starts from scratch automatically. If it was only part done you can *Continue Round* or *Start Over*. You only get alerts for bundle drops, no-throw houses and special requests, once for each time your route goes past one. *Alert distance* sets how near a stop you have to be for its alert to come up, for all three kinds of stop: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60 metres, with 40 to begin with. Choose it on the start screen, or change it while you drive by tapping *Alerts at 40 m* in the bottom card, and the choice is remembered. An alert is a banner across the top of the map, under your side and the next turn, so the map and the buttons stay in view, and touches go through the banner to the map. It is blue for a bundle drop, red for a no-throw house (it flashes a few times to catch your eye, then stays a steady red) and purple for a special request, and it shows how far away the stop is and the **house number** on a white plate, big enough to read at a glance, since that is what you are looking for on the street. Beside the plate are the stop's name with its address under it (a stop with only an address shows that as its name) and, for a bundle drop, how many papers in a white badge that a long name can't push off. A special request shows its note in place of a name, on up to three lines in a banner a little taller than the others, and with no house number for the plate its address goes under the note. The number is read from the start of the stop's address, for example *12* from *12 Oak Road*, *12A* from *Unit 3, 12A Oak Road* and *7* from *Lot 7 Smith Road*, and is left off the name and address beside the plate so it isn't said twice. A stop with no address uses its name only when that is a number (*No. 14*, *#14* or *14*) or reads like an address (*14 Elm Street*), so a shop called *24 Hour Pharmacy* has no number, and a stop with none shows no plate. The pencil beside a stop in the Stops list is where to add or fix its address. At the edge of the banner is which side of the road the stop is on, with an arrow and the word *Left* or *Right*: the left or right of the way you are driving, so on a street you drive both ways the same house is on the right one way and the left the other. The side is the one shown for that stop in the Stops list (see above); for a stop off the route, which has no stretch of the route beside it, it is worked out as you come up to the stop, from the way you are heading. When it still can't be told, the banner shows a plain icon in place of the arrow. There is nothing to tap. The banner goes by itself once you are away from the stop, and driving on past a stop counts it as done, which is what the number in the bottom card counts down. If you back away from a stop before you reach it, its alert goes, and comes back when you approach again. A stop off the route works differently (see Stops above): it alerts when you get within the alert distance of it, counts as done once you have been that close and left, and stays in the count if you never go near it. No-throw houses in a row (each no more than 60 m along the route from the one before it, on the same side) come up as one alert with a plate for every house in order, on one line or on two when there are more than five (and past nine, the first nine and how many more), so all the numbers are in view before you reach them. A row reaches no further than 250 m ahead and doesn't carry on round a U-turn to the houses on the way back; as you pass each house it drops off the row, and the banner moves on to the next. A house with no number has the start of its name on its plate. On the map those houses stand out as well: each has its number beside it, on the side away from the road, and the nearest has a ring round it. When other alerts overlap, the nearest no-throw house still ahead shows first, with a small +N beside the title for how many more are waiting. Each kind of stop has a sound of its own as well: one beep for a bundle drop, three low ones for a no-throw house and two rising notes for a special request. The side you are throwing and the next turn sit side by side along the top, above the alert, so they stay where they are when an alert comes and goes, and the part of the route you have driven is drawn faded, so what is left stands out. The buttons at the bottom right, above the bottom card, follow your location, show the whole route, point the map the way you are heading (the compass), skip to a spot or section (the flag) and turn the voice on and off (all below), and *Reset* in the bottom card starts the round again.

Where you are along the route is judged against where you were a moment ago and the way you are heading, not just by the nearest line, so a street you drive more than once (down and back, say) is told pass from pass, and the arrow shows only the turn for the pass you are on, never one from later in the route. *Continue Round* picks up from the furthest of where the round was started or skipped to, the furthest you got (kept every 100 m) and the last special stop done, which tells the passes apart at the start too.

**Starting part way**: under the start buttons, *Choose Where to Start* opens a map of the route. Tap the line where you want to begin, or pick *Beginning of the route* or a named section from the list (a section shows how many stops it has; when you skip while driving it also shows how many of them are not done yet), and tap *Start Here*. The stops before that spot count as done and the ones after it as not done, whatever was done before, so it also takes you back if you want to redo a part; a stop just before the spot (within 15 m, or within the alert distance if that is shorter) still comes up. Where the route goes along a road more than once and a tap could mean either time, it asks which time through you mean (unless the passes are separate throw-side sections, where the line you tap says). The round then carries on from the spot: nothing alerts until the first GPS fix says where you are, the part of the route before the spot is drawn as already driven and has no turn cards, and *Continue Round* later picks up from the furthest of there and where you got to (the start screen says where). The map shows where you are while you choose. While you drive, the flag button (*Skip to a spot or section*) opens the same map with a *Skip to Here* button. It asks first, saying how many stops that are not done yet will count as done and how many done ones will come up again, and the round keeps the way it was heading. If your first fix on the route is a little way (under 300 m) past the spot, the stops in between count as done too. It changes which stops count as done and where *Continue Round* picks up from, not where you are: the next GPS fix still puts you where you really are on the route, so use it where you are driving.

**Changing the throw side while driving**: if a side was put in wrongly, tap the side at the top of the screen (*Left*, *Right*, *Both* or *Transit*). Choose the side, then how much it applies to: from the last turn (or the start of the stretch) to the next turn (or its end), from here to the next turn, the named section you are in (only when you are in one), from here to the end of the stretch, or the whole stretch. Only the choices that make sense are listed (nothing shorter than 15 m, and none that repeat another), and a named section changes only the part of it inside this stretch. Only that part changes: the route is split there, and the rest of the stretch keeps its side. A part that is from here on starts where the car is when you choose, not where you tapped. It says how many no-throw houses that makes quiet or alert, counting every house along the part, including any you have passed (houses that start to alert behind you count as done at once), and *Undo* is there for twelve seconds. It needs a GPS fix of 65 m or better, within 40 m of the route (or 2.5 times the fix's error if more), and the app has to know where you are, so right after starting or skipping it says it can't tell until the first fix lands on the route. The change is saved with the route, and what was done in the round stays done.

**Map pointing**: the compass button with the map buttons turns the map to point the way you are heading instead of north (the choice is remembered). Your dot sits in the lower part of the screen, with a small blue arrow above it, so more of the road ahead shows. It follows the direction of travel the phone reports; when the phone has none it uses the way you have been going over the last few dozen metres, and at the start of a round it points along the route. It holds still when you stop, so it doesn't spin at a red light. While it is on and the map follows you, one finger can't drag the map, and two fingers still zoom (following never zooms out past its usual level). *Show whole route* always shows north up, and *Follow my location* or the compass brings the turned map back. Labels and house numbers stay upright, and a house number goes on the side of the house away from the road as it looks on the screen. On the round the OpenStreetMap credit is the small note at the bottom left, so it can't turn with the map.

**Turn directions**: when a turn in your route is within 200 m, a dark card beside the side pill shows an arrow bent the way the route turns, what to do (*Turn left*, *Bear right*, *Sharp left*, *U-turn*) and how far away it is, counting down to *Now*. It goes once you are through the turn. When the street names have been looked up (see Setup), the street the turn leads into is under the distance, and the voice says it (*Turn left onto Oak Road*; it says the first part of a name in two languages, and says *St*, *Rd* and the like in full, while the card shows the whole name). It is dark so it can't be mistaken for a stop alert, which are blue, red and purple, and like them it has nothing to tap. The turns are worked out from the shape of the line you drew, so the app knows nothing of junctions, and its street names are only what the lookup found: a corner, or a curve tight enough to be one, is a turn; a road that merely bends along its length is not; and a roundabout is one turn, as far as it turns you (going straight on through it is none). The card is left off when your GPS fix is worse than 65 m, or you are more than 40 m from your route (further if the fix is poor: 2.5 times its error), or the app has lost track of where you are, because then it can't tell where you are along the route.

The turns are silent unless you ask to hear them. Choose *On* under *Voice* on the start screen, or tap the speaker button with the map buttons while you drive (the choice is remembered). Each turn is then said twice, as a warning when it comes into view (*In 200 metres, turn left*) and again once you are within 60 m of it (*Turn left*; a turn first met inside 60 m only gets that), in the phone's own voice, so it works with no signal. The voice also says each alert once its beep is over (switching it on while an alert is showing says that alert at once): the numbers of a row of no-throw houses (*No throw, 12, 14, 16*), a bundle drop's number and how many papers, and a special request's number and its note. Like the alert tones, it may be muted by the phone's silent switch. The button isn't shown on a browser that can't speak.

The start of a spoken word can go missing over a car's Bluetooth, because the link goes idle between sounds and takes a moment to wake. So the voice is led in by a short tick (or by the alert's beep), and with *Keep car audio awake* on, which it is once the voice is, a sound too quiet to hear plays all the time to keep the link open. Turn that off on the start screen if you hear a hiss or it matters for a car's battery.

## Notes
- The map opens on Australia until it finds you or your route, and place searches lean toward Australia when you have no route yet.
- Address lookup, place search and Route by Address use OpenStreetMap's free Nominatim service, and Follow roads, Route by Address and street names (below) use a free routing service. They need internet. House numbers are not always in OpenStreetMap: when only the street is found, the position is approximate and the app says so. Everything else works offline.
- **Follow roads** asks the free OSRM routing server that FOSSGIS runs at `routing.openstreetmap.de` (the one behind the directions on openstreetmap.org, car profile) for the way from your last point to each tap. Each lookup sends just the two ends of that stretch, plus your IP address and the app's web address. It is one request per tap, one at a time and never more than one a second, as FOSSGIS's [terms of use](https://www.fossgis.de/arbeitsgruppen/osm-server/nutzungsbedingungen/) require; the map credits OpenStreetMap and links to *Fix the map*, which they also ask for. Those terms say the server is best effort with no promise it keeps running or stays the same, and that heavy or high-traffic use is not allowed, so it suits one person tracing their own rounds. If the app is ever shared widely, run your own OSRM and build with `VITE_ROUTING_URL` set to its address (for example `https://osrm.example.com/route/v1/driving`).
- **Route by Address** sends the two addresses you type to Nominatim, with your IP address and the app's web address, one search at a time and never more than one a second, as its [usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/) requires (there is no search-as-you-type). The road itself comes from the same FOSSGIS server as Follow roads, which is sent only the coordinates of the stretch, so the same terms and the same advice about `VITE_ROUTING_URL` apply. A road is looked for up to 250 m from each address, since a house can sit well back from its street.
- **Street names** come from the same FOSSGIS OSRM server as Follow roads, with the same terms: at most one request for each turn, one at a time, started by you and never repeated for a turn that has an answer. Each request sends just the coordinates of a point on the road just after the turn and which way it runs, plus your IP address and the app's web address. A `VITE_ROUTING_URL` that points elsewhere must contain `/route/v1/` (as the example does), because street names use the same server's `/nearest/v1/`; without it they can't be looked up. FOSSGIS's terms also ask that an operator's email address be easy to find on a website that uses the server; this app has none shown, which is for whoever runs it to decide. Names can be wrong where a tunnel or bridge lies under or over the road, or a nearer street is within 25 m of the point (with no road within 25 m the turn gets no name), and *Clear Street Names* and a new lookup is the way to redo them.
- Routes are saved automatically on the phone, after every change, and are still there next time you open the app. There is no Save button. A red bar appears if the phone refuses to store anything (for example in a private tab).
- They live only on that phone. On an iPhone, Safari and an app added to the Home Screen keep separate copies, so draw your routes in whichever one you will use (the Home Screen app is best). Deleting the app or clearing Safari's website data removes them.
- **Back up and restore** (in Routes): *Back Up This Route* or *Back Up All Routes* shows a block of text. *Copy* it into Notes, or *Save File* to keep it as a file (on a phone this opens the share sheet, with Save to Files and AirDrop), to keep a safe copy. *Restore from Backup* takes the pasted text, or a saved file with *Open File*, and adds its routes to your list. A route with the same name as one you already have can replace it or be kept beside it (you are asked), and a replaced route's progress starts over. A backup carries the route's named sections and street names too. It is also how you move routes to another phone or from a computer.
- Safari has no vibration API, so the no-throw warning is a flashing red banner plus a tone (the phone's silent switch mutes it). Vibration is used where supported.
- GPS only updates while the app is open and the screen is on. Mount the phone and keep it unlocked.
- Map tiles need signal; offline you still see the route, stops and your position on a plain background.

## Install on iPhone
Geolocation needs HTTPS, so host the built files once (GitHub Pages via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`), open the URL in Safari, then Share > Add to Home Screen. Open it once online so it caches; after that it works offline.

## Develop
```bash
npm install
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
```

Follow roads is `src/roads.ts` (the routing request, the answer turned into points, the straight-line fallback and undo of a road stretch, tested in `src/roads.test.ts` with `fetch` mocked) and `src/useRoadFollow.ts` (runs the lookups for Setup). Build with `VITE_ROUTING_URL` set to point it at a different OSRM server.

The box that opens on the map where a section is tapped is `src/SidePopover.tsx`, placed by `placePopover` in `src/placement.ts` (above the tapped spot, below it when there is no room, or slid sideways clear of the sheet; tested in `src/placement.test.ts`).

Route by Address is `src/transit.ts` (looking the two addresses up, the road between them, how it joins the route, and what to say when it can't be done, tested in `src/transit.test.ts` with both services mocked) and `src/TransitDialog.tsx` (the From and To box).

Which stops are met by where you are, rather than along the route, is `byPosition` and `approachingOccurrences` in `src/geo.ts` and `zoneExit` and `approachSide` in `src/alerts.ts`. The house number on an alert is `houseNumber` and `withoutNumber` in `src/routeEdit.ts`. Each is tested in the matching `.test.ts` file.

Where you are along the route is `src/progress.ts` (the tracker, tested against simulated drives over streets driven twice, loops and GPS glitches in `src/progress.test.ts`). Sounds and the keep-alive signal are `src/sound.ts`. Which way the phone is heading when it has no course of its own is `src/heading.ts`. Which alert shows, the plates of a row and what is said for them are in `src/alerts.ts`. Turn directions are `src/turns.ts` (finding the turns in a route, which one to show, when to speak and the shape of the arrow, tested in `src/turns.test.ts`), `src/TurnCard.tsx` (the card) and `src/speech.ts` (the phone's voice, tested in `src/speech.test.ts` with the speech mocked).

Named sections are `src/namedSections.ts` (finding where each lies on the route from the spot it was anchored at, naming, renaming and removing them, tested in `src/namedSections.test.ts`) and `src/SectionsTab.tsx`; starting or skipping part way is `src/resume.ts` (which stops count as done), `src/StartPicker.tsx` (the map and list) and `src/passChoice.ts` (telling the times a road is driven apart). Changing the side while driving is `src/driveEdit.ts` (the parts it can apply to) with `paintMetres` in `src/routeEdit.ts` and `rebaseProgress` in `src/progress.ts`. Street names are `src/streets.ts` (where to ask for each turn, the request and picking the name from the answer, matching the stored names to turns, the one-at-a-time lookup run) on top of the shared request code and one-a-second gap in `src/roads.ts`; how a name is shown and said is `src/streetName.ts`, and `src/StreetNames.tsx` is the Setup panel; a route's sections and street names are kept beside it (`SavedRoute` in `src/types.ts`) so editing the route never loses them. The map pointing the way you are heading is `src/mapHeading.ts` (smoothing and layout) and `HeadingUp` in `src/MapView.tsx`, which turns an oversized square of map about your dot with CSS; labels on the map are small boxes turned back by the same angle.

Styling lives in `src/index.css`, built on iOS-style design tokens at the top of the file. Shared pieces (icons, segmented control, sheet, dialogs) are in `src/Icon.tsx`, `src/ui.tsx` and `src/confirm.tsx`.
