# Paper Round

Offline PWA for a fixed newspaper round. React + Vite, data in localStorage, no server. It follows the iPhone's light or dark appearance and is styled like a native iOS app.

## Use
**Routes** keeps every round you run, for example a Monday round and a Saturday round. Tap a route to use it, *New Route* to add one (it takes you straight to Setup to draw it), and *Rename*, *Duplicate* or *Delete* to manage the one in use. *Optimise Route*, for a route with a line drawn, is under *Duplicate* (see below). Each route keeps its own sections, named sections, street names, stops, order of bundle drops, list of papers to load and progress. The route in use is named at the top of Setup and on the Round start screen; tap its name to switch.

**Plan on a computer, run on your phone.** The app works in a computer's browser too, at the same web address: the map is bigger and a mouse makes drawing and placing stops quicker. Draw the route there, then in Routes tap *Back Up This Route* and either *Copy* it or *Save File*. On your phone open Routes, tap *Restore from Backup*, and *Paste* (an iPhone and a Mac signed in to the same Apple ID share their clipboard, so the copied text is simply there) or *Open File* (AirDrop it, or keep it in iCloud Drive). Make changes on the computer later and send it again: the phone asks whether to replace the route of the same name. Restore it in the app you use for your round. On an iPhone, Safari and the Home Screen app keep separate routes, and the Home Screen app is the one to use. The computer keeps its own copy of what you drew, but it lives in that browser, so *Save File* is the safe copy.

**Setup** edits the route in use. It has four steps, shown as a segmented control at the top of the sheet (it gives way to the tool's own controls while you draw, paint a stretch or add a section): Route, Throw Side, Stops and Sections. Once the route is drawn, the first three each end with a *Next* button.

1. **Route**: no driving needed. Search for a street or town, tap *Draw Route*, then tap along your route in the order you drive it. The *Both / Port / Starboard / Transit* picker on the drawing panel says what the stretch you are drawing is for (the sides it is thrown on, or *Transit*, where you only drive): change it and keep tapping, and a new section starts there, so you can lay out the whole route with its sides in one go. To fix a mistake, drag a point to move it, tap a point to select it and *Delete Point*, tap a **+** between two points to add one there, or *Undo* the last point. Zoom in for the bends. *Done* finishes and fits the route on screen. (*Record by Driving Instead* still works if you prefer to trace it with GPS.)

   **Follow roads** (optional, needs internet): switch it on beside *Undo* and *Done* while drawing, and each tap follows the streets from your last point to the tap instead of joining them with a straight line, so a curvy street takes one tap instead of many. The side you pick works exactly as before. It routes for a car, so one-way streets are respected: a tap on the far side of a one-way pair can send the route round the block, so tap the stretch you actually drive. While a road is being looked up the tap shows as a ring and *Undo* turns into *Cancel*; afterwards, *Undo* takes back a whole stretch of road at once. If there is no signal, the service is slow (over 5 seconds) or down, or there is no road near the tap, that stretch is drawn as a straight line and a note says why (except for a U-turn along the road, see below). Dragging and adding points work on the result as usual, and the switch is remembered. With it off, tapping is unchanged and works fully offline.

   **U-turn**: once the route has a direction (two points that are not in the same place), U-turn buttons appear under *Undo*, *Follow roads* and *Done* while you draw. *U-turn here*, with the note *Draws a U-turn at the end of your route. Then tap where you head next.*, is there with Follow roads on or off. It adds a U-turn to the end of the route at once, with no road lookup, so it works offline and on a one-way street: three points that go on 6 m past the end, 5 m across to the right (traffic keeps left, so a U-turn goes round to the right) and back down the road 14 m, ending 8 m behind where the route ended, unless the route turned a corner off a straight road just before: then the way back ends short of that where it has to, even a few metres past where the route ended, so that it ends at least 6 m from the line of the road before the corner (a square corner 4 m back gives a way back that ends 2 m past the end, 6 m along the side street from the main road's line, 5 m to the side). That way, with Follow roads on, the next road starts on the street the U-turn is in and is drawn on from the U-turn's first point (see below). Where the way back ends less than 8 m behind the end like that, the U-turn goes on further than the 26 m past the corner (see below), as far as it takes for a way back of at least 26 m, and where the way back ends does not change: the same square corner 4 m back goes on 28 m (22 m to be 26 m past the corner, and 6 m more), 32 m along the side street (of the cases measured, the furthest it goes on is 34.5 m, after a corner of 135 degrees 5 m back). That keeps a turn out of the street, tapped next, apart from the U-turn: with Follow roads off, or a straight line drawn when the road service can't be reached, a tap at the junction and on along the road, or one straight to the road, reads as the turn in, the U-turn and the turn out the right way, for a corner of 60 to 120 degrees 3.5 to 12 m back, where it used to read *Turn right, Turn left*. On the round the route then turns round 30 to 40 m into the side street, further in than you will, but a car that turns round where you tapped, or a little further in, and drives back out is put on the way back by its second fix heading out, so the turn out comes up and so do the stops along the road out (where you are along the route is under Round, below): in simulated drives with Follow roads on and off, at corners of 60 to 120 degrees 3.5 to 12 m back, where the round put you never fell 40 m or more behind the car once it was 25 m past its turn (0 of 3,912 drives, out of the side street at 3 m/s), and the turn out was on the card in all but about 1 in 60 (66 of 4,032): 60 of those at corners of 120 degrees 3.5 to 6 m back, which leave room for a fix or none heading out (at 3.5 m back about 1 in 4 went without it), and 6 at 120 degrees 12 m back with straight taps. Now and then the card goes back to an earlier turn for a fix after it has shown the turn out (73 of 4,032 drives, and 90 slowing to 2 or 3 m/s at the junction); the voice does not say it again. A car that slows to give way at the junction and goes on up the road the way it came still now and then falls that far behind: slowing to 1, 2 or 3 m/s there, in 17, 8 and 8 of 4,032 simulated drives, and a no-throw house on the road out was then never alerted in 3 of 504 drives slowing to 2 or 3 m/s, and in 10 of 504 slowing to 1 m/s (all but one at corners of 60 degrees). A corner 14 to 20 m back keeps the 8 m behind, so the way back is only 14 to 20 m, and straight taps out from there can still read wrong. The road before the corner counts as straight when its last 4 m run within 20 degrees of the way it heads over its last 12 m; one that bends into the corner, or the piece across of a U-turn drawn just before, keeps the 8 m. The 6 m on keeps the side of the stretch it carries on, and the turn and the way back are thrown on the side picked for new points, so a section starts at the turn when that side is different. If the route turns a corner (40 degrees or more) in its last 26 m, the U-turn goes on further before it turns where it has to, so that it turns at least 26 m past the corner (it goes on 26 m less the distance back to the corner, for example 23 m for a corner 3 m back, or further, see above; a U-turn too close after a corner the same way would read as one turn the wrong way round, see U-turns below). The way the route is heading is taken over its last 12 m, or over just the stretch since a corner 3 to 12 m back. The note then says *U-turn added. Tap where the road goes next.*, and when you are drawing *Port* or *Starboard* also *Port and Starboard are as you drive, so after the U-turn this is the other side of the street.* The button can't be pressed while a road is being looked up, and *Undo* takes all three points off at once. The round finds the U-turn as one turn, a few metres past where the route ended (see U-turns below).

   *U-turn along the road* (with Follow roads on) is a switch under it, with the note *For the next tap only: the road turns round and heads back the way you came.* Switch it on, then tap the map: the road for that tap leaves your last point heading back the way the route arrived, so it turns round there, where the router would otherwise take the nearest legal way, which is often a loop round the block. It switches itself off after that tap, and also if you undo, move or delete the last point, clear the route, press *Done* or turn Follow roads off. A tap you cancel, or one on the last point, leaves it on. The switch can't be pressed while a road is being looked up, it is off each time you start drawing and it is not remembered. It works on two-way roads. The road back has to start within 30 m of your last point (enough for the other carriageway of a dual carriageway, to which your last point is joined by a short piece), while the tap itself can be up to 50 m from a road as usual. On a one-way street, where no road within 30 m of your last point runs back that way, where the tap is more than 50 m from any road, or where no road joins the two, the U-turn that *U-turn here* draws is put at the end of your route instead, the tap is not added, and a note says *No road to turn round on there (it may be a one-way street), so a U-turn was drawn at the end of your route. Tap where you head next.* The note can't say which of these it was, because the routing service gives the same answer for all of them. With no signal, or a service that is slow (over 5 seconds), down or answers with an error, it does the same and the note says *Couldn't reach the road service, so a U-turn was drawn at the end of your route. Tap where you head next.* A lookup you cancel draws nothing. *Undo* takes back the whole stretch, or the U-turn drawn in its place, as usual, and the round finds a U-turn drawn either way as one turn (see U-turns below). It is still one request for each tap, one at a time and never more than one a second.

   **The road after a U-turn drawn on the spot** (with Follow roads on): the road service starts the road for the next tap at the point of the road nearest your last point, and the U-turn ends 5 m to the side of the road you came in on, so the road would start with a 5 m jog back across to its middle, and the two bends of that jog make a corner or a bend just after the U-turn read with it as one turn the wrong way, or no U-turn at all. So when the road for the tap after a U-turn drawn by *U-turn here*, or drawn in place of a *U-turn along the road*, starts on the line the route came in on, within 1 m of where the U-turn's way back meets it, the road is drawn on from the U-turn's first point (6 m or more past where the route ended), straight back down the road, in place of the U-turn's turn and way back. On the map the route then doubles back on its own line there, with no box, the 6 m on keeps its side and the road is thrown on the side picked for new points. The round reads it as one U-turn to the right, and a corner either way from about 13 m behind where the route ended (5 m on from where the U-turn's way back ended) as a turn of its own after it (a bend of 20 or 30 degrees from about 18 m behind is no turn, and one of 45 degrees is a *Bear*). That covers a U-turn drawn just after a corner too, whose way back ends clear of the road before the corner (see above): the road back out to that road then reads as the turn in, the U-turn and the turn out the right way, always for a corner of 75 to 105 degrees from 3.5 m back, and always for one of 60 to 135 degrees from 5 m back. Closer than 5 m a corner of 120 to 135 degrees (or one of 60 degrees 3.5 m back) can still read wrong: about 1 in 4 times at 135 degrees 3.5 m back, 1 in 6 or 7 at 4 to 4.5 m, and 1 in 50 at 120 degrees or 1 in 24 at 60 degrees 3.5 m back, a gap that is left. It is done only where the turn directions read the result as a U-turn to the right wherever their 5 m pieces fall, and as the whole route falls on them (see U-turns below): where the road curves through the U-turn that is not always so, and the jog is kept, with that gap. A U-turn that a slight bend the same way just before it is joined into counts, so a road out with a bend of 20 to 30 degrees 18 to 28 m before the end goes straight back (it used to keep the jog, which now and then read wrong), while a slight bend to the left a few metres before the end, where the whole route would show the U-turn's arrow to the left, keeps the jog. A U-turn whose points, or the route just before it, have been moved since it was drawn, or one tapped by hand, is not one drawn on the spot, and keeps the jog too. *Undo* of that tap takes the road off and puts the U-turn's turn and way back again, on the sides they had, and a second *Undo* takes the U-turn off.

   **Route by Address** (needs internet): choose *Transit* in the picker and a *Route by Address* button appears under it, for the stretches between the places you deliver. Type where the transit starts (*From*) and where it ends (*To*), then tap *Find Fastest Route*. The app looks both addresses up, asks for the fastest road for a car between them, adds it to the end of your route as a Transit section and zooms to it. A note says which places were matched, how far it is and roughly how long it takes, so a wrong match is easy to spot, and *Undo* takes the whole stretch off in one go. Once the route has points you can choose *End of Route* instead of typing a start, and the road runs from where your route ends now. A typed start that is more than about 100 m from the end of your route is driven to first, so the route stays one connected drive. If an address isn't found, add the suburb or postcode. When there is no signal, a service is down or slow, no road joins the two places, or they are the same place, nothing is added and a message says why (there is no straight-line fallback here). What you typed is kept if you close the box and open it again. The road is the fastest by the routing service's car profile, which ignores traffic and respects one-way streets, so glance at it before you rely on it.

   **Street names** (optional, needs internet): once the route is ready, *Look Up Street Names* under *Route ready* finds the name of the street each turn leads into, so the round can show the street under the turn's distance and say *Turn left onto Oak Road*. It asks the same FOSSGIS routing server as Follow roads, at most one request for each turn (a corner turned the same way twice is asked once, and a turn with no room before the next one is not asked), one at a time and never more than one a second, and keeps the answers with the route, so the round itself needs no internet. About 150 turns take around four minutes, so do it at home with the screen on and stay on this screen: *Stop* ends it, going to another step or tab (or changing the route) stops it too, and what was found is kept. If the signal drops, the lookup stops after three failures in a row (or at once if the service says it is busy) and a note says why; a run that finishes with a failure or two says how many could not be looked up. What was found is kept, the panel counts the turns still without a name, and once some turns have one the button reads *Look Up the Rest* and asks for just those. Each turn is asked about the road it leaves by, a little past the corner, and only roads running that way count, so it doesn't name the cross street. A road with no name, or no road within 25 m of the point asked about, is remembered as having none (the panel counts both as roads with no name), and the turn is shown and said as it always was. A turn onto the same street as the turn before is shown plain. *Clear Street Names* forgets them, and so do *Clear Route* and *Record Again*. Names are kept with a backup, and a turn that moves is asked about again the next time you look up. Once some names have been looked up, the start screen reminds you of any turns still missing.

2. **Throw Side**: each section of the route has a *Both / Port / Starboard / Transit* toggle, and the route on the map is coloured to match (blue both, green port, orange starboard, and a solid rose-pink line for Transit, wider than the others and with larger direction arrows, so the road between delivery areas is easy to follow; the dot beside *Transit* in Setup is the same pink as the line, and the *Transit* pill on the round screen is a deeper shade of it, so the white word on it reads at a glance). *Transit* is for the stretches between the places you deliver, where nothing is thrown at all: the round screen shows *Transit* instead of a side and gives turn guidance all the way along it (see Turn directions on Transit), no-throw houses on it stay quiet, and the Stops list says so. A bundle drop (while it is next in your list: see Bundle drops), a special request or an apartment block on a transit stretch still alerts.

   **A no-throw house only alerts on a side you throw.** Which side a house is on is as the route passes it, the same side the alert shows (and the Stops list, except for a house right on the route line: see Stops), a side picked by hand included. Where *Both* is thrown it alerts. Where only *Port* is thrown it alerts only for a house on the left of the way you are driving, and likewise for *Starboard* and a house on the right. On *Transit* it never alerts. A house whose side can't be told still alerts, to be safe, and so does a house further from the route than the alert distance plus 30 m (70 m at the usual 40 m, 40 m at 10 m, 90 m at 60 m), which no stretch or side of the road makes quiet, whether the stretch beside it is transit or a side you don't throw. (That is where a house starts to be met by where you are rather than along the route, and a stretch of the route says nothing of such a house, so none that could alert by position is ever silenced, at any alert distance.) A house nearer the route than that is judged by the stretch and the side as above, so at the usual 40 m a house up to 70 m back from a transit stretch, or from one that throws only the other side, stays quiet. That reach goes with the *Alert distance* chosen on the round (see Round): the round, its map, the Stops list and Setup's notices and counts all use it, and Setup reads the distance last chosen each time it is opened. On a street you drive more than once each pass is judged on its own: the same house is on the right going out and the left coming back, so with *Port* thrown both ways it stays quiet going out and alerts coming back. A house at the turn-round of a court, a hairpin or a U-turn is passed on both legs by one pass of the route (on the right going in and the left coming out), so it is quiet only when it is quiet on both legs: thrown starboard both ways, or port both ways, it alerts once. Its alert (the beep, the banner and its distance, and the voice) is for the leg where a paper would be thrown at it, whichever leg it is nearest: it comes up the alert distance before you are beside it on that leg, and shows that leg's side, as the Stops list does. So, thrown starboard both ways, a house on the right near the end of a court closed with *U-turn here* (nearer the way back, which is 5 m to the right) comes up before you pass it going in, and the house opposite comes up before you pass it on the way back and stays up until you have; thrown port both ways where the route goes straight back down its own line, a house on the right going in comes up before you pass it coming out, and is still up as you do. Where it is thrown on both legs (both sides both ways, or port going in and starboard coming out) it alerts on the leg you come to first, with that leg's side. With nothing thrown going in and both sides coming out, the houses at the head come up the alert distance before their pass on the way out, counted along the route, which near the end of the court is still on the way in, and are named as you turn (see Voice), before you pass them coming out. A house beyond the end of a court closed with *U-turn here*, straight ahead or ahead to either side of its 5 m way across, is judged on the way in and the way back like any house at a turn-round, not on the way across, so thrown starboard both ways it alerts. Only a piece of route 12 m long or less, within 30 degrees of square to the leg before it, with the leg after it running back the other way, counts as a way across: a house beside a longer piece, such as the cross street of a block, is on its own side of it. A house between the two legs of a wider way across, 3 m or more from each (a U-turn tapped by hand, or one whose points were moved wider), is judged on the way across. Beyond it, it is on the outside of the turn as you swing round: on the left of a U-turn to the right (the usual way, with traffic keeping left), so thrown port both ways it alerts and thrown starboard both ways it stays quiet, and on the right of one to the left, the other way about. A bundle drop, a special request or an apartment block is never made quiet. Houses that won't alert are left out of every count (the stops left on the round, the stops along a named section, the rows of houses in an alert), and the round map draws them small and pale. In Setup, after any change of side, whether a whole section's, a painted stretch's or a house's own side picked by hand (the *Both / Port / Starboard / Transit* toggle, the box on the map, *Paint a Stretch* or *Set side* in the Stops list), the notice says what that did to the no-throw houses, counting each house on its own: *2 no-throw houses will now stay quiet.*, *1 no-throw house will now alert.*, or both together (*2 no-throw houses will now stay quiet; 1 no-throw house will now alert.*), since painting a stretch from Port to Starboard quiets the houses on its port side and wakes the ones on its starboard side. For a house on a street driven twice it says *... will now stay quiet on one pass* or *... will now alert on one more pass* (*some passes*, or *some more passes*, when it is more than one). When any house goes quiet the notice ends with *A no-throw house only alerts on a side you throw.* After a painted stretch it begins *Painted 120 m for starboard side.* Nothing is said when no house changes. (One case is not reported: a house that alerts once before and once after, but on the other pass.) The note under the Throw Side list says the same in short, and the total of houses that won't alert is the note under the Stops list.

   **To change a section's side, tap its line on the map** (here, or on the Route tab once the route is drawn). A small box opens right where you tapped, with the section's name and length and *Both / Port / Starboard / Transit*: tap one and that whole section takes it, and the box closes. The box goes when you tap the map, drag or zoom it, switch tab or tap its cross, and it stays clear of the sheet. The section is picked in the list too, and each card there has the same toggle if you would rather work from the list. To change just part of the road, tap *Change just part of it* in the box (or *Paint a Stretch* in the list): choose a side (it starts on one the section doesn't already have), then tap the route where the stretch starts and where it ends. The stretch becomes a section of its own, joining a neighbour that is already on that side.

   **To delete a section**, tap *Delete* in that box, or *Delete Section* on its card in the list (the card shows it once the section is picked, and not while you are painting). You are asked first, and told how much of the route goes and how many stops along it go with it. A section at the start or the end of the route just comes off, so the route starts or ends where the next or previous one does. One in the middle leaves a gap, which is closed with a straight line of transit between its two ends, since a stretch nobody has drawn can't have a side: redraw it with *Edit Route* or *Route by Address* if you want the real road. A stop goes with the section it was along unless another part of the route is still within reach of it, for example the other pass of a street you drive twice. A stop that is off the route (see Stops) was never along a section, so it stays. The only section can't be deleted (use *Clear Route*), and there is no undo, so the question is there to be read. *Merge* joins a section back to the one before. Port is your left and starboard your right as you face the way you drive, and the arrows on the map show that direction.

   **Driving a street more than once** (for example down it and back, throwing to a different side each time): tap *Repeat* on the section, then *Drive it back* (the same road the other way) or *Drive it again* (the same way, with a link back to its start). It is added to the end of your route as a new section with a side of its own, starting with the label that covers the other side of the street, and you can change it. Passes over the same road are drawn side by side on the map with their own arrows. The app follows the pass you are on, using the direction you are heading, so the side pill changes as you turn round, and a stop on that street warns you on every pass. To change the side partway along one pass, select its section first and then use *Paint a Stretch*.
3. **Stops**: choose *No-Throw*, *Bundle Drop*, *Special Request* or *Apartment Block* (the four choices sit two by two). Type or paste addresses, one per line (with the town or postcode), and tap *Look Up Addresses*. Each is matched on the map near your route and ticked when it is a close, exact match; check any that are flagged, then *Add*. Every stop has a *name* (a business name, for a bundle drop) and an *address*, kept separately and both optional. The address is the line you looked up. Each address that is found has a box for the name and, for a bundle drop or an apartment block, one for the number of *papers* (how many a bundle drop gets, or how many are thrown at a block); a stop placed on the map has boxes for the name, the address and, for those two, the papers. A stop with only an address shows that as its name. Each stop in the list shows its name with its address under it and, for a bundle drop or an apartment block, how many papers (or *no amount set*, in orange, when it has none), and the pencil beside it changes the name, the address and (for those two) the papers whenever you like, for a no-throw house as well as a bundle drop. Stops added before addresses were kept apart may have the address in the name: the pencil offers a name that looks like an address (a house number, then words) as the address instead, and nothing changes until you save. Swipe or tap the grabber at the top of the sheet to see more of the list. Each stop in the list shows which side of the road it is on as you drive the route (*Port*, or *Port, then Starboard* on a street you drive both ways), with a button to change it: pick *Port* or *Starboard* by hand, or go back to *Work It Out From the Map*. The side is worked out from where the stop sits beside your route, so a stop placed right on the route line (within 3 m of it, for example with *Use My Current Location*) may have none. A no-throw house like that alerts as if it were on the side your route throws there, since that is the side it matters on, but the list doesn't show that guess as its side: it shows *side not known* and an orange *Set side* button, on a *Port* or *Starboard* stretch as well as on *Both* (but not on a stretch that is only transit, where it never alerts). Any other kind of stop whose side can't be told shows the same, on any stretch, and its alert shows no side. When you add a no-throw house that has no side on a stretch where you throw both sides, or an apartment block that has none anywhere but on a stretch that is only transit, the app tells you to tap *Set side*. A side you pick is as you drive past it the first time, and swaps on the way back along a street you drive both ways. A no-throw house that won't alert says why in the line under its name: when every pass of it is quiet the reason takes the place of the side's words (*in transit, so no alert*, *quiet here: you throw the other side*, or *quiet here: in transit or you throw the other side*), and the side itself shows on the button at the right of the row (the first pass's side, on a street driven twice). On a street driven twice where only some passes are quiet, the sides come first and the reason after them, for example *Port, then Starboard, quiet on pass 2: you throw the other side* or, with the passes listed, *quiet on passes 1 and 3: in transit*. A house further from the route than the alert distance plus 30 m never shows a reason, as it always alerts (see Throw Side), and a house that is quiet on every pass shows its reason even when it is far enough out to be marked off the route (see below). Every stop within the alert distance plus 30 m of the route (70 m at the usual 40 m) has the side button, even one far enough out to be marked off the route, so its side can be picked by hand; only a stop further out than that, which alerts by where you are, has none. Under the list a note counts them: *2 no-throw houses won't alert: they are on transit stretches, or on the side you don't throw.*

   **Special requests** are for a place that needs something done by hand, such as a house with no driveway and only a gate. A special request has an address and a short note saying what to do, up to 60 characters, such as *Blue gate, deliver by hand*. It has no name. Choose *Special Request*, then add it like any other stop: look up its address and type the note in the box beside it, or place it on the map after filling in the *Address* and *Note* boxes. The pencil beside it changes the address and the note. A request with no note says *no note set* in orange in the list, because its alert then can't say what to do.

   **Apartment blocks** are for a block of flats or units where a number of papers are thrown. A block has a name (such as *Lakeside Apartments*), an address and its *papers*: how many to throw there. Choose *Apartment Block*, then add it like any other stop: look up its address and type the papers (and a name, if you like) in the boxes beside it, or place it on the map after filling in the *Name*, *Address* and *Papers* boxes. The pencil beside it changes all three, and a block with no papers says *no amount set* in orange in the list. On the round it is told of ahead with how many papers and which side of the road it is on, whatever side you throw and on a transit stretch too, and it is done once you drive past it (see Round). On a street you drive more than once it is told of on each pass, like a special request, with its side as that pass goes by it, since the app can't tell which pass you threw it on; one at the turn-round of a court, which one pass takes in on both legs, is told of once. It is not a bundle drop: it has no *Dropped* button and is not in the Bundles list.

   **A stop doesn't have to be along your route.** Place or look up a house on a street you didn't draw, or one set well back from the street you did. A stop more than about 60 m from the route is marked *off the route* in the Stops list, with how far, in place of a side, and when you add it the app says how it will alert. Further out than the alert distance plus 30 m, its alert comes up by where you are. Nearer than that, the list marks it off the route, but its alert still comes up as you drive past it, with the side it is on, which you can set in the list. A stop further from the route than the alert distance plus 30 m (70 m at the usual 40 m, see Throw Side) alerts by where you are, not by how far along the route you have got: whenever you get within the alert distance of the stop, on whatever road and whichever way you are going. A bundle drop is the exception: within 60 m of the route it alerts from the road at any alert distance, as its zone reaches 15 m further than it is from the road. Over 60 m off, and past the alert distance plus 30 m as well, it has only the alert distance, and whether to go to it is your call (see Bundle drops). Any other kind of stop keeps the alert distance. Such an alert needs a GPS fix accurate to 40 m or better, so a poor fix can't set off alerts for houses you are nowhere near, and the stop counts as done once you have been that close and are away again. A stop nearer the route than that still alerts as you drive along it, with the side it is on, even one far enough out to be marked off the route, and it keeps its side button in the list (see above). A no-throw house that near can still be kept quiet by the stretch beside it, and when it is quiet on every pass the list shows why in place of *off the route*, and nothing is said of it being off the route when you add it, as it never alerts; one further out than that is never made quiet, whether the stretch beside it is transit or a side you don't throw.

   **The order of bundle drops**: with two or more bundle drops on the route, the Stops step ends (under the list of stops, before *Next: Name Sections*) with *Order of bundle drops*: the bundle drops in the order you deliver them, under the note *The order you deliver them in. It never changes unless you move one.*, each numbered, with its papers and a *Move up* and a *Move down* button (48 px square, named for the bundle, as in *Move Co-op up*; one with nowhere to go is dimmed). Until you move one they are in the order the route first reaches them. The order never changes by itself: a bundle drop added after you have set it goes after the ones in it, in route order, and one you delete drops out. It is the same order as the round's Bundles list, which can change it too (*Bundle drop order* on the round's start screen opens that list), and the round's next bundle is the first in it still to do, wherever it is, and the only one that alerts (see Bundle drops).

4. **Sections**: give parts of the route a name, such as a street or an estate, so a round can start from any of them. These are separate from the throw-side sections above: they can start anywhere and say nothing about the side thrown. Tap *Add a Section*, tap the route where the section starts, and type its name; keep tapping to add more, and tap *Done Adding* when you have finished. On a road the route goes along more than once, the app asks which time through you mean (unless the passes are separate throw-side sections, where the line you tap says). A section runs from its start to where the next one starts, or to the end of the route, and the part before the first is *Start of route* (tap *Name It* to give that a name too; if the first section starts within 25 m of the start it takes the start, and there is nothing to name). A section can't start within 25 m of another or of the end of the route, nor at the turn of a road that is driven out and back, a name is up to 40 characters, and a route keeps up to 200 sections. The list shows where each section starts and ends along the route (for example *320 m to 1.4 km*) and how many stops are along it (a no-throw house that never alerts, because it is on a Transit stretch or on a side you don't throw, isn't counted); tap one to see it on the map, *Rename* it, or delete it with the bin (the route is not touched: the section before just carries on). A section is kept by where its start is on the map, not by its place in the route, so it stays put when you edit the route, and is set aside, with a note, if its start is more than 100 m from the route now or within 25 m of another section's start. *Clear Route* and *Record Again* remove the named sections. Sections are kept with a backup.

**Round**: tap *Start Round* (unlocks sound, keeps the screen awake). It starts from scratch, as *Start Over* does, and is there when nothing has been done and there is nowhere to carry on from. If the last round was finished, the button says *Start New Round* and starts from scratch automatically. If it reached the end of the route (within 150 m, as how far you got is kept 100 m at a time) with at least one bundle drop, and nothing else, still to do, as on the morning after, the main button is *Start New Round* too and a note says so (*Your last round reached the end with 1 bundle drop still to do, so this will start a new one.*; a route with no bundle drop left to do never shows this), and *Continue Round* stays under it as a plain button and carries on as ever. *Choose Where to Start* is a new round there too: yesterday's drops and misses are forgotten, and what the picker says about the spot matches. If it was only part done you can *Continue Round* or *Start Over*. With a round to carry on, *Continue Round*, *Start Over* and *Choose Where to Start* sit straight under the summary line, above Papers to load. A fresh round, a finished one and a round over but for its bundles keep them at the bottom of the screen. You only get alerts for bundle drops, no-throw houses, special requests and apartment blocks (the start screen says so), once for each time your route goes past one, and for a bundle drop only while it is next in your list (see Bundle drops). *Alert distance* sets how near a stop you have to be for its alert to come up, for all four kinds of stop: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60 metres, with 40 to begin with. It also sets how far from the route a no-throw house can be kept quiet (see Throw Side). Choose it on the start screen, or change it while you drive with *Alert Distance* in the menu (the three dots at the right of the bar along the bottom, where the current distance is shown), and the choice is remembered. The bar says *Alerts at 40 m* too, but only as a plain note with nothing to tap, and only when it has nothing else to say (see The bottom of the round). An alert is a banner across the top of the map, under your side and the next turn, so the map and the buttons stay in view, and touches go through the banner to the map, except for a bundle drop's *Dropped* button (see Bundle drops). It is blue for a bundle drop, red for a no-throw house (it flashes a few times to catch your eye, then stays a steady red), purple for a special request and teal for an apartment block, and it shows how far away the stop is and the **house number** on a white plate, big enough to read at a glance, since that is what you are looking for on the street. Beside the plate are the stop's name with its address under it (a stop with only an address shows that as its name). A bundle drop's papers are not beside the name but in a pale panel beside its *Dropped* button (see Bundle drops), which leaves the name and address the room. The distance changes as you approach and is hidden from VoiceOver, so approaching does not read the alert out again at every step; the alert's own label still says what it is for. A special request shows its note in place of a name, on up to three lines in a banner taller than an ordinary alert's (about 100 px against 68), and with no house number for the plate its address goes under the note. A bundle drop's banner is the tallest of all, about 150 to 190 px, because it holds the stop's details and the big *Dropped* button under them (see Bundle drops). An apartment block's banner is titled *Apartment block*, with its details across the top (*Throw its papers here.* in place of a name when it has no name or address) and under them how many papers to throw there, in big type in a pale panel the width of the banner, since that count is what you look for; it has no button. The number is read from the start of the stop's address, for example *12* from *12 Oak Road*, *12A* from *Unit 3, 12A Oak Road* and *7* from *Lot 7 Smith Road*, and is left off the name and address beside the plate so it isn't said twice. A stop with no address uses its name only when that is a number (*No. 14*, *#14* or *14*) or reads like an address (*14 Elm Street*), so a shop called *24 Hour Pharmacy* has no number, and a stop with none shows no plate. The pencil beside a stop in the Stops list is where to add or fix its address. At the edge of the banner is which side of the road the stop is on, with an arrow and the word *Port* or *Starboard*: the left or the right of the way you are driving, so on a street you drive both ways the same house is on the right one way and the left the other. The side is the one shown for that stop in the Stops list (see above), except that a no-throw house right on the route line, which the list shows as *side not known*, shows the side thrown there when only one side is; for a stop off the route, which has no stretch of the route beside it, it is worked out as you come up to the stop, from the way you are heading. When it still can't be told, the banner shows a plain icon in place of the arrow. A no-throw house, a special request and an apartment block have nothing to tap. The banner goes by itself once you are away from the stop, and driving on past a stop counts it as done (for the next bundle drop driven past without tapping *Dropped* that means missed, and one that is not next stays to do, see Bundle drops), which is what the number of stops left in the bar counts down. If you back away from a stop before you reach it, its alert goes, and comes back when you approach again (backing up briskly enough to be taken for turning round, it stays up instead until that is settled: see below). A stop off the route works differently (see Stops above): it alerts when you get within the alert distance of it, counts as done once you have been that close and left, and stays in the count if you never go near it. No-throw houses in a row (each no more than 60 m along the route from the one before it, on the same side) come up as one alert with a plate for every house in order, on one line or on two when there are more than five (and past nine, the first nine and how many more), so all the numbers are in view before you reach them. A row reaches no further than 250 m ahead and doesn't carry on round a U-turn to the houses on the way back; as you pass each house it drops off the row, and the banner moves on to the next. A house with no number has the start of its name on its plate. On the map those houses stand out as well: each has its number beside it, on the side away from the road, and the nearest has a ring round it. When other alerts overlap, the nearest no-throw house still ahead shows first (until you are 10 m past it: a house you have driven past no longer hides a bundle drop that is still in range, see Bundle drops), with a small +N beside the title for how many more are waiting. An apartment block still to come is not hidden by another stop's alert. While a no-throw house's, a bundle drop's or a special request's banner is up, that banner keeps its own details (the plates, the request's note, and for a bundle drop its *Dropped* button, which stays under the strip), and under them has a teal strip (which does not flash) with *Apartment block*, how many papers in big type (when it has an amount set) and the block's side with its arrow and word at that end of the strip (a building when the side can't be told), from when the block comes up until you are 10 m past it, the nearest block when there are two. The banner's label says the block too, and the block is not counted in the +N. A block gives way to a bundle drop or special request that is up, as it does to a house. When nothing else is up, the block has its own teal banner. Each kind of stop has a sound of its own as well: one beep for a bundle drop, three low ones for a no-throw house, two rising notes for a special request and two short notes of one pitch, a little lower than a bundle drop's beep, for an apartment block. The side you are throwing and the next turn sit side by side along the top, above the alert, with the heading badge (see Heading badge) at their right, so they stay where they are when an alert comes and goes (on a phone 350 px wide or narrower the turn card moves to a line of its own under the side when the two do not fit side by side). The part of the route you have driven is drawn faded and underneath, so what is left stands out (see What the round map draws). The bottom of the screen is one slim bar and a few buttons, and the rest is in a menu (see The bottom of the round): the buttons down the right of the map follow your location, point the map the way you are heading (the compass), turn the voice on and off and zoom, and *Reset Round*, which starts the round again, is in the menu.

**Papers to load**: above *Alert distance* on the start screen is the list of what to load before you start, set out as you would write it down: the bagged lines, what they add up to when there are two or more, then the unwrapped lines, then the whole. With the usual list it reads *102 bagged · 1 pallet* 2,550, *+ 18 bagged* 450, *= 120 bagged*, *+ 13 unwrapped* 650 and *133 bundles · 3,650 papers all up*. A route with no list of its own shows that usual list: 102 bagged (1 pallet), 18 bagged and 13 unwrapped, at 25 papers a bagged bundle and 50 an unwrapped one. *Edit* opens a sheet with, for each line, its bundles (on the numeric keypad), *Bagged* or *Unwrapped*, a note of up to 40 characters and a bin to remove it, then *Add line* and the papers in a bagged and in an unwrapped bundle. The whole and each line's papers change as you type. Every change is saved as it is made, so there is no *Cancel*, and *Done* only closes the sheet. Removing a line hands the focus to the bin of the line that takes its place, or to *Add line* when no line comes after it, and a screen reader says *Papers in a bagged bundle* and *Papers in an unwrapped bundle* for the two papers fields. Numbers are whole, from 0 to 9,999, and a blank field counts as 0. A list holds up to 30 lines, and taking off every line leaves an empty list (*Nothing on the list yet.*), not the usual one. The list belongs to the route, not to the round: editing the route, adjusting it while driving, *Start Over*, *Reset Round*, renaming the route and setting an order of bundle drops all leave it alone, and copying a route copies it. A backup carries it once the route has a list of its own (see Notes).

**Bundle drop order**: under the papers to load, with two or more bundle drops on the route, this button opens the Bundles list (see Bundle drops), so the order can be set with *Reorder* without going to Setup. With two or more bundles left to do in a round still going, it is that round's own list, with *Reorder*, *Mark dropped* and *Undo*. With fewer, as on a finished round, or on a round over but for its bundles however many are left (see Round), it is the list of the next round, headed *Bundles, next round*: every stop to do, in your order, with *Reorder* but no *Mark dropped* or *Undo*, and the round kept is not changed.

**Bundle drops** need you to say they are done, so a bundle drop's alert has one thing to tap: a big white *Dropped* button at least 56 px high, under the stop's details (the venue name, its address of up to two lines and the side of the road), which are in bigger type than on other alerts. The papers are not among the details but in a panel of their own beside the button, as tall as it and in big type, since the count is what you check before the tap (it is pale, not white, so it isn't taken for a second button; a bundle with no amount set has no panel and the button takes the whole width). That leaves the name and the address the full width of the banner but for the house number's plate. It is the only part of an alert that takes a touch; the rest of the banner still lets touches through to the map. Like any alert, it comes up when you are within the alert distance (only while it is next in your list: see below) and goes once you are past the stop or have backed away from it. Tapping *Dropped* marks the bundle dropped: the alert goes, a short quiet tick of two rising notes plays (higher and much quieter than any alert, so it isn't taken for one), and the bar says *Dropped* with *Undo* for 12 seconds. A second tap within a moment of the first is ignored, so a double tap drops one bundle and not the next one at the same spot. *Undo* takes the drop back. If that makes the bundle next again, its alert comes up again, with its beep, if you are still at the stop, and the alert of the bundle after it, if that is up, goes without a word (if you have left the stop by then, or the bundle is not next, it is still to do in the list: *To do*, or *Next* when it is the first still to do in your order). With the voice on, it then says the next bundle in your order (see below) once the tick is over, with how far it is from the phone in a straight line (*Next bundle, Co-op, 300 metres*; kilometres a long way off, and no distance when it is under 15 m away or the phone has no position yet). A bundle drop's alert that comes up together with a no-throw house does not take its banner while that house is still ahead of you or less than 10 m behind, since a bundle never hides a no-throw warning: it has a strip in the house's banner instead (see below). A house you have driven past (more than 10 m) no longer holds the banner, so if the bundle is still in range it then has its own.

**A no-throw house at the same spot as a bundle drop** is still the alert, in red, as a house is what stops a paper going to the wrong door. The bundle's name, its papers and its *Dropped* button are then in a blue strip at the foot of the house's banner, so a bundle at the same address as a no-throw is confirmed the same way as any other. The same goes for a bundle with its alert up behind a special request's banner. Tapping it takes the strip away and leaves the house's banner up.

**Only the bundle next in your list alerts.** With no order set the list is route order, so the rule is the same. A bundle that is not next gives no banner, beep or voice, does not close a sheet, and driving past it does not mark it missed or done: it stays *To do*. The next bundle alerts along the route on any pass still ahead of you. If every pass still to do is behind you (you drove past while it was not next, say), its last pass alerts by where you are: within the alert distance of the stop in a straight line, whatever the route says, or within 15 m further than it is from the road if that is more. So a bundle within 60 m of the road alerts from the road at any alert distance: its zone reaches 15 m further than it is from the road. On a street driven twice, a bundle set back further than the alert distance plus 30 m, and driven by while it was not next, alerts on its later pass, and going by it there settles the stop, so the list moves on. A bundle over 60 m off is marked *off the route*, and once it is past the alert distance plus 30 m as well it has the alert distance only, so it comes up only if you go to it, which is your call (see below). Other kinds of stop keep the alert distance. An alert by where you are needs a GPS fix of 40 m or better, and has *Dropped*, its words, the straight-line distance and the side as you come up to it. It is missed if you drive out of that zone plus 15 m, on a fix of 40 m or better: one poorer fix does not take the alert down or count a miss. That is true even off the route, though an alert that is up as the *Off the route* banner comes goes with the others, neither done nor missed, and comes up again as you come back to the stop. When the next changes (a drop, a miss, *Mark dropped*, *Undo*, a reorder) the rule follows at once: a bundle alert that is no longer next goes silently, not done and not missed, and may alert again when it is next, and a new next you are already beside alerts straight away. A bundle to do holds the ones after it in the list until it is dropped, missed or marked, or you move it down the list. A next bundle that went behind you while it was next, and was not counted missed, stays *Next* and alerts by place if you go back, and you are told once (see below). Bundles still to do at the end of the route stay to do and are not swept to missed: the round is simply not finished, and they can be marked in the list or still alert by place.

Driving on past the next bundle drop in its zone without tapping *Dropped* ends the alert but records the bundle as **missed**, shown in amber, and the bar says *Missed bundle: Co-op* for a few seconds (*Missed 2 bundles: A and B*, or *A, B and 2 more* when there are more; a name is cut to 26 characters). A missed bundle can be marked dropped later from the list. It takes a fix of 30 m or better to count as driven past: on a poorer one, which can be thrown ahead of the car or back from it, a bundle's alert along the route stays up with *Dropped*, and the next good fix judges it. So does a fix of 30 m or better that comes just after a throw, while the app has not yet believed the way back. If you really do drive on through poor fixes, the miss counts when the next good fix comes. (An alert by where you are goes by the 40 m above, and the alerts of other stops are not changed.) A miss is recorded only while you are following the route, with two exceptions. A bundle up by where you are, because every pass of it is behind you (above) or because it is away from the route, is missed wherever you are, with the *Off the route* or *Heading back* banner up too, and keeps *Mark dropped*. And while you are back on the route somewhere else, with the banner up that offers to carry on or go back (see Off the route), a bundle alert going by is a real miss, with its notice and *Mark dropped*, and the list moves on. Other stops going by then, and alerts along the route going by while you are off it or being taken back, stay as they were and can come up again. Leaving the route just past a bundle with its alert up (more than 15 m past it, or the alert distance if that is less) counts it missed, so it keeps *Mark dropped*. Bundles **skipped** over, neither dropped nor missed, are only those passed by skipping to a spot or section, or starting from one.

The next bundle gone by with no alert at all is **missed** too, with the same message and its *Mark dropped*, and the list moves on. That covers a stretch with no fix, or only poor ones: only from one fix to the next can a bundle be missed unseen. The bundle after it in the list is then tested the same way in the same stretch, and all of them are named together. If the next bundle's alert was up and it went by on the fix that ends a gap, the bundle after it is tested the same way on that fix. A tap of *Dropped* does not do this: the bundle after it is left to alert by place. A stop with a pass still to come stays next and alerts on that pass. A bundle not next stays to do, in that stretch as anywhere. A bundle already behind you when it became next is not counted: it alerts by place.

It counts only if the car could really have driven past. From one fix to the next, the move along the route may be at most 100 m more than the straight line between the two fixes, so a fix landing on a parallel street, or overshooting a side street, counts nothing, and those bundles still alert if you drive back. A fix worse than 30 m counts nothing, as it can be thrown ahead of the car, nor does one the app is still in doubt about after a throw, and the next good fix judges the whole stretch. A car inside the bundle's zone at the end of a gap is at the bundle: it alerts by place on that fix, it is missed the ordinary way if driven out of the zone, and no bundle after it is tested. The stretch from a start part way, a skip or a spell off the route to the first fix counts no bundle at all, as that fix is no proof you drove it (a car crossing the road on its way to the spot has not): a next bundle in it stays *Next*, alerts by place if you come to it, and you are told once if you leave it behind (see below). The other stops in that stretch, when it is under 300 m, count as done only on a fix of 30 m or better that the app is not in doubt about, which they wait for, as a poorer one can be thrown past a house you have not got to, and only as far as the car has got, not to the furthest reached (see Starting part way). Nothing is counted on the first fix after *Start Round*, *Start Over* or *Continue Round*, on the way to the route, off the route, on a jump along the route not yet believed, or across a turn round.

**A next bundle can still hold the list.** A miss is counted only where the app can tell you went by the bundle in its turn. Where it can't, the bundle stays *Next*, and no bundle after it alerts, until you go to it, mark it or move it down the list. That is when the first fix after a start part way, a skip or a spell off the route found you past it, or you carried on from where you came back onto the route with it behind you, turned round short of it, drove on past a court with it in it, cut across to another street, or went through a gap past it on a road so bent that the way along the route is over 100 m longer than the straight line. It is so too for a bundle further from the route than the alert distance plus 30 m, which has no place along the route to go by, if you never come into its zone, and for a bundle already behind you when it became next, which happens when a stop on a street driven twice goes by with no tap on its first pass: it stays *Next* for its second pass while you go by the bundles after it, and once it is dealt with the list comes to them behind you. It alerts by place if you go back, and *Mark dropped* and *Reorder* in the Bundles list are there if you don't.

**You are told once.** So that the list does not stand still in silence, you are told as soon as the next bundle starts costing you others. Once the car is past a pass of the next bundle, and has then gone by another bundle on your list that it has left no way of reaching in order, the bar says *Next bundle is still Co-op. Mark it or move it in the Bundles list.*, and with the voice on it says *Next bundle is still Co-op.* That is said even with a pass of it still to come, which on a long route can be many kilometres on, as nothing else would say why the bundles between have gone quiet. It covers a first pass gone by with no tap, and a bundle set too far from the road for you to come into its zone. It is said only of what goes by while that bundle is next, so what went by before you moved it to the top, or before the list came to it, does not count (until the Round screen opens afresh, which forgets it), and it is said only of a bundle you can no longer have after it: one with a pass still to come after the next bundle's own next pass, or close enough to it to alert as you take the next, is left alone, as you can still take the two in order. So a list in the order you mean to drive it is not told this, even with bundles left for a second pass. It is not said of a bundle you have yet to come to, however many others go by first: that is your order at work. A pass before where you started or skipped to counts as gone by, and a bundle set back from the road counts as gone by level with it.

When the next bundle has gone more than 150 m behind you along the route, with no pass of it left, and is more than 150 m away in a straight line, the bar says *Next bundle Corner shop is behind you. Mark it or move it in the Bundles list.* With the voice on it also says *Next bundle is behind you.*, in its turn, breaking in on nothing (newer words can take its place while it waits). Behind you means behind you along the route, so where the road bends back the stop can be in front of the windscreen. It means behind where you are, not the furthest you have got, so after *Continue Round* a bundle between the two is still ahead of you.

Either way it is said once for each bundle, and again only after a restart (a start, a skip or carrying on from here) or when the Round screen opens afresh (a reload, a killed app or leaving the Round tab), which forgets it was said, so a bundle told of before a reload is told of once more after it, or if you move it on or off the top of the list and the list comes back to it, or it costs you another after that. It waits for a fix of 30 m or better. It waits for a notice in the bar to go, so it never takes the place of *Undo* after a drop or the name of a bundle just missed, for a fix with no alert coming up and, with the voice on, no no-throw row held for the side, as their words would take its place, and for the Bundles list, the menu, the picker of a spot or a question to close, as those cover the bar. It waits while you are off the route, and over a turn round not sure yet, and while you are back on the route somewhere else it waits until the round carries on. It is not said while its alert is up.

It is said of any next bundle, however it came to be next, as every bundle after it is silent until it is dealt with, even those still ahead of you. That includes one the list came to by itself, by a drop, a miss, *Mark dropped* or *Undo*, so if several are behind you, you are told of each in turn as you deal with the one before it. It also includes a bundle your own order puts after one further along the route: *Next bundle is behind you.* then comes once the *Undo* in the bar has gone, after *Next bundle, Corner shop, 280 metres*, and if you are going back for it you can ignore it. The one thing that is not told of is a bundle you moved to the top of the Bundles list when it was already behind you, or that came to the top when you moved the one above it down, as you have just decided that yourself and the list shows it as *Next*. A bundle you move down the list and then drive past is not told of either, unless the list comes back to it. A start forgets what has been told and what you moved, and so does the Round screen opening afresh, so the bundle next when you first reach the route after one of those can be told of, ahead of the car when you reached the route or not: that covers a reload, a killed app opened further on, and coming onto the route past the bundle. First reaching the route after *Continue Round* can take up to 200 m of driving. The bundle stays *Next* either way.

**A bundle on a street the route goes along twice is one delivery**, however many times the route passes it. It is one row in the list, with *2 passes* under its name, its papers are counted once in the list's figures, and the stop is counted once in the list's header (*2 bundles left · 1 of 3 dropped* counts stops, not passes) and in the bar's *3 left* and *2 missed*. Dropping it on one pass, with *Dropped* or *Mark dropped* in the list (which marks the first pass still to come or missed), settles every other pass: one you have not reached never alerts and is never missed, and one that was missed is not missed any more. The row then reads *Dropped* with *Undo*, which puts every pass back exactly as it was: still to come, skipped or missed. A stop counts as missed (in the bar's *2 missed* and the list's *Missed*) only when no pass of it is left to come and none was dropped, and counts once even if both passes were missed. So driving past the first pass untapped, with a second to come, still raises *Missed bundle: Co-op*, but the row stays *Next* or *To do*, not amber, and the stop is not counted as missed until the second pass is gone by too; dropping it on the second pass clears it. When a pass goes by without a drop, earlier passes of the stop not done yet count as gone by too (neither dropped nor missed), so a first pass driven by while the stop was not next leaves the stop one miss and one notice, and does not hold up the list. Skipping to a spot keeps this: if the pass dropped is before the spot, the later pass stays done and does not alert again, and if it is after the spot, the drop and the passes it settled come up again.

The bar shows the next bundle drop when the route has any (and nothing more pressing is in the middle of the bar: see The bottom of the round). The next is the first in your order of bundle drops (set in Setup's Stops step or in the Bundles list, which *Bundle drop order* on the start screen also opens) that is still to do, that is not dropped, missed or skipped, wherever it is: not the nearest and not the next along the route, so it can be behind you. The venue has a row of its own, the full width of the line: its name, else its address, else *Bundle drop*. Above it, on one row, are *Next bundle*, how far it is from the phone in a straight line (*340 m*, *Now* when it is under 15 m away, and nothing until the phone has a position), how many bundle drops are left to do, the next one included (*3 left*, a stop on a street driven twice counted once), an amber *2 missed* when any have been missed, and the papers (*12 papers*). The lead reads just *Next* when the amber pill or the small warning button (see The bottom of the round) takes room beside it, and on a narrow screen whatever does not fit is left off whole from the end (the papers first, then the amber pill, then *3 left*), and never squeezes the venue's name. When none is left to do it reads *Bundles* over *All bundles dropped* or *No bundles left*, and a route with no bundle drops shows nothing there. Only the next bundle alerts, and with no order set your list is route order (see above). Tap the line, or choose *Bundles* in the menu, to open the list. The **Bundles** list is a sheet that comes up over the dimmed map. At the top are *Papers dropped*, *Papers to drop* and *Missed* (amber when above 0), and under the title how many bundle drops are left to do and how many of the total are dropped (*3 bundles left · 5 of 8 dropped*, *No bundles left · 6 of 8 dropped* when the rest were missed or skipped, or *All 8 bundles dropped*). Below is a row for each bundle drop in your order (a street driven twice is one row, with *2 passes* under the name), each with its place in the order, its status (*Next* in blue, *To do* in grey, *Dropped* in green, *Missed* in amber, *Skipped* in grey), venue, address, papers and how far it is from the phone in a straight line (*340 m away* or *Here now*, and nothing until the phone has a position). *Next*, *To do* and *Missed* rows have *Mark dropped* (the next one can be a long way off, with no alert to tap), *Dropped* rows have *Undo*, and *Skipped* rows have no button. A tap on *Mark dropped* or *Undo* in any row within a moment (600 ms, as on *Dropped*) of the last one taken is ignored, since one turns into the other under your finger: a double tap on *Mark dropped* marks the bundle dropped and does not take it back, and a double tap on *Undo* does not mark it again. A missed bundle marked dropped and then undone goes back to missed, and every other pass the drop settled goes back as it was (until you leave the Round tab, after which *Undo* puts back only that pass, as still to do); a skip, carrying on from here or a start from a spot keeps this for each drop it keeps. *Reorder*, beside *Done* and there only while two or more bundle drops are still to do, turns the rows into the same order editor as in Setup: each bundle still to do has *Move up* and *Move down*, the order changes as you tap, and the dropped, missed and skipped ones are shown with no buttons and are hopped over by a bundle moved past them. *Finish* goes back to the list. If a drop (with an alert's *Dropped*) or a miss leaves fewer than two to do while the editor is open, Reorder ends and the plain list comes back, and it stays the plain list, with *Reorder* beside *Done*, if an *Undo* makes two to do again. The list opens with the next bundle in the middle of the view, a note says when some bundles have no amount set (they add no papers to the figures), and on a route with no bundle drops the list says so. An alert and the heading badge stay above the sheet, so an alert's *Dropped* works while the list is open.

Bundles dropped and missed are kept with the stops done in the saved round, so they survive a reload and *Continue Round*. Starting over, *Reset Round*, editing the route in Setup, copying a route and restoring from a backup start them fresh (a backup carries no progress at all). Skipping to a spot while driving, or starting from one on a round that was only part done, keeps those before it and brings back those after it, which alert again and are no longer dropped or missed. Carrying on from here leaves every bundle as it was (see Off the route). Starting from a spot after a round that was finished, or over but for its bundles, is a new round, as *Start New Round* is: nothing from the last round is dropped or missed, so its papers are not counted in the list or the bar. (A round counts as finished when every stop has been done. A no-throw house, special request or apartment block within the alert distance of the very end of the route is never driven past, so a round with one there never counts as finished; the start screen then offers *Continue Round* and *Start Over*, and *Start Over* is the way to a new round. A bundle drop still to do leaves a round not finished too, until it is dropped, missed or marked in the list. A round over but for its bundles still leads with *Start New Round* (see Round), and *Choose Where to Start* on it is a new round too.) Changing the side while driving keeps them for the passes of the route that still exist. What a change of side while driving did to the houses at the head of a court (see Changing the throw side while driving) is kept with the round as well, so it too survives a reload and *Continue Round*, and starting over, *Start Round* (on a round with nothing done and nowhere to carry on from), *Reset Round*, editing the route in Setup, copying a route and restoring from a backup forget it (a backup carries none of it), so a house it left out alerts again on the new drive. Your order of bundle drops is not progress but your own list, kept with the route: starting over, *Reset Round*, skipping to a spot and editing the route in Setup all keep it, copying a route copies it, and a backup carries it once you have set one (see Notes).

**The bottom of the round** is kept slim, with nothing floating over the map. One bar about 56 px high runs along it. From the left it shows the number of special stops left with *stops left* under it (a green tick and *All done* when there are none), then the middle, then a three-dot *Menu* button. The middle shows the first of these that applies: a problem with the GPS that stays until it is put right (*Location permission denied.* or *Waiting for GPS signal...*), which takes the middle and leaves the count and the menu; *Finding your location...* until the phone's first fix; the next bundle drop, when the route has bundle drops (above); and only when there is nothing else to say, *Alerts at 40 m* as a plain note, since the alert distance is changed in the menu and not here. *Keep the phone awake so GPS keeps updating.* is not about where the phone is, so it does not take the middle: while the screen is not being kept awake a small amber warning button, 44 px square, stands just left of the *Menu* button, and the next bundle stays in view beside it. Tapping it says the words as a notice in the bar for about 6 seconds. Messages are shown in the bar, never over the map. A notice (what changing the side did, *Dropped*, a missed bundle, a next bundle left behind, *Carrying on from here.*) takes the whole line of the bar in a dark panel, and the menu button stays: it lasts about 6 seconds, or 12 seconds when it has an *Undo* button beside it, a long one makes the bar a line taller, and a newer notice replaces an older one.

The *Menu* button opens a sheet from the bottom over a dimmed map, with *Whole route* (see all of it, then come back with *Re-centre*), *Skip to a Spot or Section* (the map to pick a spot, as in Starting part way), *Alert Distance* (with the current one), *Bundles* (the list above, on any route), *Reset Round* (which asks first, as it always has) and the OpenStreetMap credit and *Fix the Map* links, which open in a new tab. Choosing any row but the two links closes the sheet first, so what it opens isn't under it; a bundle alert coming up closes the menu and the Bundles list too, and when either is opened while a Dropped alert, an apartment block's alert or an alert with an apartment block in it is up (anywhere but a phone on its side) it keeps below the alert, so its *Done* is never under the alert (under a bundle drop's banner with the strip it keeps 60 px more room); the menu then scrolls, and on a short portrait screen the list drops its three figures to leave room for the rows. *Done*, or a tap on the dimmed map, closes it, and the side, the next turn, the heading badge and an alert stay in view above the dimming. *Whole route* fits the route with room kept clear round it: 30 px at the left, 70 px at the top for the side and any alert, 70 px at the right for the buttons, and the bar's height, the 8 px gap under it and 30 px at the bottom (so the route ends 30 px above the bar). Setup's map keeps the same margins at the left, top and right, and clears its own panel at the bottom.

Down the right of the map are the buttons used while driving, each 48 px square with a gap between them so a thumb finds the right one in a moving car: the voice (only when the phone can speak), the compass (point the map the way you are heading, or north), the follow button and, at the bottom nearest the thumb, *Zoom in* and *Zoom out*. The follow button is a small filled arrow while the map follows you. When the map has been left where you put it (see Looking around), or the whole route is up, it becomes a big blue button named *Re-centre*. The OpenStreetMap credit, which the map's licence asks for, stays in view as a tiny *(c) OpenStreetMap contributors* note above the left end of the bar, outside the map so it can't turn with it; the word *OpenStreetMap* in it is a link to OpenStreetMap's copyright page (the chip is only about 16 px high and the rest of it takes no tap, so the menu has a full-size row for the same page). The alert, the side and the next turn are drawn above the bar and the buttons, so where a tall alert reaches down to them, as on a short screen, its *Dropped* button is the one that takes the tap, not what is under it. On a wide screen (600 px or more) the bar is a panel 440 px wide at the left and the buttons stay at the right. On a short screen (600 px high or less, as a phone on its side is) the buttons stand in two columns, and on a phone on its side the side pill, the next turn and the alerts keep to the left part of the screen (46 percent of its width, but never narrower than 320 px or wider than 440 px, so on a small phone that is more than 46 percent, and clear of a notch), so the middle of the map and the buttons are free; the menu has two columns there, without its lines of explanation, and the Bundles list stands at the right.

**Looking around**: your zoom is your own. A GPS fix never changes it, whether the map is north up or turned, so zooming out to see where to turn stays zoomed out. The map only goes in to street level (zoom 17, or closer if you are already closer) the first time it follows you, and when you come back to following from the whole route. *Zoom in* and *Zoom out* take one step each, about the middle of the map, which is your dot while the map follows you, and each press is one step even when presses come quickly: three quick presses of *Zoom in* end three steps in, the map zooming and then carrying on to the zoom you asked for (it ignores a zoom asked for while it is still zooming, so the buttons keep count of where they are heading). A two-finger pinch while the map follows you zooms about your dot too, so the dot stays put. With the map pointing north, dragging it with a finger pauses following (free look): the map stays where you put it and the follow button becomes the big blue *Re-centre* button. Tap *Re-centre*, or leave the map alone for 15 seconds, and the map goes back to you at the zoom it has, without going back to street level: a short way is glided, and a way of more than a screen is jumped, since a glide that far would show blank map tiles on the way. While you are looking around, each move of the map (the start and the end of a drag or a pinch) starts the 15 seconds again, and so does each press of *Zoom in* or *Zoom out*, as a pinch does. While a finger is on the map it doesn't jump back to you: it returns at the next fix after the finger lifts. A pinch while looking around zooms about your fingers. Zooming alone never pauses following. Showing the whole route, or switching the compass, ends free look too, and from the whole route *Re-centre* goes back to street level. With the map pointing the way you are heading, one finger can't drag the map (so it can't be knocked off you), but the zoom buttons and a two-finger pinch work.

**What the round map draws**: the route is drawn in layers, so what is ahead is always on top at full strength. The part you have already driven (up to the furthest you have got) is faded and sits underneath, and its direction arrows are left off, not faded. On a street you drive twice (down and back, say) the pass still to come stays bright and is never washed out by the faded one, and the two passes are still drawn side by side. All the white outlines sit under all the coloured lines, so one section's outline never cuts into another's line, and the stops, marks, your position and the dashed guide line always sit above the lines. Stops you have done are drawn pale (a stop on a street driven twice is done when every pass of it that alerts is done), and a no-throw house that never alerts, because nothing is thrown on its side or the stretch is Transit, is drawn small (about 60 percent of the size) and the same pale, so it reads as a place with no alert, not a job. Houses that alert are full size and turn pale when done. An apartment block is a teal rounded square with a white building in it, where the other stops are circles, drawn over the route's lines but under the other stops and your position, and faded when done. Setup draws every stop in full colour.

Where you are along the route is judged against where you were a moment ago and the way you are heading, not just by the nearest line, so a street you drive more than once (down and back, say) is told pass from pass, and the arrow shows only the turn for the pass you are on, never one from later in the route (on Transit, a heading that agrees with none of the turns is given the next one by its place on the route: see Turn directions on Transit). If you turn round short of where the route turns (a three-point turn part way down a court, or a U-turn made a little early), you are put on the way back after about three fixes in a row of the phone heading more than 120 degrees against the way out (at 3 m/s, about 11 km/h, or more, when the phone says how fast it is going), so the stops and turns of the way back come up as you drive it (turning round 250 m or more short of the end of a street driven out and back skips enough of the route to be asked about: see Off the route). Where the route itself turns round close by (a U-turn drawn on the spot, or the end of a short court, within about 45 m along the route), two fixes in a row with the phone heading out along the way back, or the road just after it, at 1.5 m/s (about 5 km/h, from which the app takes the phone's course) or more, when the phone says how fast it is going, are enough, and the route from the place level with you on the way in round to the way back is not counted against moving there: so after *U-turn here* a few metres into a side street, where the route turns round 30 to 40 m in, you are not held at the corner while you drive 30 to 80 m up the road out, even when you slow to give way at the junction (but now and then still, see *U-turn* in Setup). One fix with the course the wrong way round does not do it, and going on along the road past the junction of a court 8 to 12 m deep seldom does: with a course from the phone, in 6 of 2,880 simulated drives past one at 3 to 8 m/s, and 18 of 2,880 at 1.6 to 2.5 m/s (in traffic, say); you are then put on the road after the court, at the right place, but as a turn round not sure yet, so the furthest you have got is held for about 100 m. (Going on past a court 16 to 45 m deep, where the route goes on up the road, counts as a turn round not sure yet more often, as the move on past its junction does (see below): with a course from the phone, past one 16 m deep in 64 to 80 of 160 simulated drives at 1.6 to 3 m/s, 18 of 160 at 6 m/s and none at 8 m/s, and past one 20 or 25 m deep in 118 to 153 of 160 at 2 to 8 m/s; past one 30 or 45 m deep it nearly always does, with or without a course.) It is not sure of that, however you were put there, until you have gone 100 m along the way back, since a car backing up on the way out (past a house, say, by up to about 90 m at a brisk reversing pace on a street, drawn with no points along it or with a point every 5 to 40 m, for a phone that gives its course, and up to 70 m on the way into a court) heads against the way out just as one that has turned round does, and moves along the way back as it reverses: until then the furthest you have got stays where it was, and if you drive on along the way out instead (three fixes in a row heading against the way back), you are put back on the way out, near where you are, with its stops still to come, by the third fix going forward (the fourth on a street drawn with points along it). Backed up 80 to 90 m in a court 200 m deep, you are put back on the way in later: at 80 m by the third fix going forward in half or more of simulated drives and by the fourth (now and then the sixth) in the rest, and at 90 m by the fifth or sixth (now and then the fourth). Backed up 90 m, now and then the furthest you have got is left about 300 m on, at the court's way back (in up to 2 of 12 simulated drives with no points along the court, and up to 5 of 12 with a point every 5 or 10 m; in none of those backed up 80 m). The turn round then counts as sure, so an alert that was up as you backed up, such as a no-throw house just ahead of you in the court, can count as done, and then does not come up as you drive on past it, on the way in or on the way back. If you leave the route within 100 m of its end before the turn is sure (turned round short on the last street of the round and going home), the furthest you got goes on to where you left, so leaving at the end of the round is going home, not leaving the route (see Off the route). Until the turn round is sure, the alerts that were up when you were put on the way back stay up: they are not counted as passed, a bundle drop among them is not counted as missed, and the banner does not show one as passed while you drive the way back (one you are still at, such as a house at the turn-round of a court, still shows). A stop of the way back that comes up and that you go past in that time is neither done nor alerted again until the turn is sure. Once it is, about 100 m along the way back, the way-out stops that were up count as done and a bundle drop among them as missed, with its *Missed bundle* notice, and so do the stops of the way back gone past meanwhile, just as they would have at once, only later. If you drive on along the way out instead, the alerts that were up are still up, with no new beep, and go only once you pass them (one you backed away from by more than its alert distance and 15 m can go, and come up again with its beep as you come back to it), and a stop of the way back that came up meanwhile can come up again when you really drive it (in a court 200 m deep drawn with points along it, after backing up 80 m it did in every simulated drive, and after 90 m in all but up to 5 of 12, as when the reversing itself takes you 100 m along the way back the turn counts as sure: see above). All this about backing up holds for a back-up that starts 30 m or more before where the route turns round (the end of a street driven out and back, or the tip of a court): in simulated drives starting 30 to 85 m from it, the house just ahead was never left unalerted and the bundle drop just behind was missed while reversing only once, at 35 m. Starting within about 25 m of it, the first fix of the reverse can put you on the way back as if you had driven there, as it is no further on than the phone can have moved (with the slack for the fix's error), and the house just ahead can then go unalerted and the bundle drop just behind be counted as missed while you reverse: on a street 300 m out and back, starting 25 m from the end, each in 15 of 144 simulated drives, and starting 15 m from it, in 28 and 69 of 144; in a court 200 m deep, starting 20 m from the tip, in 14 and 24 of 96. (It used to happen starting as far as 45 m from the end, with the phone giving its course: in 5 of 12 drives starting there, and 11 or 12 of 12 starting 25 to 40 m from it.) A phone with no course of its own (whose heading is only the way it has been going), backing up 40 to 90 m on a route drawn with a point every 5 to 10 m, can be left on the way back for the rest of the way out, in up to 6 of 12 simulated drives, though the furthest you have got stays right (within 5 m), and the stops of the way out still ahead of you then do not come up: in a court 200 m deep drawn so, backing up 70 to 90 m at 4 m/s, a no-throw house 40 m ahead of where the back-up began was not alerted, and the round could not finish, in up to 6 of 12 drives (with no points along the court, or one every 40 m, it is left on the way back more often, in up to 12 of 12 drives, the house was not alerted in up to 8, and the furthest you have got was put more than 150 m on in up to 6); on a street 300 m out and back drawn with a point every 10 m, backing up 90 m at 4 m/s, the house just ahead was not alerted in 9 of 12; and on a street drawn with a point every 5 to 40 m, backing up 30 to 90 m, being left on the way back was taken for coming back onto the route further on, which is asked about and, left alone, carried on from there (see Off the route), in up to 4 of 12. Backing up a few metres inside a court 20 to 40 m deep (4 to 8 m, at 1.6 to 4 m/s) and driving on in puts you on the way back for a while in most simulated drives (in a court 40 m deep, in all 48 of 48 at 1.6 to 2.5 m/s and 40 of 48 at 3 to 4 m/s, for 8 to 10 fixes, while you drive up to about 25 m back in with the card showing the turn out; in one 20 or 30 m deep, in all 48), as a turn round not sure yet (in 35 to 48 of 48 in a court 30 or 40 m deep, but in only 3 or 4 of 48 in one 20 m deep at 2 to 4 m/s, and none at 1.6 m/s), and driving on in puts you back on the way in: no alert was lost in those drives (in courts 30 to 60 m deep with a no-throw house every 6 m along both legs, none went unalerted or came up late), and none ended with the round having lost track of the car. (A back-up that starts within about 25 m of the tip is the case above, though: in a court 40 m deep, backing up from 16 m in with a no-throw house 6 m ahead, the house was not alerted again in 40 of 240 drives.) Within 20 m of the end of the route, where there is no room left for the turn to become sure, it is taken to stand, so the round can finish there, and so it is when the phone is too far from the route to place. Starting, skipping or carrying on while the turn is not sure takes it as it stands: if you were put at or past where the turn put you, the stops it held or put by that you have gone by count as done, and a bundle drop among them as missed, and the spot then decides every stop as usual (carrying on from here, every stop but the bundles: see Off the route). *Continue Round* picks up from the furthest of where the round was started or skipped to, the furthest you got (kept every 100 m) and the last special stop done, which tells the passes apart at the start too. A stop at the turn-round of a court, which one pass takes in on both legs (see Throw Side), counts for this only as far as the leg you come to first, since it may have been done on either, so *Continue Round* never picks up on the way back of a court from it, past stops on the way in still to do. At the first GPS fix back on the route after *Continue Round*, and only then, a no-throw house at the head of a court (see Throw Side) that is not done, whose alert is on a leg more than 15 m behind you (or the alert distance if that is less) and that is thrown on the other leg, still ahead, is put on that other leg, and comes up before you pass it there, whether or not it was up when you left the Round tab (or the app reloaded): with the screen left on the way in and *Continue Round* pressed a few metres past the tip of a court closed with *U-turn here*, say. That fix only ever moves such a house: it never leaves one out or counts one done. (Thrown *Both* both ways, with the screen left on the way in and *Continue Round* pressed 4 to 16 m past the tip, no house went without its alert, of 72 simulated house-drives at each of 10, 20 and 40 m, where 64, 48 and 48 used to with *U-turn here*, and 8 at 10 m with an exact reversal; left with the alert up inside the U-turn, none of 156 did at 40 m and 1 at 60 m, where 56 and 64 used to.) Any other stop that was up when you left the Round tab, and that is more than 20 m behind you (or the alert distance if that is less) at that fix, never comes up, and holds up the round: nothing else is worked out again from where you are. (The exception is the bundle drop next in your list, which with every pass to do behind you alerts by where you are: see Bundle drops.) A house at the head of a court that a change of side left out, or kept on the leg ahead, is as the change left it (see Changing the throw side while driving). A bundle drop that was dropped does not count as a stop done for this, nor do the other passes of its stop that the drop settled: a bundle can be marked dropped in the list however far ahead it is, so it says nothing of where you got to, and marking bundles dropped from the list never moves where *Continue Round* carries on from. That comes from where you got to, saved every 100 m, so right after a drop it can carry on up to 100 m behind where you were.

Going past a court on the route without going in, you are held at its junction until the phone has lost touch with it, about 25 to 35 m past for a court about 20 to 60 m deep (or one 16 m deep, going slowly), and then, at the first fix that has lost touch, put on the street beyond at once (beyond a deeper court, the jump along the route is so big that it is doubted for two fixes first). Put there at once, you count as having turned round, not sure yet (see above), since you may only have missed the turn into the court: the alerts that were up, the court's stops, are held, not counted done (nor a bundle drop among them missed), and the furthest you have got is held, until you are about 100 m along the street beyond, when they count as done (and the bundle drop as missed) as a turn round that has become sure does. That is so in nearly every drive past a court 30 or 45 m deep, and less often past a shallower or a deeper one (in 19, 93, 96, 28 and 0 of 96 simulated drives past courts 20, 30, 45, 60 and 100 m deep, at 6 to 14 m/s). If you did miss the turn and come back to the junction, backing up or turning round on the street, you are put back at the junction, and the court's no-throw house and bundle drop then come up as you drive the court. Only coming back to the junction itself does that: the move on past it is taken back only for a phone within reach of the junction (25 m, or 10 m more than the fix's accuracy, as for losing touch), so if you go by the court and then back up a few metres on the street beyond, for a house just passed or to park, you stay on the street beyond (going by courts 30 to 60 m deep, stopping 40 to 110 m past and backing up 5 to 20 m at 2 to 4 m/s, the court's alert came back on the banner after the back-up in 63 of 36,288 simulated drives, all of them stopping 40 m past and backing up to within about 25 m of the junction, and the next no-throw house, 8 to 25 m on from where you stopped, was not up from 15 m before it to 1 m past it in 14; with the move taken back from anywhere within about 100 m of the junction, in 2,433 and 70). Missing the turn into a court 30 or 45 m deep at 10 m/s, stopping 35 m past it, then backing up at 3 m/s or turning round, on a court drawn with its corners only or with a point every 10 m, the house in the court came up and the bundle drop was not missed before you got to it in 90 of 96 simulated drives; the other 6 were all on the court 30 m deep drawn with a point every 10 m, where the house was passed with no alert, and in 4 of them the bundle drop too (taken as a plain move on, not held, both were lost in all 96). Over more such drives (courts 20 to 60 m deep, missed by 15 to 55 m, 3,840 drives), the house in the court was never alerted in 682, against 1,118 with the street beyond taken only once the junction was out of reach, 55 to 60 m past: in 563, 71, 46 and 2 of 960 at courts 20, 30, 45 and 60 m deep, so at a court 20 m deep it was still lost more often than not (and the bundle drop missed before you got to it in 539 of 960). The cost is that 48 of those drives ended with the round unfinished, against 10 (all at courts 45 and 60 m deep: 32 and 16 of the 48). A no-throw house on the street beyond, 35 or 45 m past the junction of a court 30 or 45 m deep, always came up, though late for one 35 m past: only as you drew level with it (within 3 m, either side) in 22 of 96 drives, and 3 to 10 m before it in 47 (one 45 m past came up 10 m or more before it in 74 of 96, and 3 to 10 m before it in the rest). One beyond a court 60 m deep came up by the time you were 3 m past it in only 39 and 45 of 96 drives 35 and 45 m past (at 6 to 14 m/s, with and without a course from the phone, on courts drawn with their corners only or with a point every 10 m), later in 34 and 42, and never in 23 and 9; one beyond a court 100 m deep in only 22 and 36 of 96, later in 47 and 51, and never in 27 and 9. A house 12 to 25 m past the junction is beside the street before the court too, which ends at the junction, so it also has a pass level with the junction, and that pass or its pass on the street beyond is up, and on the banner, as you come to it (it was silent in none of 96 drives for each court 20 to 100 m deep, 12 or 25 m past it).

Turning round well short of the tip of a court 45 m deep or more is not always caught: a spin or a three-point turn 20 m into a court 60 m deep, or 30 m into one 100 m deep, can leave too few fixes heading out for three in a row, so you are held at the junction all the way out and then put on the street beyond, and the pass on the way back of a house that alerts both ways, which you drove past going in, is never alerted as you pass it coming out, nor counted done, so the round does not finish (with a course from the phone, in up to 7 of 12 simulated drives at a court 60 m deep and 11 of 12 at one 100 m deep; with none, in courts 45 to 150 m deep turned round 10 to 70 m short of the tip, in up to all 12 of 12).

**Leaving the road for a stop**: being near a part of the route is not being on it. If you drive off the street to a stop set back from it, or across a street you have yet to come to, you stay where you left the route, at any speed and however long you wait there, until the phone is within about 40 m of the other part and driving along it, the way it runs, for about three fixes in a row (under about 5 km/h, for about 10 m along it). So a slow visit to a stop on a street you drive again later brings you back on the pass you left, not the later one, even where that lies a few metres nearer the stop: the next bundle is not told of as behind you, nothing is counted missed, and *Continue Round* does not start from the later pass. (Of 96 simulated visits to such a stop, at 1 to 10 m/s, waiting up to 2 minutes, 63 used to be thrown to the later pass, and none are now.) Coming back onto a later part of the route the wrong way round, you are put on it after about 100 m. Two things can't be told from being there. Driving up a street the route drives later, the way it will drive it, you are put on it after those three fixes, and the furthest you have got goes with you (you are asked about 120 m on, see Off the route, but not if you turn back sooner). And a phone that is moving but gives no heading at all has nothing to show which way it is going, so three fixes near a place are enough for it, as they always were.

**Starting part way**: under the start buttons, *Choose Where to Start* opens a map of the route. Tap the line where you want to begin, or pick *Beginning of the route* or a named section from the list (a section shows how many stops it has; when you skip while driving it also shows how many of them are not done yet), and tap *Start Here*. The stops before that spot count as done and the ones after it as not done, whatever was done before (and on a round that was finished, or over but for its bundles, the bundles dropped and missed last time are forgotten too), so it also takes you back if you want to redo a part; a stop just before the spot (within 15 m, or within the alert distance if that is shorter) still comes up. Where the route goes along a road more than once and a tap could mean either time, it asks which time through you mean (unless the passes are separate throw-side sections, where the line you tap says). The round then carries on from the spot: nothing alerts until the first GPS fix says where you are, the part of the route before the spot is drawn as already driven and has no turn cards, and *Continue Round* later picks up from the furthest of there and where you got to (the start screen says where). The map shows where you are while you choose. While you drive, *Skip to a Spot or Section* in the menu opens the same map with a *Skip to Here* button. It asks first, saying how many stops that are not done yet will count as done and how many done ones will come up again, and the round keeps the way it was heading. If your first fix of 30 m or better on the route is a little way (under 300 m) past the spot, the stops in between count as done too, but for the bundles, which stay to do: the next one stays *Next*, alerts by place if you come to it, and you are told once if you leave it behind (see Bundle drops). A skip to a spot behind the car is different: it has no stretch to catch up, so the houses and bundles after the spot stay to do on the first fix, and come up again if you drive back. A start from a spot on the start screen still catches up, as above. A skip changes which stops count as done and where *Continue Round* picks up from, not where you are: the next GPS fix still puts you where you really are on the route, so use it where you are driving.

**Sections on the round**: as you drive into a named section (see Setup, Sections), a banner under the side and the turn says *Start* in green with its name, and as you drive out of one it says *End* in dark grey with its name. A section runs to where the next one starts, so the two usually come together, the end over the start, and with the voice on it says *End of Oak Estate. Start of Beach Road.* only if nothing else is to be said, as a warning or a turn matters more: anything else takes its place or breaks in on it, and the banner shows it either way. A section named Start or End, in capitals or not, is said as *the Start section* or *the End section* (*End of the Start section. Start of Area Two.*), while the banner shows just its name. The banner stays for 8 seconds and takes no touch. A stop's alert comes first, with the banner under it, or beside it on a phone on its side, where it can lie over the map's buttons for its 8 seconds. With the list, the menu, the picker or a question open it waits for them to close, and then has its 8 seconds, unless they stay open for 30 seconds, when it goes unseen and only the newest was kept. The last section ends within 50 m of the end of the route, and the section you are in ends where the last stop is done, if that is sooner: with every stop done nothing more is told. The part of the route before the first section has no name, so it has no banner.

Each start and end is told once, however the GPS goes to and fro over the line, and going back into a section you have left says nothing. A round started, or skipped to a spot, tells of the section you are in once you get to the spot, as short of it you are still on your way there, while *Continue Round* does not, as you are carrying on in it. With nothing left to do at a spot started from, past the last stop, nothing is told. A skip starts the telling over, so a section driven again after a skip back is told again. Carrying on from here does not: it tells of the section you left and the one you are in, if you have gone forward. It takes a fix of 30 m or better while you are within 30 m of the route, going the way it runs, and a turn round not sure yet waits until it is sure. A move along the route faster than a car can go waits until there has been time for it, so a jump along the route tells of nothing, and a stretch of poor GPS over a line is told at the end of it, a little late, or after a stop off the road when the round carries on. Two lines gone over between two fixes tell of the first section ending and the last starting, and not of the one between. A route with no named sections has no banners.

**Changing the throw side while driving**: if a side was put in wrongly, tap the side at the top of the screen (*Port*, *Starboard*, *Both* or *Transit*). Choose the side, then how much it applies to: from the last turn (or the start of the stretch) to the next turn (or its end), from here to the next turn, the named section you are in (only when you are in one), from here to the end of the stretch, or the whole stretch. Only the choices that make sense are listed (nothing shorter than 15 m, and none that repeat another), and a named section changes only the part of it inside this stretch. Only that part changes: the route is split there, and the rest of the stretch keeps its side. A part that is from here on starts where the car is when you choose, not where you tapped. The notice in the bar says what that does to the no-throw houses, counting each house on its own, including any you have passed (for example *2 no-throw houses will now stay quiet; 1 no-throw house will now alert*: a house only alerts on a side that is thrown, so a change from Port to Starboard can do both, and a house on a street driven twice can go quiet on one pass only). Houses that start to alert behind you count as done at once, and it says so (but see a house at the head of a court, below). A change at the head of a court that would move a house's alert onto the leg you have already driven keeps it on the leg still ahead, when the house is thrown there: thrown starboard both ways and changed to *Both* at the tip, a house on the left going in still comes up before you pass it coming back. One that the change leaves thrown only on the leg behind you, while you still have to pass it on the leg ahead, where its side is not thrown, does not come up and is left out of the stops left and the total, but does not count as done, so the round can finish without it. What the change did is saved with the round, so the house stays left out after you leave the Round tab (or the app reloads): the start screen leaves it out of the stops left and the total (*2 of 3 done*, say, not *2 of 4*), a round with nothing else left shows there as finished, with *Start New Round*, and *Continue Round* carries on with the house left out, wherever you are when you press it. It puts back what the change did as it was, and the first fix after it only moves a house onto the leg ahead, where it is thrown, when you are between that court's two legs (see above), and never leaves one out, so a house at the head of a court further on, thrown going in only, still alerts as usual, even when that first fix is beside a later street of the route or between the two legs of that court. Taking the change back before you pass it (*Undo*, or changing the side back in any way that throws its side on the leg ahead, *Both* included) brings its alert back on the leg ahead, and it comes up before you pass it: thrown starboard both ways and changed to *Port* at the tip, then undone, or changed back to *Starboard* 2 m on with *From the last turn to the end of this stretch* (the first choice offered there, which leaves the way in on *Port*, so the house is thrown on both legs), a house on the left going in still comes up before you pass it coming back. Taken back once you are past it on both legs, it counts as done. That is only for a house whose alert on the leg behind is more than 15 m behind you (or the alert distance if that is less): one within that is left as it is, so it can come up just behind you and then counts as done as you drive on, and taking the change back does not bring it back. A house at the head of a court that starts to alert behind you while it is quiet on the leg still ahead is left out in the same way, not done. One that is thrown on the leg ahead too is kept on the leg ahead, though it did not alert before the change (it was quiet, left out or on *Transit*), and comes up before you pass it there; it counts as done only once you are past it on both legs. So with a court driven in *Transit* both ways and changed to *Both* at the tip, the houses at the tip come up before you pass them coming back. A house the change kept on the leg ahead stays there after you leave the Round tab and press *Continue Round*, as that is saved with the round too, so with *Both* at the tip it still comes up before you pass it coming back; a house changed to *Both* at the tip that was still up on the way in, not done, is put on the way back at the first fix after *Continue Round*, so it too comes up before you pass it coming back (at 40 m it still went without its alert in 12 of 54 simulated drives, where it used to in 48); and changing the side back after *Continue Round* still brings a house that was left out back on the leg ahead. Starting part way or skipping to a spot between the two legs of the court keeps what the change did (the safe way: a house kept on the leg ahead stays there, still to come); anywhere else the spot decides every stop. While a turn round is not sure yet (the first 100 m along the way back, or a car backing up on a street driven out and back: see above), a house that starts to alert, or whose alert moves, behind where the turn put you but not behind where you turned (by more than 15 m, or the alert distance if that is less) counts as done only once the turn is sure; if you were only backing up and drive on along the way out, it is still to come and alerts as you reach it. One behind where you turned counts as done at once. *Undo* is beside it for twelve seconds. It needs a GPS fix of 65 m or better, within 40 m of the route (or 2.5 times the fix's error if more), and the app has to know where you are, so right after starting or skipping it says it can't tell until the first fix lands on the route. The change is saved with the route, and what was done in the round stays done.

**Off the route**: if you are more than 50 m in a straight line from the nearest part of the route, wherever it is and not only the part the app has you on (more than twice the GPS fix's error, when that is worse than 25 m), on fixes of 40 m or better, while you are moving (faster than walking pace, about 5 km/h), for three fixes in a row and at least four seconds, an amber banner says *Off the route* with how far it is, in a straight line, to where you left it and which way (ahead of you, behind you, on your left or right, by the way the phone is heading). A dashed line on the map runs there, two falling notes sound (whether the voice is on or not), and with the voice on it says *Off route*. While you are moving and still off, the notes sound again about every 30 seconds and the voice says *Still off route*. When you are back within 40 m of the route for two fixes, and within 150 m along it of where you left, the banner goes, and with the voice on it says *Back on route* (stops in the stretch you skipped over, if you came back further on, count as done, but for the bundles: see Bundle drops). It does not go off while you are stopped, while you are within the distance above of the route, before you have reached the route at the start of a round (it counts as reached when you are on it within 100 m along it of where the round starts, or after you have driven 200 m along it, so starting or skipping to a spot, and crossing the route on the way to its start, are not leaving it, and nor is driving there along the route itself, see below), or while you visit a stop that is away from the route (within how far off the route the stop is, plus its alert distance and 60 m, of the stop). As the distance is to the nearest part of the route, driving on past a court or cul-de-sac on the route that you don't drive to its end, or don't go into at all, is not taken for leaving the route, so it doesn't bring up the offer to carry on from further on (below), though a poor fix, out by about 20 m or more, still can now and then. A turn missed onto another part of the route is still asked about, though the phone never leaves the route: when where you are along the route jumps by more than 450 m beyond the ground between (a court 150 m deep skipped both ways, and 150 m more), it is held, and once you have gone on 120 m it is asked about as coming back onto the route somewhere else (below). That is a missed turn into a loop round a block that comes back to the street, for a block about 150 m a side or bigger (one of 150 m driven past at about 50 km/h goes untold about one time in four, one of 120 m is told about half the time, and one of 100 m or less, up to about 400 m of route, is skipped in silence, like a court); a turn onto a street already driven, told as earlier on; a street driven out and back, about 250 m long or longer, that you don't turn into; and turning round 250 m or more short of the end of a street driven out and back (about 200 m short is asked about one time in three, and less only now and then). A court or a street driven out and back up to about 200 m long that you go by is skipped in silence. Several courts close together that you go by without driving them can add up to one jump of more than 450 m, though, and be asked about (*Back on the route, 700 metres further on.*): three courts 80 to 100 m deep, 30 m apart on alternate sides, were asked about in about half of simulated drives (35 of 72), two such courts only rarely (1 of 72), and courts 60 m or more apart, or 60 m deep, not at all. On the way along the route itself to where the round was started, skipped to or carried on from, a loop or a street driven out and back that lies before that spot is not asked about: the missed-turn rule (a jump of more than 450 m with the phone on the route all the time) starts once you are on the route within 100 m (along it) of the spot, or past it. This holds for starting part way, *Skip to a Spot or Section*, *Continue Round* and carrying on from here (by the button or by itself). Until then a turn missed onto the route itself is not asked about; going off the route still is. A stretch with no GPS fixes (Safari in the background for a call, or a screen that locked) or only poor ones (worse than 40 m) while you drive the route is not taken for a missed turn either. Once a fix of 40 m or better comes more than 10 s after the last one, a jump along the route from where you were last followed is taken up quietly if the time since can explain it at up to 20 m/s (about 70 km/h). A bigger jump is still asked about, and so is going off the route. Fixes keep coming while you are stopped, so a stop is not a gap. Time near a stop away from the route that is not done yet does count as a gap, though: every fix within how far off the route the stop is, plus its alert distance and 60 m, of the stop, whether you visit it or only drive past it on the route, since where you are is not followed there. So a turn missed there, into a loop round a 150 m block beside such a stop, is not asked about when the time spent near the stop explains the jump at 20 m/s: at about 20 km/h it never was (0 of 24 simulated drives), at about 36 km/h it was every time (24 of 24) and at about 50 km/h 23 times in 24. The other way round, two courts 120 m deep and 120 m apart, gone by beside such a stop, are asked about as one jump of more than 450 m at about 36 km/h and over (24 of 24; at about 20 km/h, 0 of 24), which with no such stop near they never are. It stays quiet once the round is over (every stop done, or the furthest you have got is within 50 m of the end of the route). Once it has gone off, a moving phone is reminded until it is back within 40 m of the route.

While you are off the route, or on a part of it that is not where you left it, the turn card and the spoken turns are left off, since they would be for the wrong place (the heading badge stays, as it only says which way the phone is going), and a turn that is still ahead of you when you are back is said again (with the voice on). An alert that was showing when you left goes, and comes up again when you are back; a stop already behind the place you left counts as done, and a bundle drop among them as missed, with its notice (see Bundle drops). Stop alerts need the app to know where you are along the route, which it does only within about 60 m of it (more with a poor fix), so houses on the route do not alert while you are further off than that; stops set away from the route still do, and so does a bundle drop alerting by where you are (see Bundle drops).

If you come back onto the route somewhere else, it doesn't quietly re-aim. Once you have been on the new part for about 120 m (this also catches a short cross street between two streets of the route, if it skips more than about 200 m of route, and a turn missed onto the route itself, see above), the notes sound, the voice says how far further on or earlier on you came back onto it, and the banner offers two buttons and says what *Carry on from here* would do to the stops, and that left alone it carries on from here after about a minute. *Carry on from here* carries the round on from where you are now, without asking, as *Skip to Here* does: stops before that place count as done and stops after it come up again, even ones already done, so coming back earlier on re-opens the part since. Every bundle is left exactly as it was, dropped, missed or to do, on either side of that place, as you may be going back for one or working down your list out of route order, and the banner counts only the other stops (a next bundle left behind that way is told of: see Bundle drops). While the banner is up, a bundle alert going by is a real miss, and other stops going by stay as they were (see Bundle drops). Carrying on by itself (below) does the same; skipping from the menu and starting from a spot do not (see Starting part way). *Take me back* keeps the dashed line guiding you to where you left (the banner reads *Heading back* and is quiet: if you leave the route again on the way, it reads *Off the route* but with no notes and no voice), and the turns come back once you are within 150 m along the route of that place; if you keep going along the route instead, until you are 500 m further along it than the nearest you have been to where you left, it carries on from where you are. If you press neither within a minute of the banner appearing, it carries on from where you are at your next good fix on the route while you are moving, with a short message in the bar (*Carrying on from here.*) and the voice, if it is on, says *Carrying on from here*, but no notes, unless you are driving back towards where you left, which it takes as *Take me back*. Streets less than about 80 m apart, a cut-through that skips less than about 200 m of route, or a turn missed onto the route itself that jumps less than 450 m beyond the ground between (see above), can still be crossed to without a warning, and the wrong pass of a street you drive twice can be taken for the right one for a short while.

**Map pointing**: the compass button with the map buttons turns the map to point the way you are heading instead of north (the choice is remembered). Your dot sits in the lower part of the screen, as low as it can and still clear the bar along the bottom with about 72 px to spare (never lower than 82 percent of the way down nor higher than halfway; the bar's real height is measured, so a long notice that makes it taller moves the dot up a little), with a small blue arrow above it, so more of the road ahead shows. It follows the direction of travel the phone reports; when the phone has none it uses the way you have been going over the last few dozen metres, and at the start of a round it points along the route. It holds still when you stop, so it doesn't spin at a red light. While it is on and the map follows you, one finger can't drag the map, and two fingers still zoom about your dot, as do *Zoom in* and *Zoom out*; the zoom is your own, see Looking around. *Whole route* in the menu always shows north up, and *Re-centre* or the compass brings the turned map back. Labels and house numbers stay upright, and a house number goes on the side of the house away from the road as it looks on the screen. The heading badge at the top right agrees with the turned map (see Heading badge). On the round the OpenStreetMap credit is the small note above the left end of the bar, so it can't turn with the map.

**Turn directions**: when a turn in your route is within 200 m (on a Transit stretch the card is always up, see below), a dark card beside the side pill shows an arrow bent the way the route turns, what to do (*Turn left*, *Bear right*, *Sharp left*, *U-turn*) and how far away it is, counting down to *Now*. It stays until you are 15 m past the turn, but when the next turn starts less than 30 m after it, the card moves on to that one earlier: 15 m before it starts, though never before the end of this turn's swing (for a corner drawn with a point, as soon as you reach the corner). A turn that the furthest you have got is through never comes back, however your place wanders, so in a short court the turn in does not come back once you are through it, nor the U-turn; a fix that falls short of them can still bring one back for that fix (see What is left below). Just past a corner whose card moved on early, a phone still heading the way into that corner counts for the next turn too, as a heading lags a turn. Not so past a U-turn, where only the heading can say you have turned round, as a place just past it is also a place just before it: there a phone still heading within 60 degrees of the way in keeps the U-turn up until you are 15 m past it or at the next turn, and one not yet round is shown nothing beyond the turn after it. A U-turn that is the next turn stays up for any heading nearer its way in than its way out, and a phone heading out of the corner before a close U-turn moves the card on to it, though the fix lags. On the round, a court 8 to 16 m deep at 55 to 125 degrees to the road (see U-turns below) is shown and said as the turn in, the U-turn and the turn out, in that order, with the U-turn said before you turn round and nothing after the turn out, every time where the app knows exactly where you are, crawling with no heading or at 3 to 6 m/s (about 11 to 22 km/h). Tracked from fixes 3 m out at 3 to 6 m/s, they come in order in 98 to 99 percent of simulated drives, the U-turn is said before you turn round in 99.2 to 99.7 percent, and the turn in or the U-turn is shown or said after the turn out in 0.7 to 1.2 percent. Crawling with no course (under 1.5 m/s, where the tracker moves in steps of about 6 m), the U-turn is said before you turn round in 59 percent of drives (35 percent in a court 8 m deep, 49 at 10 m, 65 at 12 m and 88 at 16 m), and the turn in or the U-turn comes after the turn out in 5.5 percent. What is left, at 3 to 6 m/s: a fix that puts the car on the road past the court as it reaches the corner, so the turn out is said first (24 of 3,840 drives, in courts at 120 to 125 degrees) or the U-turn not at all (15, at 55 to 90 degrees); a fix that lags at the corner until you have turned round, so the U-turn is not said either (2); in a court 16 m deep at 55 to 60 degrees, the turn-in card coming back for one fix after the U-turn's (6); in a court 8 m deep at 6 m/s, where a fix comes only every 6 m, the U-turn said only as you turn round (4 of its 320 drives); and on the road out of a court 8 m deep at 6 m/s, when that road heads within 60 degrees of the way into the court (one at 55 to 60 degrees driven on, or at 120 to 125 driven back), the U-turn's card coming back for one fix after the turn out (12 of 320, where it was 2). The voice does not say either card again. That last is the cost of keeping the U-turn up when a fix puts you past it before you get there: in those 320 drives it is said in time in 316, where it was 286. When the street names have been looked up (see Setup), the street the turn leads into is under the distance, and the voice says it (*Turn left onto Oak Road*; it says the first part of a name in two languages, and says *St*, *Rd* and the like in full, while the card shows the whole name). It is dark so it can't be mistaken for a stop alert, which are blue, red, purple and teal, and it has nothing to tap. The turns are worked out from the shape of the line you drew, so the app knows nothing of junctions, and its street names are only what the lookup found: a corner, or a curve tight enough to be one, is a turn; a road that merely bends along its length is not; and a roundabout is one turn, as far as it turns you (going straight on through it is none). The card is left off when your GPS fix is worse than 65 m, or you are more than 40 m from your route (further if the fix is poor: 2.5 times its error), or the app has lost track of where you are, because then it can't tell where you are along the route. It is also left off while the *Off the route* banner is up, or you are on a part of the route that is not where you left it (see Off the route above).

**Turn directions on Transit**: the card is up only for the next 200 m, which suits streets full of turns and houses but leaves little to go by on a long drive between delivery areas. So on a Transit stretch (the side at your place on the route is Transit) it is always up. While the next turn is more than 200 m away it is a *Continue* card: an arrow straight up on a tile in the Transit pink, *Continue* over the distance to the next turn in big type (for example *3.2 km*), and under that a small arrow bent the way the turn goes with the street it leads into (or *Turn left* and so on when the street is not known). When no turn is left it says *Route ends in* with the distance, then *Route ends* and *Here* when it is right there. Within 200 m it becomes the usual turn card, counting down to *Now*. Distances are shown to the nearest 10 m under 100 m, to the nearest 50 m under a kilometre, then as 3.2 km (whole kilometres from 10 km). Off Transit nothing changes: the card is up only for the next 200 m, and only for a turn within 60 degrees of the heading. The same rules apply as for the usual card: it is left off when the fix is worse than 65 m, you are more than 40 m from the route (more if the fix is poor), the app has lost track of where you are, the *Off the route* banner is up or you are on a part of the route that is not where you left it. Which turn is the next one on Transit is decided in three steps. First, the turn the usual card would show: within 200 m, with the way the route comes up to it within 60 degrees of the way the phone is heading. Failing that, the first turn at any distance that the route comes up to within 120 degrees of the heading, so a road that bends on the way to a far corner, or a heading that is only part off (joining the road, going round a roundabout), doesn't hide it, and a turn close by that the heading is only part against isn't passed over for one further on. Failing that, the next turn by its place on the route, whatever the heading. So the card never says *Route ends in* while a turn is left, and says it only when none is. A turn that comes up the other way along a street you drive twice is passed over for one the heading agrees with, and is shown only when the heading agrees with none.

**Heading badge**: a small badge at the top right of the round screen, in line with the side pill and the turn card (they leave room for it), shows which way the phone is heading: a dial with an arrow, and under it the compass point (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W or NW, each covering 45 degrees centred on itself). It is shown whenever the app knows which way the phone is heading (its own course while it is moving, else the way it has been going over the last few dozen metres), so it can go for a moment at a standstill or in the middle of a corner, and it stays when the turn card is off (off the route, say). It has nothing to tap, and touches go through it to the map. With the map pointing north the ring is still, with a red mark at the top for north, and the arrow turns to the heading. With the map turned to the way you are heading (the compass button) the arrow points up and the ring turns the other way, so the red mark shows where north is on the map. It follows the phone's light or dark look.

**U-turns** are found as one turn, however they were drawn: the route doubling back at a point, a hairpin of two corners a few metres apart, or the way back drawn on the other lane or carriageway (up to about 30 m to the side, with a short piece across, even a slanted one). Two or more turns the same way, each starting within about 30 m of where the last ended, that add up to between 150 and 225 degrees are one turn, and it is put in the middle of the way across, provided they are corners. One of them must be a sharp corner, turning the heading by 70 degrees or more within about 20 m of route. None may be a stretch of curve (40 degrees or more in all, but never 40 degrees within 20 m), and at most one may be a slight bend (under 40 degrees, such as the start of a slanted piece across), which is taken in; a slight bend that is one of a run of bends does not start a U-turn. A swing that is a U-turn on its own takes in only a slight bend just before it: a turn of 40 degrees or more before it, the same way included, is a turn of its own. So a road that curves round (a crescent, a court or a loop ramp, however far round it goes and however the line was thinned) is never a U-turn or a *Sharp* turn, and neither is a curve straight after or before a corner, such as a street turning onto a crescent; a tight half circle (about 25 m radius or less) is still a U-turn. That includes a right turn and another right 25 m later into a car park, since the route does reverse direction. It is shown as *U-turn* when it comes to 165 degrees or more (180 at most) and as *Sharp* if it falls a little short, and it keeps the way round it went, so its arrow turns the right way. Where the route doubles straight back along its own line at one point (a bend of 178.5 degrees or more), the line can't say which way round it went, so it is shown going round to the right, as a U-turn does where traffic keeps left. That holds wherever the reversal falls on the 5 m pieces the route is read in (a piece that ends less than 1 m from where it began takes the heading of the piece before it), except where the road's last point before it is a few metres short of it: there about 1 in 25 still shows the arrow going round to the left (the voice says only *make a U-turn*). A corner either way right next to such a reversal (a U-turn along the road, or *U-turn here* with the road drawn on from it), the same way included, is a turn of its own, shown before or after the *U-turn*, and so is a corner on each side of it: in and out of a short court or stub 8 m or more deep (or a U-turn along the road a little way into a side street) reads as the corner in, the U-turn and the corner out, whichever way you leave (one up to 16 m deep used to read as no turn at all, or a lone U-turn). Both hold for a corner before the U-turn of 45 degrees or more (every time, wherever the pieces fall, for a turn in of 45 and 46 degrees, 8 to 30 m deep). A turn in of a little over 40 degrees can fall on the 5 m pieces as a slight bend under 40, on either side, and is then left out, or taken into the U-turn when it goes the way U-turns go round: at 41 degrees in 17 percent of the places the pieces can fall, 11 at 42, 5.6 at 43 and 0.7 at 44 (before, one of 41 to 44 degrees the way U-turns go round was taken in every time, one of 45 degrees about half the time and one of 46 degrees about 1 in 8). How the round shows and says a short court's three turns is under Turn directions above. Over the places the pieces can fall, a corner next to it reads right: 9 to 12 m after it, every time; 8 m after it, all but about 1 in 300; 8 to 12 m before it, nearly always (all but about 1 in 600 at 8 m and 1 in 1,200 at 9 m, and every time from 10 m); 7 m before it, about 8 times in 10 for a right corner and all but about 1 in 300 for a left one; and 6 m before it, about 6 times in 10 for a right corner and 8 in 10 for a left one. The wrong ones read as a U-turn with its arrow to the left, a *Sharp* turn the other way after a *Turn* or *Bear*, or now and then a single turn the wrong way. A loop round a block (corners further apart than that) stays ordinary turns, and so does a dog-leg (left then right); corners that add up to less than 150 degrees, or more than 225, stay separate turns. A corner the other way right next to a U-turn of two corners is a turn of its own, shown before or after the *U-turn*, when it is 5 m or more before it or 8 m or more after it; that is only where the swing is just those two, a U-turn's worth (150 to 225 degrees) and a corner of 60 degrees or more, so a roundabout, a jog or a chicane is left whole. Some shapes are not found: a U-turn drawn as two corners (a hairpin, the way back on the other lane or carriageway, or the U-turn *U-turn here* draws) with a corner the same way within about 15 m before or after it is shown as a single turn the wrong way round (a U-turn and then a right turn that close reads as *Turn left*; *U-turn here* in Setup starts at least 26 m past a corner to keep clear of this), a corner the other way closer than 5 m before or 8 m after a U-turn can still make one turn of the two, a court or stub shallower than 8 m driven in and straight back out can still read as no turn at all, or a lone U-turn with its arrow either way (about 1 in 5 at 7 m deep, about half at 6 m, nearly all at 5 m or less), a slanted piece across of about 20 m or more along the road, with a narrow median, reads as *Sharp* rather than *U-turn*, and two corners rounded off on a radius of about 20 m, or a U-turn made of three 60-degree corners 20 m or more apart, can read as ordinary turns. Street names already looked up for corners that now form a U-turn may need *Look Up the Rest*, because the turn's position moved.

The turns are silent unless you ask to hear them. Choose *On* under *Voice* on the start screen, or tap the speaker button with the map buttons while you drive (the choice is remembered). Each turn is then said twice, as a warning when it comes into view (*In 200 metres, turn left*) and again once you are within 60 m of it (*Turn left*; a turn first met inside 60 m only gets that), in the phone's own voice, so it works with no signal. On a Transit stretch a turn is said up to four times: *In 1 kilometre, turn left onto Oak Road.* at about a kilometre, *In 500 metres, ...* at about 500 m, *In 200 metres, ...* at about 200 m (the usual warning) and *Turn left onto Oak Road.* within 60 m. The cues change over at 550 m and 225 m, a little past the distance they name, so a turn first met just beyond 500 m or 200 m is not told of twice in the same words. Each is said once and in order, and only the cue for where the turn is now: a turn first met part way in (when Transit begins, or after skipping to a spot) is told of once, for where it is (*In 700 metres, ...*), not with every cue it missed. The voice speaks of the same turn the card is about (on a *Continue* card, the turn it leads to), chosen as above, so it also speaks of a close turn that the heading is only part against, and of the last turn left when the heading agrees with none. Distances from 500 m up to a kilometre are said to the nearest 100 m, and a kilometre or more to the nearest half (*1 kilometre*, *1.5 kilometres*, *2 kilometres*). Off Transit the voice is as before: 200 m, then 60 m. The voice also says each alert once its beep is over (switching it on while an alert is showing says that alert at once, if it may be said yet): the numbers of a row of no-throw houses (*No throw, 12, 14, 16*), a bundle drop's number and how many papers, a special request's number and its note, an apartment block's number or name, how many papers and which side of the road it is on, the side its banner shows (*Apartment block, Lakeside Apartments, 12 papers, starboard.*; no side when it can't be told, and no papers when none are set), and after you tap *Dropped* on a bundle drop, the next bundle in your order (see Bundle drops). A no-throw house on the route is named only once the side thrown where you are takes in its side: on *Both* any house, on *Port* or *Starboard* only a house on that side, and on *Transit* none. Its beep and banner come up as soon as you are near it, as for any stop; only the words wait. So the houses just after a change of side are named as you cross the change, not while the side pill still shows the side before it, and one you are more than 10 m past by then is not named at all. A house whose side can't be told is named anywhere you are but on *Transit*, a house off the route (met by where you are) is named whatever is thrown, and bundle drops, special requests and apartment blocks always are. With the voice on it also says the side thrown each time it changes, in one word: *Port*, *Starboard*, *Both* or *Transit*. It is said a little before the change, so the word is done as the side changes, unless a no-throw row is being said or waits to be said when it is asked: the side word then follows that row and any other rows asked before it (a row that comes up after it follows the side word), so in a street of many no-throw houses it can come some way after the change (in simulated streets thick with them at 12 to 14 m/s, with the side changing every 100 m, it ended up to 88.5 m after the change, 126 m with a slow voice of 0.45 seconds a word, and 102 m with the side changing every 60 m). It is asked for 15 m before the change when the phone doesn't say how fast it is going, otherwise about 2.55 seconds of driving before it, at least 8 m and at most 30 m (the most from about 12 m/s, 43 km/h). That is 1.6 seconds to say the word, with the quiet lead-in it usually has (see below), and 0.95 seconds for the longest alert beep (a no-throw house's), which the word waits out when an alert comes up at the same moment. In simulated drives from 6 to 14 m/s, with a fix a second, the lead-in and a phone that starts each word at once, a word with no beep to wait out was over before the change every time. Each change is said once and the same side is never said twice running; a side word that is out of date by the time it can be said, because the side changed again while what was asked before it (a no-throw row, such as a long one said together with a request's note, or a turn) was still being said, is left out, so after a short stretch you may hear the side you are now on again (after the row or the turn) rather than the one you have just passed (in those streets, 2 of 1,140 side words were left out, on stretches of 100 m, and 11 of 1,140 with a slow voice); a change within 6 m of where the last side was said is the position wobbling across it, and is not said. The side where you are is said once as well, at the first fix on the route after *Start Round*, *Start Over*, *Reset Round*, *Continue Round*, starting part way, a skip or carrying on from here, and after the voice is turned on. When more than one thing is to be said, the voice says first what matters most on the road. A no-throw row (the words that name a no-throw house; when another stop comes up on the same fix, the words for both are said together, as one) and the side word come first, and rank the same: neither cuts the other off, and they are said in the order asked. A side word asked while a row is being said, or waits, follows it, behind the rows asked before it, and a row that comes up while a side word is being said, or waits, follows that; on the same fix the row is asked first, so it is said first, and the side word right after it. In return the side word is late there: with a single no-throw house just before a change, the side word ended up to 23 m after the change, and with a row of 2, 3, 4 or 6 houses 12 m apart starting just before it, up to 29, 36, 42 and 55 m after it at 30 m alerts (22, 29, 35 and 48 m at 40 m). A row or a side word cuts off the words of a special request, an apartment block or a bundle drop being said, which are then said again in full right after it (and after any rows and side words waiting), unless they are out of date by then, and goes before such words that wait. Words a side word cut off are said again even if you have gone by all they name meanwhile, as long as their turn comes within 2 seconds of the cut, since they wait out only one word, so a request's note or a block's side, begun as you came up to it, is finished (with the side changing 0 to 60 m after the stop, at 8 to 14 m/s and 40 m alerts, a long request note and a block's words were each heard whole in all 1,152 simulated drives); after that they are left out when out of date, as any words are. The grace is given once: words cut off part way again by a later side word keep only what is left of the first cut's 2 seconds, and are left out if out of date, so a long note is not begun again at every change. A side word asked on the same fix as an alert's words (it is asked after them) breaks in on them before they begin, while they wait out their beep: that cuts off no word of them and does not use up their grace, which they keep for the first cut part way through (with a request, a block or a bundle drop coming up on the fix of a side word, and the side changing once, or again 30 to 120 m on, each was heard whole in all 542 such simulated drives at 40 m alerts, and in all 514 at 20 m). It still holds them up for the side word, though, and words out of date by their turn are left out, as any words are (see below): with 10 m alerts, which come up only 10 m before the stop, a request or a block coming up on the fix of a side word was not said at all in 16 and 8 of 84 such drives with the side changing once, and in 96 and 48 of 504 with it changing once or again 30 to 120 m on (a bundle drop's words were heard whole in all of them). With the side changing every 50 to 100 m after a long request note, the note was begun at most twice, at most 40 m past the request, and a bundle drop 180 m on was heard whole in all but 3 of 270 simulated drives. The cost: such a note is never heard whole when the side changes every 50 or 60 m (and not in 45, 38 and 14 of 54 drives at every 70, 80 and 100 m), and with two changes 30 to 120 m apart after it, it is not heard whole in 852 of 1,320. Words a no-throw row cut off have no such grace. The side word still waits for a turn being said. Alerts of the same kind never cut each other off (a no-throw row never cuts off another, and the other alerts never cut off one another): one that comes up while another's words are being said, or while they wait for their beep, is said right after them, and several are said in the order they came up. An alert still cuts off a turn being said. Up to four rows, side words and alerts' words can wait at once; past that, the voice lets go first of words already out of date, then of the oldest of the other alerts' words, then of the oldest no-throw row, and of a side word only when nothing else is waiting. A no-throw row breaking in has a cost, as measured: the words of a request, a block or a bundle drop that a row cuts off are left out once you are 10 m past the stop, so an apartment block followed 5 to 45 m later by a no-throw house is never said in full in about a quarter of simulated drives (33 of 135 with *Port* thrown, and as many with *Both*: at 12 to 14 m/s, about 43 to 50 km/h, and at 10 m/s with a slow voice and a gap of 20 to 25 m), and a long request note followed by a house 5 to 50 m on is never said whole in 7, 14, 16 and 20 drives of 20 at 8, 10, 12 and 14 m/s (about 29 to 50 km/h); in return, the house is named before you reach it (the row was begun before the house in every one of these drives). When alerts come up faster than they can be said, words whose turn comes only once you are 10 m or more past every stop they name are left out, so the voice seldom names a stop well behind you, and the next words are then said without a pause for those left out. That is judged at the last fix, and the car goes on between fixes, so at 36 to 50 km/h words have still begun 9 to 22 m past every stop they name. Words the phone did not start are tried once more, unless they are out of date by then, except that a no-throw row is not tried again once you are level with the last house it names at your last GPS fix: named over the next houses it could be taken for one of them, so the banner carries it alone (a house met by where you are is never level, so it keeps its second try). All other alerts keep their second try. Words a side word cut off, given their 2 seconds of grace, can begin later: up to 58 m past the stop with one change of side after it, and up to 94 m with a second change 120 m on, and in simulated streets with the side changing every 100 m, 85 of 1,571 starts of alerts' words were more than 30 m past all the stops they name (none more than 100 m), at worst 93 m (with a slow voice, 81 of 1,468, 2 of them more than 100 m, at worst 116 m; with the side changing every 60 m, at worst 85 m). It also says when you leave the route and when you are back (see Off the route above), and when the next bundle is left behind you (see Bundle drops). Like the alert tones, it may be muted by the phone's silent switch. The button isn't shown on a browser that can't speak.

The start of a spoken word can go missing over a car's Bluetooth, because the link goes idle between sounds and takes a moment to wake, and a car's silence gate or the phone's own speech can lose it the same way. So with *Keep car audio awake* on, which it is once the voice is, a sound too quiet to hear plays all the time to keep the link open: low-pitched noise at about -44 dB at its peak, stronger than it used to be, which sits under the road noise. Turn that off on the start screen if you hear a low hum or it matters for a car's battery. With it on or off, after about 2 seconds with nothing said, words get a very quiet spoken lead-in just before them (*uh*, at volume 0.02), so that whatever is lost at the start is that and not the first word. This matters most for *No throw*: clipped, *No throw, 17* says the opposite of what it means. Words right after other words have none. Words therefore start a little later after a silence. With no keep-alive running (turned off, or its audio stopped by a call or Siri) the voice is led in by a short tick when sound went out in the last 2 seconds, or else by a soft 0.3 second opener, with about 0.7 seconds' wait before the words. A beep that has just started opens the link by itself, so neither is added to it. During a round, with the voice and *Keep car audio awake* on, each GPS fix tries to bring back audio stopped by a call or Siri.

*Test Voice*, under *Keep car audio awake* on the start screen and there only with the voice on, is for trying this over the car's speakers before you set off. From the tap it says the three throw side words, *Port*, *Starboard* and *Both*, one at a time, a word every 4 seconds, which leaves about 3 seconds of silence between them, in just the way the round says them, so each is a single word after a silence, as on the road. It starts the keep-alive if that is on. The words still to come stop if the round starts, the voice goes off or the Round tab is left, and a second tap starts it again from the first word.

None of this has been tried on a real iPhone or in a car, so whether it cures the missing words is not known yet: it has only been run against a stand-in for the phone's speech, and *Test Voice* is the way to find out. **Optimise Route** (needs internet): under *Duplicate* in Routes, for a route with a line drawn. It works out a shorter way to drive the route in use, with every street you throw at and every stop still on it, and nothing changes until you tap *Accept*. *Start* gets the map of the roads round the route and reads your route against it, which can take a minute or two when the map service is busy. Offline, with the service too busy, or if something breaks on the way, it says so plainly and offers *Try Again*.

Then it asks what it may do. It says where the new route starts and ends: the section named Start if there is one, in capitals or not, otherwise the route's first point. *Keep my bundle order*, there when the route has two or more bundle drops, is on to begin with, so the route is worked out for your list of bundle drops as it stands, because the round only alerts the next bundle in that list. A list you haven't set yourself is the order today's route meets the bundles in, and with a Start part way round it is read from Start and comes round, as the new route will drive it. Switched off, you get the shortest route it can find, and your list then follows the new drive. *Allow throwing starboard or both* lists the streets, with the main roads first and switched off, so they are thrown from the port side only and each side takes a pass of its own, and everything else switched on. A long list of other streets is folded into one line saying how many are on, with *Show All* to list them; a street that is off is always listed. A part you throw at, of 16 m or more, that isn't on any road on the map is said here before the run, as *Accept* will stay off. *Find My Route* takes about 20 seconds, with a bar to watch. *Cancel* stops it and takes you back to these choices, and *Close* leaves altogether, either way with your route as it was.

The results set today and the new route side by side: the total, *Port*, *Starboard*, *Both*, *Transit*, thrown twice, U-turns and side changes. Today's total is measured along the roads, so it can differ from the length in the Routes list, and from today's sides and transit, which are measured along your line as drawn; a note under the figures says by about how much. If your route ends away from its start, today's total and transit both include the way back, as the new route's do. With *Keep my bundle order* on, a new route that comes out longer than today says so at the top, with the notes on why just under it. Under the figures is a map of the new route in the same colours as the round, filling its box: one finger on it scrolls the screen, and two zoom and move it. Then come any roads you don't drive today, any place it turns back with no dead end to make it, any sections that can't be placed, the new bundle order if it has changed, and what is worth checking: the stretches the new route stops throwing at first, then the one-sided ones, each longest first, with the longest few of each showing until *Show All*. A stretch under 16 m that you throw at today can be left unthrown without keeping *Accept* off (counted whole across side streets and roundabouts, as below), and on a long round these can come to a few hundred metres, most often at the end of a court where the old line looped round and the new one turns on a point; a line under the list says about how much in all. So look these over, and the stretches thrown on one side only on a street otherwise thrown on both, which may simply have no houses.

*Accept* stays off and says why if the new route would leave out a stretch of 16 m or more that you throw at today (counted whole where side streets, or the roads into a roundabout, cut it into short pieces), if a part of 16 m or more that you throw at isn't on any road on the map or a street can't be reached, if a stop would come off the route, if the finished route fails the app's own check of it (run afresh from its points, for a closed road, a road off the map, a one-way, a banned turn or an end that isn't its start; it lays the route on the map's roads a metre at a time, and 2 m of it off the roads, or 2 m against a one-way, is enough), if your bundle list doesn't fit the new route with *Keep my bundle order* on, or if the route has changed since the run started. *Change Choices* goes back for another run with no need to fetch the map again, and *Cancel* closes it with nothing changed.

*Accept* keeps your old route in the Routes list, named like *Monday Round (before optimise)*, with its sections, street names, bundle order and papers to load, and the new route stays in use under its own name, with the same stops and papers to load. To go back, tap the old route in the Routes list. Named sections are placed again in their order on the new route, starting with Start at its very beginning. Any that can't be placed are listed: they stay on the old route only, so the new one has no banner where they start or end and doesn't offer them as a place to start a round. Street names for the new turns come from the map it already has, so there is nothing to look up afterwards. A round in progress starts over, and it warns you above *Accept* before you tap it. If the phone won't keep both routes, nothing is changed and it says why: no room left, or a phone that saves nothing at all, such as a private tab.

It has run from *Start* to *Accept* in a computer's browser against the live map service, on one real route, but not yet on an iPhone or on the road. The way it now does its work in the background, built into the app's own script, has not yet run in any browser. Everything else has been checked on a computer, against that route and a saved copy of its map. Treat a new route as something to look over and drive once with care, and keep the old one until you have. It only knows what your route throws today, so it can't find a house you don't deliver to, and the map may not know a road is closed or a turn is awkward. The figures move a little from one run to the next, as the search is random.

## Notes
- The map opens on Australia until it finds you or your route, and place searches lean toward Australia when you have no route yet.
- Address lookup, place search and Route by Address use OpenStreetMap's free Nominatim service, and Follow roads, Route by Address and street names (below) use a free routing service. They need internet. House numbers are not always in OpenStreetMap: when only the street is found, the position is approximate and the app says so. Everything else works offline.
- **Follow roads** asks the free OSRM routing server that FOSSGIS runs at `routing.openstreetmap.de` (the one behind the directions on openstreetmap.org, car profile) for the way from your last point to each tap. Each lookup sends just the two ends of that stretch (and, when you use *U-turn along the road*, one direction to leave the first by with a range of 60 degrees, and that its road is to be found within 30 m of that end), plus your IP address and the app's web address. *U-turn here* sends nothing. It is one request per tap, one at a time and never more than one a second, as FOSSGIS's [terms of use](https://www.fossgis.de/arbeitsgruppen/osm-server/nutzungsbedingungen/) require; the map credits OpenStreetMap and links to *Fix the map*, which they also ask for (on the round the credit is a small note above the bar and *Fix the Map* is in its menu). Those terms say the server is best effort with no promise it keeps running or stays the same, and that heavy or high-traffic use is not allowed, so it suits one person tracing their own rounds. If the app is ever shared widely, run your own OSRM and build with `VITE_ROUTING_URL` set to its address (for example `https://osrm.example.com/route/v1/driving`).
- **Route by Address** sends the two addresses you type to Nominatim, with your IP address and the app's web address, one search at a time and never more than one a second, as its [usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/) requires (there is no search-as-you-type). The road itself comes from the same FOSSGIS server as Follow roads, which is sent only the coordinates of the stretch, so the same terms and the same advice about `VITE_ROUTING_URL` apply. A road is looked for up to 250 m from each address, since a house can sit well back from its street.
- **Street names** come from the same FOSSGIS OSRM server as Follow roads, with the same terms: at most one request for each turn, one at a time, started by you and never repeated for a turn that has an answer. Each request sends just the coordinates of a point on the road just after the turn and which way it runs, plus your IP address and the app's web address. A `VITE_ROUTING_URL` that points elsewhere must contain `/route/v1/` (as the example does), because street names use the same server's `/nearest/v1/`; without it they can't be looked up. FOSSGIS's terms also ask that an operator's email address be easy to find on a website that uses the server, so the app shows a contact line, `skupaaustralia@gmail.com`, at the bottom of *Routes*, under *Street names* in Setup and at the foot of *Route by Address*. If you build your own copy, set `VITE_CONTACT_EMAIL` to your own address. Names can be wrong where a tunnel or bridge lies under or over the road, or a nearer street is within 25 m of the point (with no road within 25 m the turn gets no name), and *Clear Street Names* and a new lookup is the way to redo them.
- **Optimise Route** asks a free public Overpass server for OpenStreetMap's roads and turn rules in a box about 1 km beyond the route. The request sends just that box, plus your IP address and the app's web address, with no street address, name or email. These servers are often busy, so five of them are tried in turn, for three rounds with a wait between them, and it gives up after three minutes. They are `lz4.overpass-api.de`, `z.overpass-api.de`, `maps.mail.ru` (run by Mail.ru, in Russia), `overpass-api.de` and `overpass.openstreetmap.fr`, in that order, and any of them may see that request. `VITE_OVERPASS_URL`, set when building, uses one server of your own instead. All the working out is done on the phone, in the background, so the screen stays free to take *Cancel*.
- Routes are saved automatically on the phone, after every change, and are still there next time you open the app. There is no Save button. A red bar appears if the phone refuses to store anything (for example in a private tab).
- They live only on that phone. On an iPhone, Safari and an app added to the Home Screen keep separate copies, so draw your routes in whichever one you will use (the Home Screen app is best). Deleting the app or clearing Safari's website data removes them.
- **Back up and restore** (in Routes): *Back Up This Route* or *Back Up All Routes* shows a block of text. *Copy* it into Notes, or *Save File* to keep it as a file (on a phone this opens the share sheet, with Save to Files and AirDrop), to keep a safe copy. *Restore from Backup* takes the pasted text, or a saved file with *Open File*, and adds its routes to your list. A route with the same name as one you already have can replace it or be kept beside it (you are asked), and a replaced route's progress starts over. A backup carries the route's named sections, street names, order of bundle drops (once you have set one) and list of papers to load (once it has one of its own) too, but not its progress (the stops done, the bundles dropped and missed, and what a change of side while driving did to the houses at the head of a court). A route replaced from a backup that has no order of bundle drops (one made before you set it, or before the app kept one) keeps its own, and so it is with the list of papers to load: a route replaced takes the backup's list, or keeps its own if the backup has none. A list damaged in storage or in a backup keeps the lines that can be read, with 25 and 50 in place of a damaged number of papers in a bundle, and if nothing can be read the route is treated as having no list. Apartment blocks are kept with their papers, as bundle drops are (a whole number, 1 or more). It is also how you move routes to another phone or from a computer. Curly quotes in a name or a note (an iPhone makes them from plain ones) are kept exactly as typed, and a backup still reads after Notes or Messages has curled the quotes of the backup itself. A backup made by an earlier version still reads, except one with a curly double quote typed in it that has also had its own quotes curled.
- Safari has no vibration API, so the no-throw warning is a flashing red banner plus a tone (the phone's silent switch mutes it). Vibration is used where supported.
- GPS only updates while the app is open and the screen is on. Mount the phone and keep it unlocked.
- Map tiles need signal; offline you still see the route, stops and your position on a plain background.

## Install on iPhone
Geolocation needs HTTPS, so host the built files once (GitHub Pages via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`), open the URL in Safari, then Share > Add to Home Screen. Open it once online so it caches; after that it works offline.
