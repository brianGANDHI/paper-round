# Paper Round

Offline PWA for a fixed newspaper round. React + Vite, data in localStorage, no server. It follows the iPhone's light or dark appearance and is styled like a native iOS app.

## Use
**Routes** keeps every round you run, for example a Monday round and a Saturday round. Tap a route to use it, *New Route* to add one (it takes you straight to Setup to draw it), and *Rename*, *Duplicate* or *Delete* to manage the one in use. Each route keeps its own sections, named sections, street names, stops and progress. The route in use is named at the top of Setup and on the Round start screen; tap its name to switch.

**Plan on a computer, run on your phone.** The app works in a computer's browser too, at the same web address: the map is bigger and a mouse makes drawing and placing stops quicker. Draw the route there, then in Routes tap *Back Up This Route* and either *Copy* it or *Save File*. On your phone open Routes, tap *Restore from Backup*, and *Paste* (an iPhone and a Mac signed in to the same Apple ID share their clipboard, so the copied text is simply there) or *Open File* (AirDrop it, or keep it in iCloud Drive). Make changes on the computer later and send it again: the phone asks whether to replace the route of the same name. Restore it in the app you use for your round. On an iPhone, Safari and the Home Screen app keep separate routes, and the Home Screen app is the one to use. The computer keeps its own copy of what you drew, but it lives in that browser, so *Save File* is the safe copy.

**Setup** edits the route in use. It has four steps, shown as a segmented control at the top of the sheet (it gives way to the tool's own controls while you draw, paint a stretch or add a section): Route, Throw Side, Stops and Sections. Once the route is drawn, the first three each end with a *Next* button.

1. **Route**: no driving needed. Search for a street or town, tap *Draw Route*, then tap along your route in the order you drive it. The *Both / Left / Right / Transit* picker on the drawing panel says what the stretch you are drawing is for (the sides it is thrown on, or *Transit*, where you only drive): change it and keep tapping, and a new section starts there, so you can lay out the whole route with its sides in one go. To fix a mistake, drag a point to move it, tap a point to select it and *Delete Point*, tap a **+** between two points to add one there, or *Undo* the last point. Zoom in for the bends. *Done* finishes and fits the route on screen. (*Record by Driving Instead* still works if you prefer to trace it with GPS.)

   **Follow roads** (optional, needs internet): switch it on beside *Undo* and *Done* while drawing, and each tap follows the streets from your last point to the tap instead of joining them with a straight line, so a curvy street takes one tap instead of many. The side you pick works exactly as before. It routes for a car, so one-way streets are respected: a tap on the far side of a one-way pair can send the route round the block, so tap the stretch you actually drive. While a road is being looked up the tap shows as a ring and *Undo* turns into *Cancel*; afterwards, *Undo* takes back a whole stretch of road at once. If there is no signal, the service is slow (over 5 seconds) or down, or there is no road near the tap, that stretch is drawn as a straight line and a note says why. Dragging and adding points work on the result as usual, and the switch is remembered. With it off, tapping is unchanged and works fully offline.

   **U-turn** (with Follow roads on): once the route has a direction (two points that are not in the same place), a *U-turn* button appears under *Undo*, *Follow roads* and *Done* while you draw, with the note *For the next tap only: the road turns round and heads back the way you came.* Switch it on, then tap the map: the road for that tap leaves your last point heading back the way the route arrived, so it turns round there, where the router would otherwise take the nearest legal way, which is often a loop round the block. It switches itself off after that tap, and also if you undo, move or delete the last point, clear the route, press *Done* or turn Follow roads off. A tap you cancel, or one on the last point, leaves it on. The button can't be pressed while a road is being looked up, it is off each time you start drawing and it is not remembered. It works on two-way roads. The road back has to start within 30 m of your last point (enough for the other carriageway of a dual carriageway, to which your last point is joined by a short piece), while the tap itself can be up to 50 m from a road as usual. On a one-way street, where no road within 30 m of your last point runs back that way, where the tap is more than 50 m from any road, or where no road joins the two, that stretch is drawn as a straight line and a note says *No road route found there for a U-turn (it may be a one-way street, or a point too far from a road), so that stretch is a straight line.* The note can't say which of these it was, because the routing service gives the same answer for all of them. *Undo* takes back the whole stretch as usual, and the round finds a U-turn drawn this way as one turn (see U-turns below). It is still one request for each tap, one at a time and never more than one a second.

   **Route by Address** (needs internet): choose *Transit* in the picker and a *Route by Address* button appears under it, for the stretches between the places you deliver. Type where the transit starts (*From*) and where it ends (*To*), then tap *Find Fastest Route*. The app looks both addresses up, asks for the fastest road for a car between them, adds it to the end of your route as a Transit section and zooms to it. A note says which places were matched, how far it is and roughly how long it takes, so a wrong match is easy to spot, and *Undo* takes the whole stretch off in one go. Once the route has points you can choose *End of Route* instead of typing a start, and the road runs from where your route ends now. A typed start that is more than about 100 m from the end of your route is driven to first, so the route stays one connected drive. If an address isn't found, add the suburb or postcode. When there is no signal, a service is down or slow, no road joins the two places, or they are the same place, nothing is added and a message says why (there is no straight-line fallback here). What you typed is kept if you close the box and open it again. The road is the fastest by the routing service's car profile, which ignores traffic and respects one-way streets, so glance at it before you rely on it.

   **Street names** (optional, needs internet): once the route is ready, *Look Up Street Names* under *Route ready* finds the name of the street each turn leads into, so the round can show the street under the turn's distance and say *Turn left onto Oak Road*. It asks the same FOSSGIS routing server as Follow roads, at most one request for each turn (a corner turned the same way twice is asked once, and a turn with no room before the next one is not asked), one at a time and never more than one a second, and keeps the answers with the route, so the round itself needs no internet. About 150 turns take around four minutes, so do it at home with the screen on and stay on this screen: *Stop* ends it, going to another step or tab (or changing the route) stops it too, and what was found is kept. If the signal drops, the lookup stops after three failures in a row (or at once if the service says it is busy) and a note says why; a run that finishes with a failure or two says how many could not be looked up. What was found is kept, the panel counts the turns still without a name, and once some turns have one the button reads *Look Up the Rest* and asks for just those. Each turn is asked about the road it leaves by, a little past the corner, and only roads running that way count, so it doesn't name the cross street. A road with no name, or no road within 25 m of the point asked about, is remembered as having none (the panel counts both as roads with no name), and the turn is shown and said as it always was. A turn onto the same street as the turn before is shown plain. *Clear Street Names* forgets them, and so do *Clear Route* and *Record Again*. Names are kept with a backup, and a turn that moves is asked about again the next time you look up. Once some names have been looked up, the start screen reminds you of any turns still missing.

2. **Throw Side**: each section of the route has a *Both / Left / Right / Transit* toggle, and the route on the map is coloured to match (blue both, green left, orange right, and a solid rose-pink line for Transit, wider than the others and with larger direction arrows, so the road between delivery areas is easy to follow; the dot beside *Transit* in Setup is the same pink as the line, and the *Transit* pill on the round screen is a deeper shade of it, so the white word on it reads at a glance). *Transit* is for the stretches between the places you deliver, where nothing is thrown at all: the round screen shows *Transit* instead of a side and gives turn guidance all the way along it (see Turn directions on Transit), no-throw houses on it stay quiet, and the Stops list says so. A bundle drop or a special request on a transit stretch still alerts.

   **A no-throw house only alerts on a side you throw.** Which side a house is on is as the route passes it, the same side the Stops list and the alert show, a side picked by hand included. Where *Both* is thrown it alerts. Where only *Left* is thrown it alerts only for a house on the left of the way you are driving, and likewise for *Right*. On *Transit* it never alerts. A house whose side can't be told still alerts, to be safe, and so does a house more than 40 m from the route, which no stretch or side of the road makes quiet: that covers every house off the route (see Stops) as well as those 40 to 60 m out, whether the stretch beside them is transit or a side you don't throw. (40 m is where, at the shortest alert distance of 10 m, a house starts to be met by where you are rather than along the route, and a stretch of the route says nothing of such a house, so none that could alert by position is ever silenced, at any alert distance.) A house within 40 m of the route is judged by the stretch and the side as above. On a street you drive more than once each pass is judged on its own: the same house is on the right going out and the left coming back, so with *Left* thrown both ways it stays quiet going out and alerts coming back. A bundle drop or a special request is never made quiet. Houses that won't alert are left out of every count (the stops left on the round, the stops along a named section, the rows of houses in an alert), and the round map draws them small and pale. In Setup, after any change of side, whether a whole section's, a painted stretch's or a house's own side picked by hand (the *Both / Left / Right / Transit* toggle, the box on the map, *Paint a Stretch* or *Set side* in the Stops list), the notice says what that did to the no-throw houses, counting each house on its own: *2 no-throw houses will now stay quiet.*, *1 no-throw house will now alert.*, or both together (*2 no-throw houses will now stay quiet; 1 no-throw house will now alert.*), since painting a stretch from Left to Right quiets the houses on its left and wakes the ones on its right. For a house on a street driven twice it says *... will now stay quiet on one pass* or *... will now alert on one more pass* (*some passes* when it is more than one). When any house goes quiet the notice ends with *A no-throw house only alerts on a side you throw.* After a painted stretch it begins *Painted 120 m for right side.* Nothing is said when no house changes. (One case is not reported: a house that alerts once before and once after, but on the other pass.) The note under the Throw Side list says the same in short, and the total of houses that won't alert is the note under the Stops list.

   **To change a section's side, tap its line on the map** (here, or on the Route tab once the route is drawn). A small box opens right where you tapped, with the section's name and length and *Both / Left / Right / Transit*: tap one and that whole section takes it, and the box closes. The box goes when you tap the map, drag or zoom it, switch tab or tap its cross, and it stays clear of the sheet. The section is picked in the list too, and each card there has the same toggle if you would rather work from the list. To change just part of the road, tap *Change just part of it* in the box (or *Paint a Stretch* in the list): choose a side (it starts on one the section doesn't already have), then tap the route where the stretch starts and where it ends. The stretch becomes a section of its own, joining a neighbour that is already on that side.

   **To delete a section**, tap *Delete* in that box, or *Delete Section* on its card in the list (the card shows it once the section is picked, and not while you are painting). You are asked first, and told how much of the route goes and how many stops along it go with it. A section at the start or the end of the route just comes off, so the route starts or ends where the next or previous one does. One in the middle leaves a gap, which is closed with a straight line of transit between its two ends, since a stretch nobody has drawn can't have a side: redraw it with *Edit Route* or *Route by Address* if you want the real road. A stop goes with the section it was along unless another part of the route is still within reach of it, for example the other pass of a street you drive twice. A stop that is off the route (see Stops) was never along a section, so it stays. The only section can't be deleted (use *Clear Route*), and there is no undo, so the question is there to be read. *Merge* joins a section back to the one before. Left and right are as you face the way you drive, and the arrows on the map show that direction.

   **Driving a street more than once** (for example down it and back, throwing to a different side each time): tap *Repeat* on the section, then *Drive it back* (the same road the other way) or *Drive it again* (the same way, with a link back to its start). It is added to the end of your route as a new section with a side of its own, starting with the label that covers the other side of the street, and you can change it. Passes over the same road are drawn side by side on the map with their own arrows. The app follows the pass you are on, using the direction you are heading, so the side pill changes as you turn round, and a stop on that street warns you on every pass. To change the side partway along one pass, select its section first and then use *Paint a Stretch*.
3. **Stops**: choose *No-Throw*, *Bundle Drop* or *Special Request*. Type or paste addresses, one per line (with the town or postcode), and tap *Look Up Addresses*. Each is matched on the map near your route and ticked when it is a close, exact match; check any that are flagged, then *Add*. Every stop has a *name* (a business name, for a bundle drop) and an *address*, kept separately and both optional. The address is the line you looked up. Each address that is found has a box for the name and, for a bundle drop, one for the number of *papers* it gets; a stop placed on the map has boxes for the name, the address and the papers. A stop with only an address shows that as its name. Each stop in the list shows its name with its address under it and, for a bundle drop, how many papers (or *no amount set*, in orange, when it has none), and the pencil beside it changes the name, the address and the papers whenever you like, for a no-throw house as well as a bundle drop. Stops added before addresses were kept apart may have the address in the name: the pencil offers a name that looks like an address (a house number, then words) as the address instead, and nothing changes until you save. Swipe or tap the grabber at the top of the sheet to see more of the list. Each stop in the list shows which side of the road it is on as you drive the route (*Left*, or *Left, then Right* on a street you drive both ways), with a button to change it: pick *Left* or *Right* by hand, or go back to *Work It Out From the Map*. The side is worked out from where the stop sits beside your route, so a stop placed right on the route line (for example with *Use My Current Location*) may have none. A no-throw house like that takes the side your route throws on there, and when that can't say either (you throw both sides) the list shows an orange *Set side* button and the app tells you when you add it. A side you pick is as you drive past it the first time, and swaps on the way back along a street you drive both ways. A no-throw house that won't alert says why in the line under its name: when every pass of it is quiet the reason takes the place of the side's words (*in transit, so no alert*, *quiet here: you throw the other side*, or *quiet here: in transit or you throw the other side*), and the side itself shows on the button at the right of the row (the first pass's side, on a street driven twice). On a street driven twice where only some passes are quiet, the sides come first and the reason after them, for example *Left, then Right, quiet on pass 2: you throw the other side* or, with the passes listed, *quiet on passes 1 and 3: in transit*. A house more than 40 m from the route never shows a reason, as it always alerts (see Throw Side). Under the list a note counts them: *2 no-throw houses won't alert: they are on transit stretches, or on the side you don't throw.*

   **Special requests** are for a place that needs something done by hand, such as a house with no driveway and only a gate. A special request has an address and a short note saying what to do, up to 60 characters, such as *Blue gate, deliver by hand*. It has no name. Choose *Special Request*, then add it like any other stop: look up its address and type the note in the box beside it, or place it on the map after filling in the *Address* and *Note* boxes. The pencil beside it changes the address and the note. A request with no note says *no note set* in orange in the list, because its alert then can't say what to do.

   **A stop doesn't have to be along your route.** Place or look up a house on a street you didn't draw, or one set well back from the street you did. A stop more than about 60 m from the route is marked *off the route* in the Stops list, with how far, in place of a side, and the app says so when you add it. Its alert comes up by where you are, not by how far along the route you have got: whenever you get within the alert distance of the stop, on whatever road and whichever way you are going. Such an alert needs a GPS fix accurate to 40 m or better, so a poor fix can't set off alerts for houses you are nowhere near, and the stop counts as done once you have been that close and are away again. A stop that is only a little off the route still alerts as you drive along it, as before. A no-throw house more than 40 m from the route, which takes in every one off the route (more than about 60 m), is never made quiet by the stretch beside it, whether that is transit or a side you don't throw.

4. **Sections**: give parts of the route a name, such as a street or an estate, so a round can start from any of them. These are separate from the throw-side sections above: they can start anywhere and say nothing about the side thrown. Tap *Add a Section*, tap the route where the section starts, and type its name; keep tapping to add more, and tap *Done Adding* when you have finished. On a road the route goes along more than once, the app asks which time through you mean (unless the passes are separate throw-side sections, where the line you tap says). A section runs from its start to where the next one starts, or to the end of the route, and the part before the first is *Start of route* (tap *Name It* to give that a name too; if the first section starts within 25 m of the start it takes the start, and there is nothing to name). A section can't start within 25 m of another or of the end of the route, nor at the turn of a road that is driven out and back, a name is up to 40 characters, and a route keeps up to 200 sections. The list shows where each section starts and ends along the route (for example *320 m to 1.4 km*) and how many stops are along it (a no-throw house that never alerts, because it is on a Transit stretch or on a side you don't throw, isn't counted); tap one to see it on the map, *Rename* it, or delete it with the bin (the route is not touched: the section before just carries on). A section is kept by where its start is on the map, not by its place in the route, so it stays put when you edit the route, and is set aside, with a note, if its start is more than 100 m from the route now or within 25 m of another section's start. *Clear Route* and *Record Again* remove the named sections. Sections are kept with a backup.

**Round**: tap *Start Round* (unlocks sound, keeps the screen awake). If the last round was finished, the button says *Start New Round* and starts from scratch automatically. If it was only part done you can *Continue Round* or *Start Over*. You only get alerts for bundle drops, no-throw houses and special requests, once for each time your route goes past one. *Alert distance* sets how near a stop you have to be for its alert to come up, for all three kinds of stop: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60 metres, with 40 to begin with. Choose it on the start screen, or change it while you drive with *Alert Distance* in the menu (the three dots at the right of the bar along the bottom, where the current distance is shown), and the choice is remembered. The bar says *Alerts at 40 m* too, but only as a plain note with nothing to tap, and only when it has nothing else to say (see The bottom of the round). An alert is a banner across the top of the map, under your side and the next turn, so the map and the buttons stay in view, and touches go through the banner to the map, except for a bundle drop's *Dropped* button (see Bundle drops). It is blue for a bundle drop, red for a no-throw house (it flashes a few times to catch your eye, then stays a steady red) and purple for a special request, and it shows how far away the stop is and the **house number** on a white plate, big enough to read at a glance, since that is what you are looking for on the street. Beside the plate are the stop's name with its address under it (a stop with only an address shows that as its name). A bundle drop's papers are not beside the name but in a pale panel beside its *Dropped* button (see Bundle drops), which leaves the name and address the room. The distance changes as you approach and is hidden from VoiceOver, so approaching does not read the alert out again at every step; the alert's own label still says what it is for. A special request shows its note in place of a name, on up to three lines in a banner taller than an ordinary alert's (about 100 px against 68), and with no house number for the plate its address goes under the note. A bundle drop's banner is the tallest of all, about 150 to 190 px, because it holds the stop's details and the big *Dropped* button under them (see Bundle drops). The number is read from the start of the stop's address, for example *12* from *12 Oak Road*, *12A* from *Unit 3, 12A Oak Road* and *7* from *Lot 7 Smith Road*, and is left off the name and address beside the plate so it isn't said twice. A stop with no address uses its name only when that is a number (*No. 14*, *#14* or *14*) or reads like an address (*14 Elm Street*), so a shop called *24 Hour Pharmacy* has no number, and a stop with none shows no plate. The pencil beside a stop in the Stops list is where to add or fix its address. At the edge of the banner is which side of the road the stop is on, with an arrow and the word *Left* or *Right*: the left or right of the way you are driving, so on a street you drive both ways the same house is on the right one way and the left the other. The side is the one shown for that stop in the Stops list (see above); for a stop off the route, which has no stretch of the route beside it, it is worked out as you come up to the stop, from the way you are heading. When it still can't be told, the banner shows a plain icon in place of the arrow. A no-throw house and a special request have nothing to tap. The banner goes by itself once you are away from the stop, and driving on past a stop counts it as done (for a bundle drop driven past without tapping *Dropped* that means missed, see Bundle drops), which is what the number of stops left in the bar counts down. If you back away from a stop before you reach it, its alert goes, and comes back when you approach again. A stop off the route works differently (see Stops above): it alerts when you get within the alert distance of it, counts as done once you have been that close and left, and stays in the count if you never go near it. No-throw houses in a row (each no more than 60 m along the route from the one before it, on the same side) come up as one alert with a plate for every house in order, on one line or on two when there are more than five (and past nine, the first nine and how many more), so all the numbers are in view before you reach them. A row reaches no further than 250 m ahead and doesn't carry on round a U-turn to the houses on the way back; as you pass each house it drops off the row, and the banner moves on to the next. A house with no number has the start of its name on its plate. On the map those houses stand out as well: each has its number beside it, on the side away from the road, and the nearest has a ring round it. When other alerts overlap, the nearest no-throw house still ahead shows first (until you are 10 m past it: a house you have driven past no longer hides a bundle drop that is still in range, see Bundle drops), with a small +N beside the title for how many more are waiting. Each kind of stop has a sound of its own as well: one beep for a bundle drop, three low ones for a no-throw house and two rising notes for a special request. The side you are throwing and the next turn sit side by side along the top, above the alert, with the heading badge (see Heading badge) at their right, so they stay where they are when an alert comes and goes (on a phone 350 px wide or narrower the turn card moves to a line of its own under the side when the two do not fit side by side). The part of the route you have driven is drawn faded and underneath, so what is left stands out (see What the round map draws). The bottom of the screen is one slim bar and a few buttons, and the rest is in a menu (see The bottom of the round): the buttons down the right of the map follow your location, point the map the way you are heading (the compass), turn the voice on and off and zoom, and *Reset Round*, which starts the round again, is in the menu.

**Bundle drops** need you to say they are done, so a bundle drop's alert has one thing to tap: a big white *Dropped* button at least 56 px high, under the stop's details (the venue name, its address of up to two lines and the side of the road), which are in bigger type than on other alerts. The papers are not among the details but in a panel of their own beside the button, as tall as it and in big type, since the count is what you check before the tap (it is pale, not white, so it isn't taken for a second button; a bundle with no amount set has no panel and the button takes the whole width). That leaves the name and the address the full width of the banner but for the house number's plate. It is the only part of an alert that takes a touch; the rest of the banner still lets touches through to the map. Like any alert, it comes up when you are within the alert distance and goes once you are past the stop or have backed away from it. Tapping *Dropped* marks the bundle dropped: the alert goes, a short quiet tick of two rising notes plays (higher and much quieter than any alert, so it isn't taken for one), and the bar says *Dropped* with *Undo* for 12 seconds. A second tap within a moment of the first is ignored, so a double tap drops one bundle and not the next one at the same spot. *Undo* takes the drop back, and the alert comes up again, with its beep, if you are still at the stop (if you have left it by then, the bundle is *Later*, *Behind you*, in the list). With the voice on, it then says the next bundle once the tick is over (*Next bundle, Co-op, 300 metres*; kilometres a long way off, and no distance when it is right there). A bundle drop's alert that comes up together with a no-throw house waits behind it while that house is still ahead of you or less than 10 m behind, since a bundle never hides a no-throw warning. A house you have driven past (more than 10 m) no longer hides it, so if the bundle is still in range its alert and its button come up as soon as the house is behind you. A bundle at the very spot of a no-throw house, or only a few metres from it, is the exception: the house outranks it for as long as the house is live, and the two are gone by almost together, so the bundle's alert may show for only part of its zone, and not at all when they are at the same spot. A bundle that never gets to show is recorded as missed once you have driven past, and *Mark dropped* in the Bundles list (see below) is the way to say it was dropped.

Driving on past a bundle drop in its zone without tapping *Dropped* ends the alert but records the bundle as **missed**, shown in amber, and the bar says *Missed bundle: Co-op* for a few seconds (*Missed 2 bundles: A and B*, or *A, B and 2 more* when there are more; a name is cut to 26 characters). A missed bundle can be marked dropped later from the list. A miss is recorded only while you are following the route. Bundles that become done any other way are **skipped**, neither dropped nor missed: skipping to a spot or section, starting from one, carrying on from here, the catch-up after a start, and leaving the route and rejoining it further on. A bundle that never alerted (the phone had no fix there) is not missed either: it stays *Later* and *Behind you*, with *Mark dropped* in the list.

**A bundle on a street the route goes along twice is one delivery**, however many times the route passes it. Its papers are counted once in the list's figures, and the stop is counted once in the list's header (*1 of 3 bundles dropped* counts stops, not passes) and in the bar's *2 missed*. Dropping it on one pass, with *Dropped* or *Mark dropped* in the list, settles every other pass: one you have not reached never alerts and is never missed, and one that was missed is not missed any more. In the list those passes read *Dropped on pass 1*, in grey, with no button, and the pass dropped has *Undo*, which puts every pass back exactly as it was: still to come, skipped or missed. A stop counts as missed (in the bar's *2 missed* and the list's *Missed*) only when no pass of it is left to come and none was dropped, and counts once even if both passes were missed. So driving past the first pass untapped, with a second to come, still raises *Missed bundle: Co-op* and turns that row amber, but the stop is not counted as missed until the second pass is gone by too; dropping it on the second pass clears it. Skipping to a spot keeps this: if the pass dropped is before the spot, the later pass stays done and does not alert again, and if it is after the spot, the drop and the passes it settled come up again.

The bar shows the next bundle drop when the route has any (and nothing more pressing is in the middle of the bar: see The bottom of the round). The venue has a row of its own, the full width of the line: its name, else its address, else *Bundle drop*. Above it, on one row, are *Next bundle*, how far away it is (*340 m*, *Now* when it is here), an amber *2 missed* when any have been missed, and the papers (*12 papers*). The lead reads just *Next* when the amber pill or the small warning button (see The bottom of the round) takes room beside it, and on a narrow screen whatever does not fit is left off whole, the papers first, and never squeezes the venue's name. A bundle more than 10 m behind you is no longer the next. When none is left ahead it reads *Bundles* over *All bundles dropped* or *No more bundles ahead*, and a route with no bundle drops shows nothing there. Tap the line, or choose *Bundles* in the menu, to open the list. The **Bundles** list is a sheet that comes up over the dimmed map. At the top are *Papers dropped*, *Papers to drop* and *Missed* (amber when above 0), and under the title how many bundles of the total are dropped. Below are all the route's bundle drops in route order (a street driven twice has a row for each pass, marked *Pass 2 of 2*), each with its status (*Dropped* in green, *Missed* in amber, *Next* in blue, *Later* and *Skipped* in grey, and *Dropped on pass 1* in grey for a pass that the drop of another pass of its stop covers), venue, address, papers and how far ahead it is (*340 m ahead*, *Here now* or *Behind you*). *Missed* and *Later* rows have *Mark dropped*, *Dropped* rows have *Undo*, and *Next*, *Skipped* and *Dropped on pass 1* rows have no button, as the next one has its alert's *Dropped*. A missed bundle marked dropped and then undone goes back to missed, and every other pass the drop settled goes back as it was (until you leave the Round tab, after which *Undo* puts back only that pass, as *Later*). The list opens with the next bundle in the middle of the view, a note says when some bundles have no amount set (they add no papers to the figures), and on a route with no bundle drops the list says so. An alert and the heading badge stay above the sheet, so an alert's *Dropped* works while the list is open.

Bundles dropped and missed are kept with the stops done in the saved round, so they survive a reload and *Continue Round*. Starting over, *Reset Round*, editing the route in Setup, copying a route and restoring from a backup start them fresh (a backup carries no progress at all). Skipping to a spot while driving, or starting from one on a round that was only part done, keeps those before it and brings back those after it, which alert again and are no longer dropped or missed. Starting from a spot after a round that was finished is a new round, as *Start New Round* is: nothing from the last round is dropped or missed, so its papers are not counted in the list or the bar. (A round counts as finished when every stop has been done. A no-throw house or special request within the alert distance of the very end of the route is never driven past, so a round with one there never counts as finished; the start screen then offers *Continue Round* and *Start Over*, and *Start Over* is the way to a new round.) Changing the side while driving keeps them for the passes of the route that still exist.

**The bottom of the round** is kept slim, with nothing floating over the map. One bar about 56 px high runs along it. From the left it shows the number of special stops left with *stops left* under it (a green tick and *All done* when there are none), then the middle, then a three-dot *Menu* button. The middle shows the first of these that applies: a problem with the GPS that stays until it is put right (*Location permission denied.* or *Waiting for GPS signal...*), which takes the middle and leaves the count and the menu; *Finding your location...* until the phone's first fix; the next bundle drop, when the route has bundle drops (above); and only when there is nothing else to say, *Alerts at 40 m* as a plain note, since the alert distance is changed in the menu and not here. *Keep the phone awake so GPS keeps updating.* is not about where the phone is, so it does not take the middle: while the screen is not being kept awake a small amber warning button, 44 px square, stands just left of the *Menu* button, and the next bundle stays in view beside it. Tapping it says the words as a notice in the bar for about 6 seconds. Messages are shown in the bar, never over the map. A notice (what changing the side did, *Dropped*, a missed bundle, *Carrying on from here.*) takes the whole line of the bar in a dark panel, and the menu button stays: it lasts about 6 seconds, or 12 seconds when it has an *Undo* button beside it, a long one makes the bar a line taller, and a newer notice replaces an older one.

The *Menu* button opens a sheet from the bottom over a dimmed map, with *Whole route* (see all of it, then come back with *Re-centre*), *Skip to a Spot or Section* (the map to pick a spot, as in Starting part way), *Alert Distance* (with the current one), *Bundles* (the list above, on any route), *Reset Round* (which asks first, as it always has) and the OpenStreetMap credit and *Fix the Map* links, which open in a new tab. Choosing any row but the two links closes the sheet first, so what it opens isn't under it; a bundle alert coming up closes the menu and the Bundles list too, and when either is opened while a Dropped alert is up (anywhere but a phone on its side) it keeps below the alert, so its *Done* is never under the *Dropped* button; the menu then scrolls, and on a short portrait screen the list drops its three figures to leave room for the rows. *Done*, or a tap on the dimmed map, closes it, and the side, the next turn, the heading badge and an alert stay in view above the dimming. *Whole route* fits the route with room kept clear round it: 30 px at the left, 70 px at the top for the side and any alert, 70 px at the right for the buttons, and the bar's height, the 8 px gap under it and 30 px at the bottom (so the route ends 30 px above the bar). Setup's map keeps the same margins at the left, top and right, and clears its own panel at the bottom.

Down the right of the map are the buttons used while driving, each 48 px square with a gap between them so a thumb finds the right one in a moving car: the voice (only when the phone can speak), the compass (point the map the way you are heading, or north), the follow button and, at the bottom nearest the thumb, *Zoom in* and *Zoom out*. The follow button is a small filled arrow while the map follows you. When the map has been left where you put it (see Looking around), or the whole route is up, it becomes a big blue button named *Re-centre*. The OpenStreetMap credit, which the map's licence asks for, stays in view as a tiny *(c) OpenStreetMap contributors* note above the left end of the bar, outside the map so it can't turn with it; the word *OpenStreetMap* in it is a link to OpenStreetMap's copyright page (the chip is only about 16 px high and the rest of it takes no tap, so the menu has a full-size row for the same page). The alert, the side and the next turn are drawn above the bar and the buttons, so where a tall alert reaches down to them, as on a short screen, its *Dropped* button is the one that takes the tap, not what is under it. On a wide screen (600 px or more) the bar is a panel 440 px wide at the left and the buttons stay at the right. On a short screen (600 px high or less, as a phone on its side is) the buttons stand in two columns, and on a phone on its side the side pill, the next turn and the alerts keep to the left part of the screen (46 percent of its width, but never narrower than 320 px or wider than 440 px, so on a small phone that is more than 46 percent, and clear of a notch), so the middle of the map and the buttons are free; the menu has two columns there, without its lines of explanation, and the Bundles list stands at the right.

**Looking around**: your zoom is your own. A GPS fix never changes it, whether the map is north up or turned, so zooming out to see where to turn stays zoomed out. The map only goes in to street level (zoom 17, or closer if you are already closer) the first time it follows you, and when you come back to following from the whole route. *Zoom in* and *Zoom out* take one step each, about the middle of the map, which is your dot while the map follows you, and each press is one step even when presses come quickly: three quick presses of *Zoom in* end three steps in, the map zooming and then carrying on to the zoom you asked for (it ignores a zoom asked for while it is still zooming, so the buttons keep count of where they are heading). A two-finger pinch while the map follows you zooms about your dot too, so the dot stays put. With the map pointing north, dragging it with a finger pauses following (free look): the map stays where you put it and the follow button becomes the big blue *Re-centre* button. Tap *Re-centre*, or leave the map alone for 15 seconds, and the map goes back to you at the zoom it has, without going back to street level: a short way is glided, and a way of more than a screen is jumped, since a glide that far would show blank map tiles on the way. While you are looking around, each move of the map (the start and the end of a drag or a pinch) starts the 15 seconds again, and so does each press of *Zoom in* or *Zoom out*, as a pinch does. While a finger is on the map it doesn't jump back to you: it returns at the next fix after the finger lifts. A pinch while looking around zooms about your fingers. Zooming alone never pauses following. Showing the whole route, or switching the compass, ends free look too, and from the whole route *Re-centre* goes back to street level. With the map pointing the way you are heading, one finger can't drag the map (so it can't be knocked off you), but the zoom buttons and a two-finger pinch work.

**What the round map draws**: the route is drawn in layers, so what is ahead is always on top at full strength. The part you have already driven (up to the furthest you have got) is faded and sits underneath, and its direction arrows are left off, not faded. On a street you drive twice (down and back, say) the pass still to come stays bright and is never washed out by the faded one, and the two passes are still drawn side by side. All the white outlines sit under all the coloured lines, so one section's outline never cuts into another's line, and the stops, marks, your position and the dashed guide line always sit above the lines. Stops you have done are drawn pale (a stop on a street driven twice is done when every pass of it that alerts is done), and a no-throw house that never alerts, because nothing is thrown on its side or the stretch is Transit, is drawn small (about 60 percent of the size) and the same pale, so it reads as a place with no alert, not a job. Houses that alert are full size and turn pale when done. Setup draws every stop in full colour.

Where you are along the route is judged against where you were a moment ago and the way you are heading, not just by the nearest line, so a street you drive more than once (down and back, say) is told pass from pass, and the arrow shows only the turn for the pass you are on, never one from later in the route (on Transit, a heading that agrees with none of the turns is given the next one by its place on the route: see Turn directions on Transit). *Continue Round* picks up from the furthest of where the round was started or skipped to, the furthest you got (kept every 100 m) and the last special stop done, which tells the passes apart at the start too. A bundle drop that was dropped does not count as a stop done for this, nor do the other passes of its stop that the drop settled: a bundle can be marked dropped in the list however far ahead it is, so it says nothing of where you got to, and marking bundles dropped from the list never moves where *Continue Round* carries on from. That comes from where you got to, saved every 100 m, so right after a drop it can carry on up to 100 m behind where you were.

**Starting part way**: under the start buttons, *Choose Where to Start* opens a map of the route. Tap the line where you want to begin, or pick *Beginning of the route* or a named section from the list (a section shows how many stops it has; when you skip while driving it also shows how many of them are not done yet), and tap *Start Here*. The stops before that spot count as done and the ones after it as not done, whatever was done before (and on a round that was finished, the bundles dropped and missed last time are forgotten too), so it also takes you back if you want to redo a part; a stop just before the spot (within 15 m, or within the alert distance if that is shorter) still comes up. Where the route goes along a road more than once and a tap could mean either time, it asks which time through you mean (unless the passes are separate throw-side sections, where the line you tap says). The round then carries on from the spot: nothing alerts until the first GPS fix says where you are, the part of the route before the spot is drawn as already driven and has no turn cards, and *Continue Round* later picks up from the furthest of there and where you got to (the start screen says where). The map shows where you are while you choose. While you drive, *Skip to a Spot or Section* in the menu opens the same map with a *Skip to Here* button. It asks first, saying how many stops that are not done yet will count as done and how many done ones will come up again, and the round keeps the way it was heading. If your first fix on the route is a little way (under 300 m) past the spot, the stops in between count as done too. It changes which stops count as done and where *Continue Round* picks up from, not where you are: the next GPS fix still puts you where you really are on the route, so use it where you are driving.

**Changing the throw side while driving**: if a side was put in wrongly, tap the side at the top of the screen (*Left*, *Right*, *Both* or *Transit*). Choose the side, then how much it applies to: from the last turn (or the start of the stretch) to the next turn (or its end), from here to the next turn, the named section you are in (only when you are in one), from here to the end of the stretch, or the whole stretch. Only the choices that make sense are listed (nothing shorter than 15 m, and none that repeat another), and a named section changes only the part of it inside this stretch. Only that part changes: the route is split there, and the rest of the stretch keeps its side. A part that is from here on starts where the car is when you choose, not where you tapped. The notice in the bar says what that does to the no-throw houses, counting each house on its own, including any you have passed (for example *2 no-throw houses will now stay quiet; 1 no-throw house will now alert*: a house only alerts on a side that is thrown, so a change from Left to Right can do both, and a house on a street driven twice can go quiet on one pass only). Houses that start to alert behind you count as done at once, and it says so. *Undo* is beside it for twelve seconds. It needs a GPS fix of 65 m or better, within 40 m of the route (or 2.5 times the fix's error if more), and the app has to know where you are, so right after starting or skipping it says it can't tell until the first fix lands on the route. The change is saved with the route, and what was done in the round stays done.

**Off the route**: if you are more than 50 m from the route in a straight line (more than twice the GPS fix's error, when that is worse than 25 m), on fixes of 40 m or better, while you are moving (faster than walking pace, about 5 km/h), for three fixes in a row and at least four seconds, an amber banner says *Off the route* with how far it is, in a straight line, to where you left it and which way (ahead of you, behind you, on your left or right, by the way the phone is heading). A dashed line on the map runs there, two falling notes sound (whether the voice is on or not), and with the voice on it says *Off route*. While you are moving and still off, the notes sound again about every 30 seconds and the voice says *Still off route*. When you are back within 40 m of the route for two fixes, and within 150 m along it of where you left, the banner goes, and with the voice on it says *Back on route* (stops in the stretch you skipped over, if you came back further on, count as done). It does not go off while you are stopped, while you are within the distance above of the route, before you have reached the route at the start of a round (it counts as reached when you are on it within 100 m along it of where the round starts, or after you have driven 200 m along it, so starting or skipping to a spot, and crossing the route on the way to its start, are not leaving it), or while you visit a stop that is away from the route (within how far off the route the stop is, plus its alert distance and 60 m, of the stop). It stays quiet once the round is over (every stop done, or the furthest you have got is within 50 m of the end of the route). Once it has gone off, a moving phone is reminded until it is back within 40 m of the route.

While you are off the route, or on a part of it that is not where you left it, the turn card and the spoken turns are left off, since they would be for the wrong place (the heading badge stays, as it only says which way the phone is going), and a turn that is still ahead of you when you are back is said again (with the voice on). An alert that was showing when you left goes, and comes up again when you are back; a stop already behind the place you left counts as done. Stop alerts need the app to know where you are along the route, which it does only within about 60 m of it (more with a poor fix), so houses on the route do not alert while you are further off than that; stops set away from the route still do.

If you come back onto the route somewhere else, it doesn't quietly re-aim. Once you have been on the new part for about 120 m (this also catches a short cross street between two streets of the route, if it skips more than about 200 m of route), the notes sound, the voice says how far further on or earlier on you came back onto it, and the banner offers two buttons and says what *Carry on from here* would do to the stops, and that left alone it carries on from here after about a minute. *Carry on from here* carries the round on from where you are now, without asking, as *Skip to Here* does: stops before that place count as done and stops after it come up again, even ones already done, so coming back earlier on re-opens the part since. *Take me back* keeps the dashed line guiding you to where you left (the banner reads *Heading back* and is quiet: if you leave the route again on the way, it reads *Off the route* but with no notes and no voice), and the turns come back once you are within 150 m along the route of that place; if you keep going along the route instead, until you are 500 m further along it than the nearest you have been to where you left, it carries on from where you are. If you press neither within a minute of the banner appearing, it carries on from where you are at your next good fix on the route while you are moving, with a short message in the bar (*Carrying on from here.*) and the voice, if it is on, says *Carrying on from here*), but no notes, unless you are driving back towards where you left, which it takes as *Take me back*. Streets less than about 80 m apart, or a cut-through that skips less than about 200 m of route, can still be crossed to without a warning, and the wrong pass of a street you drive twice can be taken for the right one for a short while.

**Map pointing**: the compass button with the map buttons turns the map to point the way you are heading instead of north (the choice is remembered). Your dot sits in the lower part of the screen, as low as it can and still clear the bar along the bottom with about 72 px to spare (never lower than 82 percent of the way down nor higher than halfway; the bar's real height is measured, so a long notice that makes it taller moves the dot up a little), with a small blue arrow above it, so more of the road ahead shows. It follows the direction of travel the phone reports; when the phone has none it uses the way you have been going over the last few dozen metres, and at the start of a round it points along the route. It holds still when you stop, so it doesn't spin at a red light. While it is on and the map follows you, one finger can't drag the map, and two fingers still zoom about your dot, as do *Zoom in* and *Zoom out*; the zoom is your own, see Looking around. *Whole route* in the menu always shows north up, and *Re-centre* or the compass brings the turned map back. Labels and house numbers stay upright, and a house number goes on the side of the house away from the road as it looks on the screen. The heading badge at the top right agrees with the turned map (see Heading badge). On the round the OpenStreetMap credit is the small note above the left end of the bar, so it can't turn with the map.

**Turn directions**: when a turn in your route is within 200 m (on a Transit stretch the card is always up, see below), a dark card beside the side pill shows an arrow bent the way the route turns, what to do (*Turn left*, *Bear right*, *Sharp left*, *U-turn*) and how far away it is, counting down to *Now*. It goes once you are through the turn. When the street names have been looked up (see Setup), the street the turn leads into is under the distance, and the voice says it (*Turn left onto Oak Road*; it says the first part of a name in two languages, and says *St*, *Rd* and the like in full, while the card shows the whole name). It is dark so it can't be mistaken for a stop alert, which are blue, red and purple, and it has nothing to tap. The turns are worked out from the shape of the line you drew, so the app knows nothing of junctions, and its street names are only what the lookup found: a corner, or a curve tight enough to be one, is a turn; a road that merely bends along its length is not; and a roundabout is one turn, as far as it turns you (going straight on through it is none). The card is left off when your GPS fix is worse than 65 m, or you are more than 40 m from your route (further if the fix is poor: 2.5 times its error), or the app has lost track of where you are, because then it can't tell where you are along the route. It is also left off while the *Off the route* banner is up, or you are on a part of the route that is not where you left it (see Off the route above).

**Turn directions on Transit**: the card is up only for the next 200 m, which suits streets full of turns and houses but leaves little to go by on a long drive between delivery areas. So on a Transit stretch (the side at your place on the route is Transit) it is always up. While the next turn is more than 200 m away it is a *Continue* card: an arrow straight up on a tile in the Transit pink, *Continue* over the distance to the next turn in big type (for example *3.2 km*), and under that a small arrow bent the way the turn goes with the street it leads into (or *Turn left* and so on when the street is not known). When no turn is left it says *Route ends in* with the distance, then *Route ends* and *Here* when it is right there. Within 200 m it becomes the usual turn card, counting down to *Now*. Distances are shown to the nearest 10 m under 100 m, to the nearest 50 m under a kilometre, then as 3.2 km (whole kilometres from 10 km). Off Transit nothing changes: the card is up only for the next 200 m, and only for a turn within 60 degrees of the heading. The same rules apply as for the usual card: it is left off when the fix is worse than 65 m, you are more than 40 m from the route (more if the fix is poor), the app has lost track of where you are, the *Off the route* banner is up or you are on a part of the route that is not where you left it. Which turn is the next one on Transit is decided in three steps. First, the turn the usual card would show: within 200 m, with the way the route comes up to it within 60 degrees of the way the phone is heading. Failing that, the first turn at any distance that the route comes up to within 120 degrees of the heading, so a road that bends on the way to a far corner, or a heading that is only part off (joining the road, going round a roundabout), doesn't hide it, and a turn close by that the heading is only part against isn't passed over for one further on. Failing that, the next turn by its place on the route, whatever the heading. So the card never says *Route ends in* while a turn is left, and says it only when none is. A turn that comes up the other way along a street you drive twice is passed over for one the heading agrees with, and is shown only when the heading agrees with none.

**Heading badge**: a small badge at the top right of the round screen, in line with the side pill and the turn card (they leave room for it), shows which way the phone is heading: a dial with an arrow, and under it the compass point (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W or NW, each covering 45 degrees centred on itself). It is shown whenever the app knows which way the phone is heading (its own course while it is moving, else the way it has been going over the last few dozen metres), so it can go for a moment at a standstill or in the middle of a corner, and it stays when the turn card is off (off the route, say). It has nothing to tap, and touches go through it to the map. With the map pointing north the ring is still, with a red mark at the top for north, and the arrow turns to the heading. With the map turned to the way you are heading (the compass button) the arrow points up and the ring turns the other way, so the red mark shows where north is on the map. It follows the phone's light or dark look.

**U-turns** are found as one turn, however they were drawn: the route doubling back at a point, a hairpin of two corners a few metres apart, or the way back drawn on the other lane or carriageway (up to about 30 m to the side, with a short piece across, even a slanted one). Two or more turns the same way, each starting within about 30 m of where the last ended, that add up to between 150 and 225 degrees are one turn, and it is put in the middle of the way across, provided they are corners. One of them must be a sharp corner, turning the heading by 70 degrees or more within about 20 m of route. None may be a stretch of curve (40 degrees or more in all, but never 40 degrees within 20 m), and at most one may be a slight bend (under 40 degrees, such as the start of a slanted piece across), which is taken in; a slight bend that is one of a run of bends does not start a U-turn. So a road that curves round (a crescent, a court or a loop ramp, however far round it goes and however the line was thinned) is never a U-turn or a *Sharp* turn, and neither is a curve straight after or before a corner, such as a street turning onto a crescent; a tight half circle (about 25 m radius or less) is still a U-turn. That includes a right turn and another right 25 m later into a car park, since the route does reverse direction. It is shown as *U-turn* when it comes to 165 degrees or more (180 at most) and as *Sharp* if it falls a little short, and it keeps the way round it went, so its arrow turns the right way. A loop round a block (corners further apart than that) stays ordinary turns, and so does a dog-leg (left then right); corners that add up to less than 150 degrees, or more than 225, stay separate turns. Some shapes are not found: a U-turn followed or preceded within about 20 m by another corner is shown as a single turn, which can be the wrong way round (a U-turn and then a right turn within about 15 m reads as *Turn left*; a U-turn and a left turn that close reads as *Turn right*), a slanted piece across of about 20 m or more along the road, with a narrow median, reads as *Sharp* rather than *U-turn*, and two corners rounded off on a radius of about 20 m, or a U-turn made of three 60-degree corners 20 m or more apart, can read as ordinary turns. Street names already looked up for corners that now form a U-turn may need *Look Up the Rest*, because the turn's position moved.

The turns are silent unless you ask to hear them. Choose *On* under *Voice* on the start screen, or tap the speaker button with the map buttons while you drive (the choice is remembered). Each turn is then said twice, as a warning when it comes into view (*In 200 metres, turn left*) and again once you are within 60 m of it (*Turn left*; a turn first met inside 60 m only gets that), in the phone's own voice, so it works with no signal. On a Transit stretch a turn is said up to four times: *In 1 kilometre, turn left onto Oak Road.* at about a kilometre, *In 500 metres, ...* at about 500 m, *In 200 metres, ...* at about 200 m (the usual warning) and *Turn left onto Oak Road.* within 60 m. The cues change over at 550 m and 225 m, a little past the distance they name, so a turn first met just beyond 500 m or 200 m is not told of twice in the same words. Each is said once and in order, and only the cue for where the turn is now: a turn first met part way in (when Transit begins, or after skipping to a spot) is told of once, for where it is (*In 700 metres, ...*), not with every cue it missed. The voice speaks of the same turn the card is about (on a *Continue* card, the turn it leads to), chosen as above, so it also speaks of a close turn that the heading is only part against, and of the last turn left when the heading agrees with none. Distances from 500 m up to a kilometre are said to the nearest 100 m, and a kilometre or more to the nearest half (*1 kilometre*, *1.5 kilometres*, *2 kilometres*). Off Transit the voice is as before: 200 m, then 60 m. The voice also says each alert once its beep is over (switching it on while an alert is showing says that alert at once): the numbers of a row of no-throw houses (*No throw, 12, 14, 16*), a bundle drop's number and how many papers, and a special request's number and its note, and after you tap *Dropped* on a bundle drop, the next bundle (see Bundle drops). It also says when you leave the route and when you are back (see Off the route above). Like the alert tones, it may be muted by the phone's silent switch. The button isn't shown on a browser that can't speak.

The start of a spoken word can go missing over a car's Bluetooth, because the link goes idle between sounds and takes a moment to wake. So the voice is led in by a short tick (or by the alert's beep), and with *Keep car audio awake* on, which it is once the voice is, a sound too quiet to hear plays all the time to keep the link open. Turn that off on the start screen if you hear a hiss or it matters for a car's battery.

## Notes
- The map opens on Australia until it finds you or your route, and place searches lean toward Australia when you have no route yet.
- Address lookup, place search and Route by Address use OpenStreetMap's free Nominatim service, and Follow roads, Route by Address and street names (below) use a free routing service. They need internet. House numbers are not always in OpenStreetMap: when only the street is found, the position is approximate and the app says so. Everything else works offline.
- **Follow roads** asks the free OSRM routing server that FOSSGIS runs at `routing.openstreetmap.de` (the one behind the directions on openstreetmap.org, car profile) for the way from your last point to each tap. Each lookup sends just the two ends of that stretch (and, when you use U-turn, one direction to leave the first by with a range of 60 degrees, and that its road is to be found within 30 m of that end), plus your IP address and the app's web address. It is one request per tap, one at a time and never more than one a second, as FOSSGIS's [terms of use](https://www.fossgis.de/arbeitsgruppen/osm-server/nutzungsbedingungen/) require; the map credits OpenStreetMap and links to *Fix the map*, which they also ask for (on the round the credit is a small note above the bar and *Fix the Map* is in its menu). Those terms say the server is best effort with no promise it keeps running or stays the same, and that heavy or high-traffic use is not allowed, so it suits one person tracing their own rounds. If the app is ever shared widely, run your own OSRM and build with `VITE_ROUTING_URL` set to its address (for example `https://osrm.example.com/route/v1/driving`).
- **Route by Address** sends the two addresses you type to Nominatim, with your IP address and the app's web address, one search at a time and never more than one a second, as its [usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/) requires (there is no search-as-you-type). The road itself comes from the same FOSSGIS server as Follow roads, which is sent only the coordinates of the stretch, so the same terms and the same advice about `VITE_ROUTING_URL` apply. A road is looked for up to 250 m from each address, since a house can sit well back from its street.
- **Street names** come from the same FOSSGIS OSRM server as Follow roads, with the same terms: at most one request for each turn, one at a time, started by you and never repeated for a turn that has an answer. Each request sends just the coordinates of a point on the road just after the turn and which way it runs, plus your IP address and the app's web address. A `VITE_ROUTING_URL` that points elsewhere must contain `/route/v1/` (as the example does), because street names use the same server's `/nearest/v1/`; without it they can't be looked up. FOSSGIS's terms also ask that an operator's email address be easy to find on a website that uses the server, so the app shows a contact line, `skupaaustralia@gmail.com`, at the bottom of *Routes*, under *Street names* in Setup and at the foot of *Route by Address*. If you build your own copy, set `VITE_CONTACT_EMAIL` to your own address. Names can be wrong where a tunnel or bridge lies under or over the road, or a nearer street is within 25 m of the point (with no road within 25 m the turn gets no name), and *Clear Street Names* and a new lookup is the way to redo them.
- Routes are saved automatically on the phone, after every change, and are still there next time you open the app. There is no Save button. A red bar appears if the phone refuses to store anything (for example in a private tab).
- They live only on that phone. On an iPhone, Safari and an app added to the Home Screen keep separate copies, so draw your routes in whichever one you will use (the Home Screen app is best). Deleting the app or clearing Safari's website data removes them.
- **Back up and restore** (in Routes): *Back Up This Route* or *Back Up All Routes* shows a block of text. *Copy* it into Notes, or *Save File* to keep it as a file (on a phone this opens the share sheet, with Save to Files and AirDrop), to keep a safe copy. *Restore from Backup* takes the pasted text, or a saved file with *Open File*, and adds its routes to your list. A route with the same name as one you already have can replace it or be kept beside it (you are asked), and a replaced route's progress starts over. A backup carries the route's named sections and street names too, but not its progress (the stops done, and the bundles dropped and missed). It is also how you move routes to another phone or from a computer.
- Safari has no vibration API, so the no-throw warning is a flashing red banner plus a tone (the phone's silent switch mutes it). Vibration is used where supported.
- GPS only updates while the app is open and the screen is on. Mount the phone and keep it unlocked.
- Map tiles need signal; offline you still see the route, stops and your position on a plain background.

## Install on iPhone
Geolocation needs HTTPS, so host the built files once (GitHub Pages via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`), open the URL in Safari, then Share > Add to Home Screen. Open it once online so it caches; after that it works offline.

## Develop
```bash
npm install
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
```

Follow roads is `src/roads.ts` (the routing request, the answer turned into points, the straight-line fallback and undo of a road stretch, tested in `src/roads.test.ts` with `fetch` mocked) and `src/useRoadFollow.ts` (runs the lookups for Setup, and keeps the U-turn switch). The U-turn request is `roadUrl(..., uturnFrom)` in `src/roads.ts`, which looks for the road to leave by within `UTURN_SNAP_M` (30 m) of the last point and says `UTURN_NOTICE` when none is found, with `arrivalBearing`, `uturnAvailable` and `uturnIsOn`, tested in `src/roads.test.ts` (which has a stand-in routing service that honours `radiuses` and `bearings`), and its button is in `src/RouteTab.tsx`. `src/uturnRoute.test.ts` draws U-turns the way Follow roads does (a stubbed road service, `extendAlongRoads`, then `routeTurns`) and checks that turn detection finds one U-turn for a dual-carriageway way back (3 to 25 m to either side, with straight or slanted joins), an exact reversal and U-turns with other turns around them, and that loops round a block, crescents and loop ramps, and a street turning onto a crescent are not U-turns. Build with `VITE_ROUTING_URL` set to point it at a different OSRM server.

The box that opens on the map where a section is tapped is `src/SidePopover.tsx`, placed by `placePopover` in `src/placement.ts` (above the tapped spot, below it when there is no room, or slid sideways clear of the sheet; tested in `src/placement.test.ts`).

Route by Address is `src/transit.ts` (looking the two addresses up, the road between them, how it joins the route, and what to say when it can't be done, tested in `src/transit.test.ts` with both services mocked) and `src/TransitDialog.tsx` (the From and To box).

Which stops are met by where you are, rather than along the route, is `byPosition` and `approachingOccurrences` in `src/geo.ts` and `zoneExit` and `approachSide` in `src/alerts.ts`. The house number on an alert is `houseNumber` and `withoutNumber` in `src/routeEdit.ts`. Each is tested in the matching `.test.ts` file.

Which no-throw houses stay quiet is `alertable`, `quietReason`, `quietHouseIds` and `quietText` in `src/alerts.ts` (only a house within `QUIET_REACH_M`, 40 m, of the route can be quiet), tested in `src/alerts.test.ts` with streets driven twice, hand-picked sides, both sides thrown, stops off the route and sides that can't be told; `src/StopsTab.tsx` says why in the list. `src/driveEdit.ts` also builds what a change of side did to the no-throw houses (`sideChangeNote`, and `sideChangeSentence` with its full stop and the reason), tested in `src/driveEdit.test.ts` along with the choices of how far a change reaches.

Where you are along the route is `src/progress.ts` (the tracker, tested against simulated drives over streets driven twice, loops and GPS glitches in `src/progress.test.ts`). Sounds and the keep-alive signal are `src/sound.ts`. Which way the phone is heading when it has no course of its own is `src/heading.ts`. Which alert shows (`pickCurrent`: a no-throw house that is live first, a house gone by no longer hiding a bundle drop still in range), the plates of a row and what is said for them are in `src/alerts.ts`, which also counts the stops left and whether the round is finished (`roundCounts`). Turn directions are `src/turns.ts` (finding the turns in a route, joining the halves of a U-turn, which one to show and how far ahead and within what heading (the `tolerance` option of `upcomingTurn`), when to speak, including the kilometre and 500 m warnings on Transit, and the shape of the arrow; the join needs a corner of `UTURN_CORNER_DEG` (70 degrees) and no stretch of curve or second slight bend; tested in `src/turns.test.ts`), `src/guidance.ts` (what to tell a driver on a Transit stretch: the next turn in three steps (the usual card's turn, then the first within `TRANSIT_HEADING_TOLERANCE`, 120 degrees, of the heading, then the next by its place), carrying on to it or to the end of the route, the card's words and the compass labels, tested in `src/guidance.test.ts`), `src/transitView.ts` (what the round screen does with it: which card to show, what the voice says, the straight card's lines and how the heading badge turns, tested in `src/transitView.test.ts`), `src/TurnCard.tsx` (the card, with its straight variant), `src/HeadingBadge.tsx` (the badge), `src/guidance.css` (their styles) and `src/speech.ts` (the phone's voice, tested in `src/speech.test.ts` with the speech mocked).
